# The paper as a dependency graph: statements, background blocks and edge demands

Paper: Praxis: Practical Witness Encryption for NP, David Tse, Nikhil Vanjani, Psi Vesely. arXiv:2611.28552v1 (Cryptology ePrint Archive 2026/2971). Proofs are left out. Cited theorems are named in the statements that use them.

## Results

### `lem:we-hiding`: Lemma 3.7, Extractability implies hiding (lemma)

If $\mathsf{WE}$ is extractable with error $\delta$ and any extraction time, then for every instance $S$ that has no witness, all $m_0, m_1 \in \{0,1\}^\mu$, and every adversary $\mathcal{A}$ as in Definition 3.6, the probabilities $p_b := \Pr\bigl[ \mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{O}}(ct) = 1 : ct \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}^{\mathcal{O}}(S, m_b) \bigr]$, for $b \in \{0,1\}$, satisfy $|p_1 - p_0| \le 2\,\delta(t, Q, Q_H)$. That is, $\mathsf{WE}$ is hiding in the sense of Garg, Gentry, Sahai, and Waters \cite{STOC:GGSW13}, with error $2\delta$.

### `lem:rank-one`: Lemma 4.3, Rank gap (lemma)

Let $W$ be a nonzero passing matrix of rank one. Then $W_{00} \ne 0$ and $W = W_{00}\, w w^\top$ for a witness $w$ of $S$. In particular, if $S$ is false, every nonzero passing matrix has rank at least two.

### `lem:gp-rank`: Lemma 4.5, Rank bound (lemma)

For every stack $\mathbf{W} = (W_c)_{c \in C}$: (a) $\operatorname{gp}(\mathbf{W}) \le g^{-\operatorname{rank} \mathbf{W}}$, with equality if the row space of $\mathbf{W}$ is spanned by unit vectors $e_i$ with $i \in [N]$; (b) $\operatorname{gp}(v^\top) \ge \operatorname{gp}(\mathbf{W})$ for every $v$ in the row space of $\mathbf{W}$.

### `lem:qap`: Lemma 4.7, QAP criterion (lemma)

For an admissible choice of evaluation points and a symmetric matrix $W \in \mathbb{F}_q^{(N+1) \times (N+1)}$, the following are equivalent: (a) $W$ passes the tests of $S$ (Definition 4.2); (b) $P_W(X) - W_{00}\, L_\rho(X) = h(X)\, Z(X)\, (X - \rho) + Z_0(X)\, R(X)$ for a polynomial $h(X)$ and an allowed polynomial $R(X)$; (c) $P_W(X) = h'(X)\, Z(X) + Z_0(X)\, R(X)$ for a polynomial $h'(X)$ and an allowed polynomial $R(X)$. In (b) and (c), $R(X)$ is the polynomial $R_W(X) \in \mathbb{F}_q^{<\nu n_{\mathsf{c}}}[X]$ with $R_W(p) = P_W(p)/Z_0(p)$ for every $p$ in the cosets, $h(X) \in \mathbb{F}_q^{<D-1}[X]$, and $h'(X) \in \mathbb{F}_q^{<D}[X]$.

### `lem:honest`: Lemma 5.4, Honest combination (lemma)

Let $w$ be a witness of $S$ and $ct$ an output of $\mathsf{Enc}(\mathsf{pp}, m)$ (Figure [full-enc]), for any coins, with scalars $\gamma_c$ and $\kappa$ as there, with weights $\xi_c(w)$ for the forms of its matrix $M$. Then the divisions of Equation [cancel] are exact, and the combinations defined by $\mathsf{Comb}$ in Figure [full-dec] give \[ \Lambda = [\xi_{\mathrm{key}}(w)\, \kappa + r(w)\, \psi]_T, \qquad \Psi = [\psi]_T, \qquad \Lambda^\alpha = \alpha \cdot \Lambda, \qquad \Psi^\alpha = \alpha \cdot \Psi, \] where $\psi := y(w) \sum_{c \in C} \gamma_c\, \xi_c(w)$.

### `lem:range`: Lemma 5.5, Search range (lemma)

Let $w \in \{0,1\}^{N+1}$ with $w_0 = 1$, and let $r$ and $c_w$ be those of Figure [enc], Figure [dec], or $r \leftarrow \mathcal{D}_g$ and the center of Section 5.2. Then $|r(w) - c_w| \le B$ for every $r$ in the support, and the expectation of $(r(w) - c_w)^2$ is at most $\bigl( g \sqrt{(1 + \operatorname{wt}(w))/12} + 1/2 \bigr)^2$ in the first case and $\bigl( g \sqrt{(N+4)/48} + 3/2 \bigr)^2$ in the second.

### `thm:correct`: Theorem 5.6, Correctness (theorem)

For every admissible choice of evaluation points, Praxis as specified in Appendix A is correct with error $2/q$ (Definition 3.5). For a witness $w$, decryption fails exactly when $\xi_{\mathrm{key}}(w) = 0$.

### `prop:efficiency`: Proposition 5.7, Efficiency (proposition)

For Praxis as specified in Appendix A, with the evaluation points placed in $\mathcal{H}$ as in Section 4.4 and $n \le D$, and with $n_1 := (2K_1 + 1)(N+1) + \Delta + (\nu - 1)n_{\mathsf{c}}/J'$ and $n_2 := 6K_2 (N+1) + 3K_2 + \frac{3n}{2}(J + J') + 13n - 8$: (a) \emph{Size.} A ciphertext consists of $n_1$ elements of $\mathbb{G}_1$, $2 n_2$ of $\mathbb{G}_2$, one of $\mathbb{G}_T$, the seed, and $\mu$ bits. (b) \emph{Encryption.} $\mathsf{Enc}$ computes one scalar multiplication of a generator for each of these group elements and one more in $\mathbb{G}_T$. (c) \emph{Decryption.} Outside line [full-search], $\mathsf{Dec}$ computes $18K_2 + 4J + 2J' + 2$ pairings; at most $(2K_1 + 1) N$ additions and MSMs (sums $\sum_{i \le s} a_i P_i$ of length $s$) of total length at most $3n + 2D + (\nu - 1)n_{\mathsf{c}}$ in $\mathbb{G}_1$; at most $12 K_2 N$ additions and MSMs of total length $3n\,(J + J') + 26n - 16$ in $\mathbb{G}_2$; and $18K_2 + 4J + 2J' + 1$ additions and four scalar multiplications in $\mathbb{G}_T$. (d) \emph{Search.} line [full-search] can be computed with $O(\sqrt{|t|} + 1)$ operations in $\mathbb{G}_T$, where $t$ is its result. On a ciphertext of $\mathsf{Enc}$, this is $O(\sqrt{g}\, N^{1/4})$ operations in expectation over the coins of $\mathsf{Enc}$. Each of $\mathsf{Enc}$ and $\mathsf{Dec}$ also makes $k(\ell + 1)$ queries to $H_{\mathsf{seed}}$ and at most one to $H_{\mathsf{key}}$, and performs $O(n_1 + n_2 + k(\ell + 1)\, d_R \log d_R + n \log n + D \log D + |S|)$ operations in $\mathbb{F}_q$. $\mathsf{Setup}$ performs $O(|S| + D \log D + \log q)$.

### `cor:asymptotics`: Corollary 5.8, Asymptotic costs (corollary)

With these parameters and the evaluation points placed in $\mathcal{H}$ as in Section 4.4, for systems of linear size (Section 4.1) with $N \ge \lambda^2$: (a) \emph{Size.} A ciphertext has $O(\lambda N)$ field elements. (b) \emph{Encryption.} $\mathsf{Enc}$ takes $O(\lambda^2 N / \log \lambda)$ field operations. (c) \emph{Decryption.} $\mathsf{Dec}$ takes $O(\lambda N \log \lambda)$ field operations outside line [full-search]. In it, $\mathsf{Dec}$ takes $O(2^{\lambda/4} N^{1/4})$ field operations in expectation over the coins of $\mathsf{Enc}$.

### `thm:extraction`: Theorem 5.9, Extraction (theorem)

Let the parameters be admissible, that is, satisfy (P1) to (P5) below. There is an extractor $\mathcal{E}$ (Appendix B.6) such that for every system $S$ of the shape, every choice of evaluation points that meets the conditions of Definition 4.6, all $m_0, m_1 \in \{0,1\}^\mu$, and every adversary $\mathcal{A}$ that on every run makes at most $Q < 2^\lambda$ calls to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$ and at most $2^\lambda$ queries to each RO: (a) \emph{Extraction.} Let $s_0$ and $s_1$ be the advantages against $\mathsf{MSIS}_{k,\ell,\beta}$ (Definition 3.3) of the reductions $\mathcal{B}_0$ and $\mathcal{B}_1$ of Appendix B.5, which run $\mathcal{A}$ once and do not use its output. In the experiment of Definition 3.6, \[ \Pr\bigl[\, \mathcal{A} \text{ queries } H_{\mathsf{key}} \text{ at } [\kappa]_T \;\wedge\; \mathcal{E} \text{ outputs no witness of } S \bigm| b \,\bigr] \le \frac{Q+1}{2^\lambda} + s_b . \] (b) \emph{Soundness.} On every input, every output of $\mathcal{E}$ is $\bot$ or a witness of $S$. (c) \emph{Running time.} $\mathcal{E}$ runs in time $O\bigl( (Q+1) (nN + |S|) + L \bigr)$, where $|S|$ is the length of $S$ (Section 3.5) and $L$ counts the labels given to $\mathcal{A}$ (the generators' and the ciphertext's).

### `cor:ext-error`: Corollary 5.10, Extractability error (corollary)

For admissible parameters, Praxis is extractable with error $\delta$ and the extraction time of Theorem 5.9(c), where $\delta(t, Q, Q_H) = (Q+1)/2^{\lambda+1} + \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{MSIS}}(t_R)$ if $Q < 2^\lambda$ and $Q_H \le 2^\lambda$, and $\delta = 1/2$ otherwise. Here $t_R = t + O(Q + d_R\, Q_H + \mu) + \mathsf{poly}(D, \log q)$ bounds the reductions' running time (Proposition B.15), and $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{MSIS}}(t_R)$ is the largest advantage against $\mathsf{MSIS}_{k,\ell,\beta}$ of an algorithm, with any advice, that runs in time at most $t_R$. Running times are as in Appendix B.5, descriptions and advice included.

### `lem:ledger`: Lemma B.2, Generic bound (lemma)

$E$ is a polynomial of degree two in $u$ with nonnegative coefficients. If $E(2) \le 2^{-\lambda}$ and $E(2^{\lambda+1}) - E(2) \le 1 - 2^{-\lambda}$, then $E(2Q + 2) \le (Q+1)/2^\lambda$ for every integer $0 \le Q < 2^\lambda$. For $\lambda \ge 1$, the second premise requires $\theta < 2^{-(2\lambda + 1)}$.

### `lem:degree`: Lemma B.3, Degrees (lemma)

Let $\varpi := \Omega_{22}\, \zeta\, \zeta'\, \zeta''\, \varepsilon$ and $d_f := 3D + 2n + 2K_1 + 6$. For every expression $\mathsf{D}$ of $\mathbb{G}_T$ and every $\chi \in \mathbb{F}_q$, the function $\varpi \cdot \bigl( \operatorname{val}(\mathsf{D}; \mathbf{c}, r) - \chi\kappa \bigr)$ is a polynomial in the entries of $\mathbf{c}$ and of $r$ of total degree at most $d_f$. For expressions $\mathsf{E}, \mathsf{E}'$ of $\mathbb{G}_1$ or of $\mathbb{G}_2$, the same bound holds for $\varpi \cdot \bigl( \operatorname{val}(\mathsf{E}) - \operatorname{val}(\mathsf{E}') \bigr)$.

### `lem:view`: Lemma B.4, View (lemma)

Let $\mathsf{D}$ be an expression of $\mathbb{G}_T$, $\chi \in \mathbb{F}_q$, and $r, r' \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$. (a) \emph{Passing stacks.} If $\mathsf{D}$ is $\chi\kappa$ identically at $r$, then in each sector $\mathsf{s}$ the stack $\mathbf{W}^{\mathsf{s}}(\mathsf{D})$ passes and satisfies the ties, $W^{\mathsf{s}}_c(\mathsf{D})\, r = u^{\mathsf{s}}_c(\mathsf{D})$ for every cell $c$, and the corner $\bigl( W^{\mathrm{plain}}_{c_{\mathrm{key}}}(\mathsf{D}) \bigr)_{00}$ is $\chi$. (b) \emph{Locus.} If $\mathsf{D}$ is $\chi\kappa$ identically at $r$ and $W^{\mathsf{s}}_c(\mathsf{D})\, r' = u^{\mathsf{s}}_c(\mathsf{D})$ for every sector and cell, then $\mathsf{D}$ is $\chi\kappa$ identically at $r'$. In particular, if $\mathsf{D}$ is $\chi\kappa$ identically at $r$ and has zero stacks, then $\mathsf{D}$ is $\chi\kappa$ formally, and $\chi = 0$. (c) \emph{Coincidence.} If $\mathsf{D}$ is not $\chi\kappa$ identically at $r$, $\Pr[\operatorname{val}(\mathsf{D}; \mathbf{c}, r) = \chi\kappa] \le d_f/(q - D) \le \varepsilon_f$ for uniform valid $\mathbf{c}$. (d) \emph{No formal key scalar.} If $\chi \ne 0$, then $\mathsf{D}$ is not $\chi\kappa$ formally. (e) \emph{Source groups.} If $\mathsf{E}$ and $\mathsf{E}'$ are expressions of $\mathbb{G}_1$, or of $\mathbb{G}_2$, that are not formally equal, then for every $r$ their values at $(\mathbf{c}, r)$ are equal with probability at most $\varepsilon_f$ over $\mathbf{c}$.

### `lem:moore`: Lemma B.5, Consecutive conjugates (lemma)

Let $h \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n}^d$ have entries that are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_q$, with $d \le n$. Then for every integer $s$ the vectors $\sigma^s(h), \sigma^{s+1}(h), \dots, \sigma^{s+d-1}(h)$ form a basis of $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}^d$.

### `lem:classification`: Lemma B.6, Classification (lemma)

Let $\mathbf{W}$ be a stack of symmetric matrices that pass every Booleanity test and that satisfies the ties. Suppose that its row space $\mathcal{V}$ has dimension at most three and contains no kernel vector. (a) There are a set $\mathcal{W} \subseteq \mathcal{V} \cap \{0,1\}^{N+1}$ of assignments and $\chi_w \in \mathbb{F}_q$ with $\mathbf{W} = \sum_{w \in \mathcal{W}} \chi_w\, \mathbf{H}(w)$. (b) If $\mathbf{W}$ passes every constraint's test, every such representation has $\chi_w \ne 0$ only for witnesses $w$ of $S$. In particular, a nonzero usable stack whose row space has dimension at most three and contains no kernel vector is a combination, with a nonzero coefficient, of honest stacks of witnesses in its row space.

### `lem:heavy`: Lemma B.8, Heavy stacks (lemma)

### `lem:lift`: Lemma B.9, Lifting step (lemma)

Let $2\beta < q$ and $24\beta^4 < q^3$. There is a deterministic algorithm that returns, on input a nonzero $v \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ with $v_0 = 0$, a short multiple of $v$, or $\bot$ if there is none. It makes $N - 1$ extensions, each with at most $32$ integer programs and $c_{\mathrm{HNF}} = O(1)$ Hermite normal forms, both in dimension at most four, and $O(N)$ further operations, on inputs of $O(N \log q)$ bits; its running time $(N-1)\bigl(32\, T_{\mathsf{IP}} + c_{\mathrm{HNF}}\, T_{\mathsf{HNF}} + O(N)\bigr)$, for the times $T_{\mathsf{IP}}$ and $T_{\mathsf{HNF}}$ of these programs and forms, is polynomial in $N$ and $\log q$.

### `lem:reduction`: Lemma B.14, Reductions (lemma)

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an adversary and $b \in \{0,1\}$. (a) \emph{Law.} For a uniform challenge, the run of $\mathcal{A}$ in (R1) has the law of the run with $m_b$ of the experiment, as the values that (R1) sets are uniform; the coins of (R2) are independent of it. (b) \emph{Soundness.} Every output of $\mathcal{B}_b$ other than $\bot$ is a solution of MSIS for its challenge, the columns $1, \dots, \ell$ of $M$. (c) \emph{Detection.} For every run whose first mark has an expression $\mathsf{D}$, an expression of (R3) is formally equal to $\mathsf{D}$, except with probability at most $\varepsilon_f$ over $\mathsf{a}_{\mathrm{p}}$. (d) \emph{Recovery.} For every run whose first mark is good, with expression $\mathsf{D}$ and mark sector $\mathsf{s}$, the vectors $\nabla^{(l,\mathsf{s})}(\mathsf{D})$ lie in the row space of the mark stack, and span it except with probability at most $\varepsilon_f$ over the recovery points; and if $\mathsf{s}$ is the alpha sector, the vectors $\nabla^{(l,\mathrm{plain})}(\mathsf{D})$ are zero. Consequently, in the run with $m_b$ and a uniform $M$, the probability that the first mark is good and the row space of its mark stack contains a kernel vector is at most $s_b + 2\varepsilon_f$.

### `prop:reduction-time`: Proposition B.15, Running time of the reductions (proposition)

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 5.9, if $\mathcal{A}$ runs in time $t$ and makes at most $Q_H$ queries to each of $H_{\mathsf{seed}}$ and $H_{\mathsf{key}}$, then $\mathcal{B}_0$ and $\mathcal{B}_1$ run in time at most \begin{equation} t + 148\, Q + (d_R + 5)\, Q_H + C_R + 2 T_{\mathsf{LIFT}} , \quad C_R := 8 I + 3 L + 2 C_{\mathrm{post}} + C_{\mathrm{set}} + C_{\mathrm{desc}} , \end{equation} where $I$, $C_{\mathrm{post}}$, $C_{\mathrm{set}}$, and $C_{\mathrm{desc}}$, sums given in the proof, and hence $C_R$, do not depend on $\mathcal{A}$, and $T_{\mathsf{LIFT}}$, the time of the lifting step, is polynomial in $N$ and $\log q$ (Lemma B.9, by (P3) and (P4)). We call this bound $t_R$; it is $t + O(Q + d_R\, Q_H + \mu) + \mathsf{poly}(D, \log q)$, as $C_R = O((N + n) D + \mu)$, $N < D$, and $n \le D$.

### `lem:extractor`: Lemma B.16, Extractor (lemma)

(a) Every output of $\mathcal{E}$ is $\bot$ or a witness of $S$. (b) If the first mark of a run has an expression $\mathsf{D}$ and $\mathcal{E}$ outputs $\bot$, then the tests of the sketches of $\mathsf{D}$ in both sectors find nothing. (c) Let $\mathbf{W}$ be a nonzero usable stack whose row space $\mathcal{V}$ has dimension at most three and contains no kernel vector, and let the $v^{(l)}_c$ be independent and uniform. Then a test of the three vectors $\sum_c W_c\, v^{(l)}_c$, $l \in [3]$, outputs a witness, except with probability at most $2/q$. Consequently, the probability that the first mark of a run is good, the row space of its mark stack contains no kernel vector, and $\mathcal{E}$ outputs no witness is at most $2/q$.

### `lem:lattice-facts`: Lemma B.17, Integer lattices (lemma)

Let $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$ be a lattice of rank $d$ with a basis matrix $B$. (a) If $\Theta$ has a largest minor at $J$, then $|y_i| \le \|y_J\|_1$ for all $y \in \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Theta)$ and $i \in I$. (b) If $\Theta$ is saturated, then $B^\top \mathbb{Z}^I = \mathbb{Z}^d$. (c) If an integer matrix $\Delta$ with rows indexed by $I$ has rank $k$ and $k \times k$ minors below $q$ in absolute value, then every $c \in \mathbb{Z}^I$ with $c^\top \Delta = 0 \bmod q$ is congruent modulo $q$ to a $u \in \mathbb{Z}^I$ with $u^\top \Delta = 0$.

### `lem:prefix`: Lemma B.18, Prefix lattices (lemma)

Let $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$ be a saturated lattice of rank $d \ge 1$ with a basis matrix $B$ and a largest minor at $J$. Order $I$ as $i_1, \dots, i_{|I|}$ with $J$ first, and for $d \le k \le |I|$ let $I_k := \{ i_1, \dots, i_k \}$, and let $\Theta_k \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^{I_k}$ be the integer points of the column span of the submatrix $B_{I_k}$, with height $h_k$ and largest fiber density $m_k$. (a) $\Theta_d = \mathbb{Z}^{I_d}$, so $h_d = 1$ and $m_d = g^{-d}$; $\Theta_{|I|} = \Theta$; and each $\Theta_k$ has rank $d$ and a largest minor at $J$. (b) For $d \le k < |I|$ there is an integer $s_k \ge 1$ with $h_{k+1} = s_k h_k$ and $m_{k+1} \le m_k \lceil g/s_k \rceil/g$. If $s_k \ge g$, the entry at $i_{k+1}$ is constant on each fiber of $\Theta_{k+1}$; if $s_k < g$, then $m_{k+1}^b h_{k+1}^{b-1} \le m_k^b h_k^{b-1}$. (c) If $d = 3$ and $m(\Theta) \ge \mu_{\mathrm{tr}}$, then $h(\Theta) \le H_3$ and $\Theta$ is regular.

### `lem:trim`: Lemma B.19, Trimming (lemma)

Trimming a set by such a lattice $\Theta$ lowers its density by at most $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}$.

### `lem:sparse`: Lemma B.20, Trimmed sets are sparse (lemma)

Let $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$, with $|I| \le N$, be a lattice of rank $d \in [3]$ with a basis matrix $B$, $F$ a nonempty set of box points on $I \cup \{i'\}$, $i' \notin I$, trimmed by $\Theta$, and $\Phi(r) := (B^\top r_I, r_{i'})$. (a) If $\dim \Phi(F) = d + 1$ and $\Phi(F)$ is in a coset of a subgroup of index $q$ of $\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}$, then $\Pr[F] \le \Lambda_d\, g^d\, h(\Theta)\, m(\Theta)/q$. (b) If $F^\perp$ has a rank $d'$ with $1 \le d' < d$ and a largest minor at a set $J \subseteq I$, and the restriction to $I$ of each vector of $F^\perp$ lies in $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Theta)$, then $\Pr[F]\, h(F^\perp) \le \Lambda_{d-d'}\, g^{d-d'}\, h(\Theta)\, m(\Theta)$.

### `lem:regularize`: Lemma B.21, Regularization (lemma)

Let $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$, $|I| \le N$, be a saturated lattice of a rank $d \in [3]$ with $m(\Theta) \ge \mu_{\mathrm{tr}} + (3 - d)\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}$. Then $\Theta$ lies in a regular saturated $\Theta' \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$ of rank in $[d, 3]$ with $m(\Theta') \ge m(\Theta) - (3 - d)\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}$.

### `blob:ggm`: Definition 3.1, Bilinear groups and the generic bilinear group model (model)

A bilinear group $(\mathbb{G}_1,\mathbb{G}_2,\mathbb{G}_T,q,G_1,G_2,e)$ consists of cyclic groups $\mathbb{G}_1$, $\mathbb{G}_2$, and $\mathbb{G}_T$ of prime order $q$, generators $G_1$ of $\mathbb{G}_1$ and $G_2$ of $\mathbb{G}_2$, and a non-degenerate bilinear map $e\colon \mathbb{G}_1 \times \mathbb{G}_2 \to \mathbb{G}_T$. We write the groups additively and let $G_T := e(G_1,G_2)$. For $s \in \{1,2,T\}$, $[x]_s := x\,G_s$ is the encoding of $x \in \mathbb{F}_q$ in $\mathbb{G}_s$, taken entrywise for vectors and matrices, so that $e([x]_1,[y]_2) = [x\,y]_T$. Our groups are of Type III: no efficiently computable homomorphism between $\mathbb{G}_1$ and $\mathbb{G}_2$ is known, in either direction. Generic bilinear group model. Fix the order $q$ and a label length $\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}$ with $2^{\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}} \ge q$. At the start of each run of an experiment, the model samples independent, uniformly random injections $\mathsf{lab}_1,\mathsf{lab}_2,\mathsf{lab}_T\colon \mathbb{F}_q \to \{0,1\}^{\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}}$; the label of $[v]_s$ is $\mathsf{lab}_s(v)$. Every group element that an algorithm receives or outputs is a label, and every algorithm receives the labels of $G_1$, $G_2$, and $G_T$. For $s \in \{1,2,T\}$, $a, b \in \{0,1\}^{\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}}$, and $c \in \mathbb{F}_q$, the group oracle $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$ answers $\mathsf{add}(s,a,b) := \mathsf{lab}_s(\mathsf{lab}_s^{-1}(a) + \mathsf{lab}_s^{-1}(b))$, $\mathsf{smul}(s,c,a) := \mathsf{lab}_s(c\,\mathsf{lab}_s^{-1}(a))$, $\mathsf{pair}(a,b) := \mathsf{lab}_T(\mathsf{lab}_1^{-1}(a)\,\mathsf{lab}_2^{-1}(b))$, and $\mathsf{name}(a) := \{\mathsf{lab}_T(v), \mathsf{lab}_T(-v)\}$ for $v := \mathsf{lab}_T^{-1}(a)$, and returns $\bot$ if an argument is not a label of its group. A call to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$ counts also when it returns $\bot$. An algorithm may call $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$, or query a random oracle, on a string that it has not received: a string fixed before the injections are sampled is a label with probability $q/2^{\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}}$. A guessed label is that of an element whose exponent the algorithm does not know, as in oblivious sampling, and the security proof treats it as a new variable.

### `blob:rom`: Definition 3.2, Random oracle model; the key oracle and the seed oracle (model)

### `blob:msis`: Definition 3.3, The ring $R_q$ and Module-SIS (assumption)

Let $d_R$ be a power of two, $R := \mathbb{Z}[Y]/(Y^{d_R}+1)$, and $R_q := R/qR = \mathbb{F}_q[Y]/(Y^{d_R}+1)$ for the group order $q$, with $q \equiv 1 \bmod 2d_R$. ### `blob:we`: Definitions 3.4 to 3.6, Witness encryption, correctness, extractability (definition)

Let $\mathcal{R}$ be an NP relation: a set of pairs $(S,w)$ of an instance $S$ and a witness $w$, decidable in time $\mathsf{poly}(|S|)$, with $|w| \le \mathsf{poly}(|S|)$, where $|S|$ is the length of an encoding of $S$. For Praxis, $\mathcal{R}$ pairs Boolean quadratic systems of a fixed shape with their witnesses, the Boolean assignments that satisfy every constraint. Witness encryption. A witness encryption scheme for an NP relation $\mathcal{R}$ and messages in $\{0,1\}^\mu$ is a pair $\mathsf{WE} = (\mathsf{Enc}, \mathsf{Dec})$ of algorithms with access to the oracles $\mathcal{O} := (\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}, H_1, \dots, H_{n_H})$ of the generic bilinear group model and the random oracle model. The probabilistic algorithm $\mathsf{Enc}^{\mathcal{O}}(S, m)$ outputs, in time $\mathsf{poly}(|S|, \lambda)$, a ciphertext $ct$ of a message $m \in \{0,1\}^\mu$ for an instance $S$, and $\mathsf{Dec}^{\mathcal{O}}(S, ct, w)$, whose running time is not bounded, outputs a message or $\bot$. Correctness. $\mathsf{WE}$ is correct with error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cor}}$ if for every $(S,w) \in \mathcal{R}$ and every $m \in \{0,1\}^\mu$, $\Pr[\, \mathsf{Dec}^{\mathcal{O}}(S, ct, w) \ne m : ct \gets \mathsf{Enc}^{\mathcal{O}}(S, m) \,] \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cor}}$, where $S$, $w$, and $m$ are fixed before $\mathcal{O}$ is sampled, and the probability is over $\mathcal{O}$ and the coins of $\mathsf{Enc}$ and $\mathsf{Dec}$. Extractability. $\mathsf{WE}$ is extractable with error $\delta$ and extraction time $t_{\mathcal{E}}$ if there is an extractor $\mathcal{E}$, which makes no oracle call, such that for every instance $S$, all $m_0, m_1 \in \{0,1\}^\mu$, and every adversary $\mathcal{A}$, with any advice, that runs in time $t$ and makes at most $Q$ calls to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$ and at most $Q_H$ queries to each RO, $\mathcal{E}$ runs in time at most $t_{\mathcal{E}}(|S|, Q)$ and $\Pr[\, b' = b \wedge (S, w) \notin \mathcal{R} \,] \le \frac{1}{2} + \delta(t, Q, Q_H)$ in the experiment that samples $b$ uniformly from $\{0,1\}$ and runs $ct \gets \mathsf{Enc}^{\mathcal{O}}(S, m_b)$, $b' \gets \mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{O}}(ct)$, and $w \gets \mathcal{E}(S, ct, \mathsf{tr})$, where $\mathsf{tr}$ is the transcript of $\mathcal{A}$'s answered calls to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$, in order, with their answers. The experiment fixes $S$, $m_0$, $m_1$, and $\mathcal{A}$ before it samples $\mathcal{O}$, and the probability is over $\mathcal{O}$, $b$, and the coins of $\mathsf{Enc}$, $\mathcal{A}$, and $\mathcal{E}$. So if $\mathcal{A}$ guesses $b$ with probability $\frac12 + \varepsilon$, then $\mathcal{E}$ outputs a witness with probability at least $\varepsilon - \delta(t, Q, Q_H)$. The extractor is universal and straight-line: it is fixed before the adversary and runs once, on the transcript of the finished run.

### `blob:bqs`: Definition 4.1, Boolean quadratic systems, witnesses, shape (definition)

Vectors in $\mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ have indices $0, \dots, N$; $e_i$ is the unit vector of index $i$, and each vector $v$ defines the linear form $v(w) := \langle v, w \rangle = \sum_{i=0}^{N} v_i w_i$. Boolean quadratic system. A Boolean quadratic system (BQS) $S$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ in $N$ unknowns is a finite list of constraints. Constraint $t$ has $F_t \ge 1$ products and vectors $a_{t,f}, b_{t,f}, c_t \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ for $f \in [F_t]$, and reads $\sum_{f \in [F_t]} a_{t,f}(w)\, b_{t,f}(w) = w_0\, c_t(w)$. An assignment is a $w \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ with $w_0 = 1$, and a witness of $S$ is an assignment in $\{0,1\}^{N+1}$ that satisfies every constraint. $S$ is true if it has a witness and false otherwise. As $w_0 = 1$, entry $0$ of a vector is its form's constant term, and the factor $w_0$ makes every constraint homogeneous quadratic in $w$. With one product per constraint, $a_{t,1}(w)\, b_{t,1}(w) = c_t(w)$, a BQS is a rank-one constraint system (R1CS) with a Boolean witness. BQS express every NP relation: a witness-checking circuit gives one constraint per gate, such as $e_i(w)\, e_j(w) = w_0\, e_k(w)$ for an AND gate $w_k = w_i w_j$, plus $e_o(w)\, e_0(w) = w_0\, e_0(w)$, setting the output $w_o$ to $1$; a witness is an accepting input with the values of all gates. Shape. A system has shape $(N, n_{\mathsf{c}}, F_{\max})$ if it has $N$ unknowns, at most $n_{\mathsf{c}}$ constraints, and at most $F_{\max}$ products per constraint. Padding with constraints $0 = 0$ (one product, zero vectors) keeps the witnesses, so we assume exactly $n_{\mathsf{c}}$ constraints. The systems of one shape and their witnesses form the NP relation of the scheme. A system has linear size if $F_{\max}\,n_{\mathsf{c}} = O(N)$ and it has $O(N)$ nonzero coefficients; the systems of Boolean circuits above have linear size, with $F_{\max} = 1$ and $n_{\mathsf{c}} \le N + 1$.

### `blob:tests`: Definition 4.2, Moment matrices, linear tests, passing matrices, cheats (definition)

Rows and columns of matrices in $\mathbb{F}_q^{(N+1) \times (N+1)}$ have indices $0, \dots, N$. For an assignment $w$, every condition on a witness is linear in its moment matrix $w w^\top$: $w_j \in \{0,1\}$ exactly when $w_j^2 = w_0 w_j$, and constraint $t$ holds exactly when $\sum_f a_{t,f}^\top w w^\top b_{t,f} = e_0^\top w w^\top c_t$. The tests ask the same of any symmetric matrix $W$. Linear tests. Let $S$ be a system in $N$ unknowns. A symmetric matrix $W \in \mathbb{F}_q^{(N+1) \times (N+1)}$ passes the Booleanity test of $j \in [N]$ if $W_{jj} = W_{0j}$, and the test of constraint $t$ if $\sum_{f \in [F_t]} a_{t,f}^\top W\, b_{t,f} = e_0^\top W c_t$. $W$ passes if it passes every test of $S$. The passing matrices form a subspace, and $w w^\top$ passes exactly when the assignment $w$ is a witness. A cheat is a passing matrix that is not a linear combination of moment matrices of witnesses; a false system can have cheats too.

### `blob:sampler-search`: Definition 5.3, The centered sampler, the center, and the search (algorithm)

Fix an integer $g \ge 1$ with $g + 1 < q$, the range, and the search bound $B := (N+1)(g+1)$, with $2B + 1 < q$. Praxis hides a vector $r = (r_0, r_1, \dots, r_N)$ of small integers, the search vector, and decryption with a witness $w$ ends by searching for the integer $r(w) = \sum_i w_i r_i$. For $w \in \{0,1\}^{N+1}$ with $w_0 = 1$, $\operatorname{wt}(w)$ is the number of ones among $w_1, \dots, w_N$. Centered sampler. For an integer $g \ge 1$ with $g + 1 < q$, the centered sampler $\mathcal{D}_g$ draws $r_1, \dots, r_N, r_\star$ independently and uniformly from $\{2, \dots, g+1\}$ and outputs the integer vector $r = (r_0, r_1, \dots, r_N)$ with $r_0 := \lceil N(g+1)/2 \rceil + r_\star - \lfloor \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i \in [N]} r_i \rfloor$, which is reduced modulo $q$ wherever it multiplies a matrix over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Center. With $\mathcal{D}_g$, the center of the search is $c_w := \lfloor \lceil N(g+1)/2 \rceil + \frac{g+3}{2} ( 1 + \operatorname{wt}(w) - N/2 ) \rfloor$. As $r_0$ subtracts half the sum of the others, each other $r_i$ enters $r(w) - c_w$ as $\pm \frac{1}{2} ( r_i - \frac{g+3}{2} )$, symmetric about zero, so with the same law for $w_i = 0$ and $1$. Through $r_\star$, $r_0$ is uniform on $g$ consecutive integers given the others. The scheme as first stated in Section 5.1 instead draws $r_0, \dots, r_N$ independently and uniformly from $\{2, \dots, g+1\}$ and has the center $c_w := \lfloor (1 + \operatorname{wt}(w))(g+3)/2 \rfloor$; the full specification (App. A) and the theorems use $\mathcal{D}_g$. Sign classes. The search compares elements of $\mathbb{G}_T$ up to sign. The sign class of $V \in \mathbb{G}_T$ is $\{V, -V\}$, and its name $\mathsf{name}(V)$ identifies it: $\mathsf{name}(V) = \mathsf{name}(V')$ exactly when $V' = \pm V$. The generic model gives names to every algorithm. The search. Given elements $\Psi^\alpha$ and $\Lambda'$ of $\mathbb{G}_T$, the search returns the integer $t$ of least absolute value with $|t| \le B$ and $t \cdot \Psi^\alpha = \Lambda'$, or reports that there is no such integer. The scheme states the search by its result: any algorithm that finds this integer serves. We search outward from the center: one fixed table of the names of the baby steps $b \cdot \Psi^\alpha$ for $0 \le b < \bar b$, with $\bar b$ of order $\sqrt{g}\, N^{1/4}$, serves $b$ and $-b$ at once, and the giant steps $\Lambda' - j(2\bar b - 1) \cdot \Psi^\alpha$ for $j = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$ cover the integers outward from $0$.

### `blob:stacks-gp`: Definition 4.4, Stacks, rank, guessing probability, heavy and light (definition)

### `blob:qap`: Definition 4.6, The QAP of a system; quotients and remainder of a witness (definition)

A pair $(a, b)$ of vectors stands for the product $a(w)\, b(w)$; the pair's term on a matrix $W$ is $a^\top W\, b$, and each test sets a sum of terms to zero. QAP of a system. Let $S$ be a system of shape $(N, n_{\mathsf{c}}, F_{\max})$. A choice of evaluation points for $S$ consists of points $d_0, \dots, d_N$ and $\rho$ of $\mathbb{F}_q$, of cosets $\mathcal{K}_1, \dots, \mathcal{K}_{n_{\mathsf{c}}}$ of the subgroup of order $\nu$ of $\mathbb{F}_q^*$, and, in each $\mathcal{K}_t$, of points $p_{t,1}, \dots, p_{t,F_t}$ and $p'_t$. To a matrix $W \in \mathbb{F}_q^{(N+1) \times (N+1)}$ the QAP assigns $P_W(X) := \sum_{i,j} W_{ij}\, A_i(X)\, B_j(X) = A(X)^\top W\, B(X)$, whose values on $\mathcal{P}$ are listed last. At $d_j$ with $j \in [N]$: $A(d_j) = e_j - e_0$, $B(d_j) = e_j$, $P_W(d_j) = W_{jj} - W_{0j}$. At $d_0$: $A = 0$, $B = e_0$, $P_W = 0$. At $\rho$: $A = e_0$, $B = e_0$, $P_W = W_{00}$. So for a polynomial of degree below $\nu\,n_{\mathsf{c}}$, the sums over the cosets $\mathcal{K}_t = \delta_t \mathcal{G}$ are, up to the nonzero factor $\nu$, the $n_{\mathsf{c}} \times n_{\mathsf{c}}$ Vandermonde matrix of the values $\delta_t^\nu$, distinct as the cosets are, applied to the polynomial's coefficients at the exponents divisible by $\nu$; they all vanish exactly when the polynomial is allowed. $R_W$ denotes the polynomial of degree below $\nu\,n_{\mathsf{c}}$ with the values $P_W(p)/Z_0(p)$ on the cosets. Quotients and remainder of a witness. For a witness $w$, $w w^\top$ passes, with corner $1$, and $P_{w w^\top} = A_w B_w$ for $A_w := \sum_i w_i A_i$ and $B_w := \sum_i w_i B_i$. ### `blob:forms-weights`: Definition 5.1, Forms of a matrix; the weights $U$ and $V$ from the extension field (definition)

The forms come from a uniform matrix over the ring $R_q$ of degree $d_R$, with $k$ rows and columns $0, \dots, \ell$, for $\ell := \lceil N/d_R \rceil$; its columns $1$ to $\ell$ form an MSIS instance. For $w \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ and $j \in [\ell]$, let $\hat w_j \in R_q$ be the polynomial $\sum_{s < d_R} w_{(j-1) d_R + s + 1}\, Y^s$, where $w_c := 0$ for $c > N$. The forms of $M$ are the linear forms $\varphi_0, \dots, \varphi_{n-1}$ on $\mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ with $\varphi_0(w) := w_0$, with $\varphi_e := 0$ for $0 < e < d_R$, and with $\varphi_{i d_R + s}(w)$ defined as the coefficient of $Y^s$ in $M_{i,0}\, w_0 + \sum_{j \in [\ell]} M_{i,j}\, \hat w_j$, for $i \in [k]$ and $0 \le s < d_R$. $\mathsf{Forms}(M)$ denotes the list of their coefficient vectors. A vector $v$ with $v_0 = 0$ is in the kernel of every form exactly when the columns $1, \dots, \ell$ of $M$, a matrix in $R_q^{k \times \ell}$, map $(\hat v_1, \dots, \hat v_\ell)$ to zero. Weights. Let $c_0 \in \mathbb{F}_q$ be such that $P(T) := T^n - c_0$ is irreducible, and write the elements of $\mathbb{F}_{q^n} := \mathbb{F}_q[T]/(P)$ as polynomials in $\mathbb{F}_q^{<n}[T]$ (polynomials of degree below $n$). Its Frobenius automorphism $\sigma$ maps $T$ to $\omega T$, where $\omega := c_0^{(q-1)/n}$ has order $n$; so $\omega^{n/2} = -1$, and $\sigma^j$ maps $a(T)$ to $a(\omega^j T)$. Together the forms are one form $z(w) := \sum_{e<n} \varphi_e(w)\, T^e$ with values in $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ and coefficient vector $z = \sum_{e<n} \varphi_e T^e \in \mathbb{F}_{q^n}^{N+1}$. For $x \in \mathbb{F}_q$ or a formal polynomial $x$, $z[x] := \sum_{e<n} x^e \varphi_e$ is $z$ with $x$ substituted for $T$. The weights are the coefficients of two products in $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$: $U(w) := z(w) \cdot \sigma^{n/2-1}(z(w)) = \sum_{k<n} U_k(w)\, T^k$ and $V(w) := z(w) \cdot \sigma^{n/2}(z(w)) = \sum_{j<n/2} V_j(w)\, T^{2j}$. The $n$ forms are the coefficients of the one form $z$, and $z(v) = 0$ exactly when $\varphi_e(v) = 0$ for every $e$. A conjugate $\sigma^j(z) = z[\omega^j T]$ rescales coefficient $e$ by $\omega^{je}$, so it is again a public form. The three consecutive conjugates $\sigma^{n/2-1}(z)$, $\sigma^{n/2}(z)$, and $\sigma^{n/2+1}(z)$ are $z[-T/\omega]$, $z[-T]$, and $z[-\omega T]$. The third product, $z \cdot \sigma^{n/2+1}(z)$, is the conjugate under $\sigma^{n/2+1}$ of $U$, as $\sigma^n$ is the identity, so it needs no copies of its own; and $V$ is fixed by $\sigma^{n/2}$, which maps $T$ to $-T$, so it has only even powers of $T$.

### `blob:copies-ties`: Sec. 5.1, Copies and shared factors, Copies, the grid of cells, ties, and the honest stack (definition)

Copies. Each weight has a copy of the table, whose exponent is that of the weight's power of $T$: copy $(\mathrm{U}, k)$, for $k \in [0, n-1]$, has the weight $U_k$ and the exponent $k$, and copy $(\mathrm{V}, j)$, for $j \in [0, n/2-1]$, the weight $V_j$ and the exponent $2j$. The copies of $U$ form $C_U$, those of $V$ form $C_V$, and each of the $3n/2$ copies $c \in C := C_U \cup C_V$ has weight $\xi_c$ and exponent $k_c$. Only the key copy $c_{\mathrm{key}} := (\mathrm{U}, 0)$, with weight $\xi_{\mathrm{key}} := U_0$, carries the key. Ties. For a stack $\mathbf{W} = (W_c)_{c \in C}$, let $W_U(T) := \sum_{c \in C_U} W_c\, T^{k_c}$ and $W_V(T) := \sum_{c \in C_V} W_c\, T^{k_c}$. The stack satisfies the ties if $P(T)$ divides its tie polynomials $t_1, t_2, t_3 \in \mathbb{F}_q[T]^{N+1}$: $t_1(T) := W_U(T)\, z[-T] - W_V(T)\, z[-T/\omega]$, $t_2(T) := W_U(T)\, z[-\omega T] - W_U(-\omega T)\, z[-T/\omega]$, and $t_3(T) := W_V(T)\, z[-\omega T] - W_U(-\omega T)\, z[-T]$. Read $W_U$ and $W_V$ as matrices over $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}$, and let $\sigma$ act on them entry by entry. The ties ask that $W_U\, \sigma^{n/2}(z) = W_V\, \sigma^{n/2-1}(z)$, $W_U\, \sigma^{n/2+1}(z) = \sigma^{n/2+1}(W_U)\, \sigma^{n/2-1}(z)$, and $W_V\, \sigma^{n/2+1}(z) = \sigma^{n/2+1}(W_U)\, \sigma^{n/2}(z)$, identities in $\mathbb{F}_{q^n}^{N+1}$ whose differences, before reduction modulo $P(T)$, are the tie polynomials. As $\sigma^j$ maps $a(T)$ to $a(\omega^j T)$ and $\omega^{n/2} = -1$, $P(T)$ divides the tie polynomials exactly if the stack satisfies the ties as stated in this second form. Honest stack. The stack $\mathbf{H}(v) := (\xi_c(v)\, v v^\top)_{c \in C}$ of every vector $v$ satisfies the ties; that of a witness is its honest stack. For the stack of $v$, $W_U$, $W_V$, and $\sigma^{n/2+1}(W_U) = W_U(-\omega T)$ are $v v^\top$ times the three products at $v$, and both sides of each tie are the same product of three conjugates of $z(v)$ times $v$; for the first, $z(v)\, \sigma^{n/2-1}(z(v))\, \sigma^{n/2}(z(v))\, v$. Cells. For positive integers $K_1$ and $K_2$ with $K_1 K_2 = n/2$, the copies are the cells of a $K_1 \times 3K_2$ grid, with one left factor for each row, a left leg $k_1 \in [0, K_1 - 1]$, and one right factor for each column, a right leg. A right leg $\mathrm{leg} = (\mathrm{X}, k_2) \in \{\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{V}\} \times [0, K_2 - 1]$ has kind $\mathrm{X}$ and exponent $k_{\mathrm{leg}} := 2K_1 k_2$, or $2K_1 k_2 + 1$ for kind O. The cell $c = (k_1, \mathrm{leg})$ is the copy of exponent $k_c = 2k_1 + k_{\mathrm{leg}}$: $(\mathrm{U}, k_c)$ on E and O legs and $(\mathrm{V}, k_c/2)$ on V legs; every copy is exactly one cell, and the key copy is $(0, (\mathrm{E}, 0))$.

(P3) The conditions (I1) to (I6) of the inverse theorem (Lemma B.7) on $(q, N, g, \theta, \beta)$, which include $g^{-4} < \theta$ and $2\beta < q$. Under them, a nonzero vector in the row space of a heavy stack on which all forms vanish has a short multiple, which solves MSIS. (P4) $24\beta^4 < q^3$, so that the lifting step finds a short multiple if one exists, in polynomial time (Lemma B.9). (P5) $E(2) \le 2^{-\lambda}$ and $E(2^{\lambda+1}) - E(2) \le 1 - 2^{-\lambda}$ for the quadratic polynomial $E$ of Definition B.1, an explicit bound on what is left to chance, so that the proof's generic term $E(2Q+2)$ is at most $(Q+1)/2^\lambda$ (Lemma B.2); this requires $\theta < 2^{-(2\lambda+1)}$.

### `blob:monitor`: App. B.4, Runs, the monitor, good and charged marks, Runs, the monitor, and first marks (good, charged) (definition)

Runs. The run $\mathsf{R}_m$ is the experiment of extractability until $\mathcal{A}$ outputs, with $\mathsf{Enc}$ encrypting $m$ and no bit $b$; the $L$ labels given to $\mathcal{A}$ are received. On every run with $m_0$ or $m_1$, $\mathcal{A}$ makes at most $Q < 2^\lambda$ calls to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$, rejected ones included, and at most $Q_H \le 2^\lambda$ queries to $H_{\mathsf{key}}$; we may assume it on every sequence of answers, as stopping $\mathcal{A}$ before a call or query beyond these changes no such run. The label of $[\kappa]_T$ is the target label. The monitor. A label that $\mathcal{A}$ guesses gets a new guess variable (M2); so a generic expression is an expression over $\mathcal{X}_1$ and $\mathcal{X}_2$ enlarged by guess variables of $\mathbb{G}_1$ and $\mathbb{G}_2$, plus, in $\mathbb{G}_T$, a linear combination of guess variables of $\mathbb{G}_T$. Let $\mathsf{X}^\circ$ be $\mathsf{X}$ with every guess variable set to zero. Generic expressions are formally equal if the coefficients of each product of guess variables in them are, the empty product included, and $\mathsf{X}$ has a guess variable if it is not formally equal to $\mathsf{X}^\circ$. The monitor, a function of a finished run for the analysis only, reads the calls of $\mathcal{A}$ to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$ and its queries to $H_{\mathsf{key}}$, in order, and keeps for each group a table of entries, labels with generic expressions. It stops when it sets a mark, the run's first mark; a run in $\mathsf{Tgt}$ has one (M4). (M1) Start. The received labels are entered with the expressions of their elements. The mark is $\mathsf{init}$ if two received elements of a group have one label and expressions that are not formally equal. (M2) Operand. An operand string of a linear operation or pairing, answered or rejected, that is a label of its group and has no entry, a guessed label, is entered with a new guess variable. (M3) Result. A result is that of an answered linear operation or pairing, computed from the entries of its operands, or, at the first answered name request on a label $\mathsf{u}$, the negative of the entry of $\mathsf{u}$, at the other label of its name. A result $\mathsf{F}$ at a label without an entry is entered; at a label with an entry $\mathsf{X}$ not formally equal to $\mathsf{F}$, the mark is $\mathsf{coll}$, or $\mathsf{diff}(\mathsf{F}^\circ - \mathsf{X}^\circ)$ if the group is $\mathbb{G}_T$ and $\mathsf{F} - \mathsf{X}$ has no guess variable. (M4) Target. The first query of $H_{\mathsf{key}}$ at the target label sets $\mathsf{key}(\mathsf{X}^\circ)$ if the label has an entry $\mathsf{X}$, but $\mathsf{coll}$ if $\mathsf{X}$ has a guess variable, and $\mathsf{guess}$ if it has no entry. Good and charged marks. A mark $\mathsf{diff}(\mathsf{D})$ or $\mathsf{key}(\mathsf{D})$ has the expression $\mathsf{D}$ and the multiple $\chi = 0$ or $1$. Its mark stack is $\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{plain}}(\mathsf{D})$ if this stack is nonzero and $\mathbf{W}^{\alpha}(\mathsf{D})$ otherwise, and its mark sector is this stack's sector. The mark is good if $\mathsf{D}$ is $\chi\kappa$ identically at the run's search vector $r$ and its mark stack is nonzero and heavy; this stack is then usable (by the view lemma, Lemma B.4(a)), of rank at most three (by the heavy stacks lemma, Lemma B.8(a)). A first mark is charged if it is $\mathsf{coll}$, or has an expression and is not good; so a first mark is $\mathsf{init}$, $\mathsf{guess}$, charged, or good.

### `blob:symbolic-game`: App. B.4, The coupling and the symbolic game, The coupling and the symbolic game (definition)

The coupling. Let $\mathsf{R}_\$$ be $\mathsf{R}_m$ with a uniform string $h$ in place of the masked message $m \oplus H_{\mathsf{key}}([\kappa]_T)$, so $\mathsf{Enc}$ does not query $H_{\mathsf{key}}$. In $\mathsf{R}_m$ the value of $H_{\mathsf{key}}$ at the target label is uniform and enters only the masked message and answers to queries there. So $\mathsf{R}_m$ has the law of $\mathsf{R}_\$$ with $H_{\mathsf{key}}$ answering $h \oplus m$ at the target label, and on the same coins they agree up to and including the first query of $\mathcal{A}$ there, without its answer. This part, with the label injections and the coins of $\mathsf{Enc}$, determines $\mathsf{Tgt}$ and the first mark; so their probabilities may be computed in $\mathsf{R}_\$$, where $h$ and $H_{\mathsf{key}}$ are independent of the label injections and the coins of $\mathsf{Enc}$. The symbolic game. In the symbolic game, $\mathcal{A}$ runs as in $\mathsf{R}_\$$, but $\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf{GG}}$ knows only the images of the label injections, the sets of labels: it keeps the monitor's table by (M1) to (M3), giving each received element and each result the label of the entry formally equal to it or else a uniformly random label of its group without an entry, which exists by (P2). It rejects calls as in the generic bilinear group model, so a name request unless it is on a received label of $\mathbb{G}_T$ or the answer to a linear operation in $\mathbb{G}_T$ or a pairing, and answers one with the name of its label and its result's. So the game uses no coin of $\mathsf{Enc}$, which allows a union bound; the evaluation, made afterwards, samples $(\mathbf{c}, r)$ as $\mathsf{Enc}$ does and an independent uniform value in $\mathbb{F}_q$ for every guess variable, giving every entry a value. A collision is a pair of entries $\mathsf{X}$, $\mathsf{X}'$ of one group, not formally equal, with equal values; it occurs when the later of the two is made, and has $\mathsf{D} := \mathsf{X} - \mathsf{X}'$ and $\chi := 0$. A key value is an entry $\mathsf{X}$ of $\mathbb{G}_T$ of value $\kappa$; it has $\mathsf{D} := \mathsf{X}$ and $\chi := 1$. Either event is good if $\mathsf{D}$ lies in $\mathbb{G}_T$, has no guess variable, and meets the conditions of a good mark at the evaluation's $r$, and bad otherwise.

### `blob:records`: App. B.4, Records, Records kept by the reductions and the extractor (definition)

The reductions and the extractor follow the answered calls and keep for a label of each group a computable record of an expression: a reduction its value at a point and its formula in the calls, the extractor its sketch data. They start from the received labels, give an operand without a record that of $0$, which it keeps, compute the record of a result from those of its operands, and keep it if its label has none. Until the monitor sets a mark, a label with a record has an entry $\mathsf{X}$, and the record is that of an expression formally equal to $\mathsf{X}^\circ$; an entry whose label has no record is a guess variable that no answered call had as an operand, so it occurs in no result. By induction: $\mathsf{X} \mapsto \mathsf{X}^\circ$ commutes with linear combinations and pairings, and a result that sets no mark meets no entry or one formally equal to it. So at a first mark $\mathsf{diff}(\mathsf{D})$, set by a result $\mathsf{F}$ at a label with the entry $\mathsf{X}$, the label has a record, at the latest from this call if it is one of its operands, as $\mathsf{F} - \mathsf{X}$ has no guess variable, and the result's record minus the label's is that of $\mathsf{D}$, up to formal equality. Formally equal expressions of $\mathbb{G}_T$ have equal stacks, by the view lemma (Lemma B.4(a)) for their difference at every $r$, so equal sketches.

### `blob:reductions`: App. B.5, The reductions (R1) to (R5), The reductions $\mathcal{B}_0$ and $\mathcal{B}_1$ (algorithm)

Each reduction $\mathcal{B}_b$ of the extraction theorem tries up to two expressions from a private monitor and, if one is a good first mark's, recovers the row space of its mark stack from three random combinations of the rows, gradients in $r$, which cost $O(1)$ operations for each call of $\mathcal{A}$, while an expression has $|\mathcal{X}_1| |\mathcal{X}_2| + 1$ coefficients: (R1) Run. (R2) Coins. Sample independently, also of the run, a private test point $\mathsf{a}_{\mathrm{p}}$ and, for $l \in [3]$ and each sector $\mathsf{s}$, a recovery point $\mathsf{a}^{(l,\mathsf{s})}$: uniformly random valid tuples, each with a uniform vector of $\mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$ in place of $r$. (R3) Private monitor. Keep records with the values at $\mathsf{a}_{\mathrm{p}}$ to the end of the run. (R4) Gradients. For each $l$ and $\mathsf{s}$, compute $\nabla^{(l,\mathsf{s})}(\mathsf{D}) \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$, the gradient in $r$ at $\mathsf{a}^{(l,\mathsf{s})}$ of the value of the part of $\mathsf{D}$ in sector $\mathsf{s}$: with the exponents taken there, its entry $j$ is $\alpha_{\mathsf{s}} \sum_{\mathrm{leg}} s_{\mathrm{leg}} \sum_{a \in \mathcal{X}_1} a\, \mathsf{D}_{a b}$ for $b = (\mathsf{H}^{\mathrm{leg}}_{j,1})^{\mathsf{s}}$. (R5) Output. Find a kernel vector $v$ in the span of the $\nabla^{(l,\mathsf{s})}(\mathsf{D})$ of the plain sector or, if there is none, of the alpha sector. If $v$ exists and $\mathsf{LIFT}(v)$ returns $x$, output $\hat x$ and stop. Here $\mathsf{LIFT}$ is the algorithm of the lifting step (Lemma B.9), which returns a short multiple of $v$, or $\bot$ if there is none, and $\hat x \in R^\ell$ has the entries $\hat x_j$ of the definition of forms, taken over $\mathbb{Z}$. Running times count operations: arithmetic operations and equality tests in $\mathbb{F}_q$; additions, subtractions, halvings, and comparisons of integers of absolute value below $2^{\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}}$; XORs and writes of strings of at most $\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}$ bits or elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$; uniform samples from an explicitly given finite set, or from the elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$ or strings of length $\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}$ that are not keys of a table; oracle calls; and insertions into and lookups in tables keyed by labels, names, oracle inputs, or elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$, where an insertion at a key that is present keeps and returns the stored value. They also count the length of the algorithm's description, advice included, one for each element of $\mathbb{F}_q$ or string of length $\ell_{\mathsf{lab}}$, with code packed into such strings. The oracle calls of $\mathcal{A}$ count in its running time $t$, the lifting step counts bit operations on its integers, and the constants in $O()$ are absolute.

### `blob:extractor`: App. B.6, Sketches and tests, The extractor $\mathcal{E}$: sketches and tests (algorithm)

Sketches. $\mathcal{E}$ samples, for every cell $c$, $l \in [3]$, and sector $\mathsf{s}$, an independent uniform probe vector $v^{(l,\mathsf{s})}_c \in \mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$. The sketches of an expression $\mathsf{D}$ of $\mathbb{G}_T$ in sector $\mathsf{s}$ are the vectors $\sum_c W^{\mathsf{s}}_c(\mathsf{D})\, v^{(l,\mathsf{s})}_c$, linear in $\mathsf{D}$. ### `blob:lattices`: App. B.7, Boxes, prefixes, lattices, Boxes, prefixes, and lattice vocabulary (saturated, height, fibers, regular) (definition)

In the proof of the inverse theorem, $v$ is the vector of that theorem: nonzero, in $\mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}$, with $v_0 = 0$ and $\operatorname{gp}(v^\top) > \theta$. Symbols other than the theorem's are local to its proof. Boxes and prefixes. For a finite set $I$, the box on $I$ is $[2, g+1]^I$, and $r_I$ is uniform on it, as $r_1, \dots, r_N$ are independent and uniform on $[2, g+1]$ for both samplers. The density of a set $F$ of box points is $\Pr[F] := |F|/g^{|I|}$; a condition on the entries at $I' \subseteq I$ has the same density in the boxes on $I'$ and $I$. For $u \in \mathbb{F}_q^I$, $\operatorname{gp}(u^\top)$ is the largest density of a modular fiber $\{ r : u^\top r = t \bmod q \}$, and $\operatorname{gp}(v^\top)$ is the guessing probability of the matrix $v^\top$, as $v_0 = 0$. Renumbering $1, \dots, N$, let $v_1, \dots, v_{N_0}$ be the nonzero ones among $v_1, \dots, v_N$, and $v_{[i]} := (v_1, \dots, v_i)$; a short multiple of $v_{[N_0]}$ (defined as for $v$), extended by zeros, is one of $v$ with $x_0 = 0$. Conditioning on $r_{i+1}$ shows that $\operatorname{gp}(v_{[i]}^\top)$ does not increase with $i$, and $g + 1 < q$ makes $x \mapsto v_1 x \bmod q$ injective on $[2, g+1]$; so $\theta < \operatorname{gp}(v^\top) = \operatorname{gp}(v_{[N_0]}^\top) \le \operatorname{gp}(v_{[i]}^\top) \le 1/g$ for $i \in [N_0]$. Lattices. A lattice $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$ of rank $d$ has basis matrices $B \in \mathbb{Z}^{I \times d}$, unique up to $B \mapsto BU$ for a unimodular $U$; $B_J$ denotes the rows $J \subseteq I$ of $B$. It is saturated if $\Theta = \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Theta) \cap \mathbb{Z}^I$, as are the integer points of any subspace; two saturated lattices of equal rank, one inside the other, are equal. For $d \ge 1$, its height $h(\Theta)$ is the largest $|\det B_J|$ over the sets $J$ of $d$ indices, and it has a largest minor at $J$ if $|\det B_J| = h(\Theta)$. Its fibers are the sets $\{ r : B^\top r = t \}$ of box points, for $t \in \mathbb{Z}^d$; its largest fiber density $m(\Theta)$ is the largest density of one, at most $g^{-d}$, as fixing the entries outside a set $J$ with $\det B_J \ne 0$ leaves at most one value of $r_J$, as in Odlyzko's lemma; and it is regular if $d \in [3]$ and $m(\Theta)\, h(\Theta) \le \Gamma_d/g^d$. As $(BU)^\top = U^\top B^\top$, none of these depends on $B$. The reduction of $\Theta$ modulo $q$ is $\bar\Theta := \{ x \bmod q : x \in \Theta \} = B\, \mathbb{F}_q^d$. A nonempty $F \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$ has affine dimension $\dim F$, that of the span of its differences, and normal lattice $F^\perp$ of the integer vectors whose inner product with the points of $F$ is constant, saturated of rank $|I| - \dim F$, as the orthogonal complement of a span of integer vectors has a basis of integer vectors. A set $F$ of box points lies in a fiber of $F^\perp$, so $\Pr[F] \le m(F^\perp)$, and if it lies in a fiber of $\Theta$, then $\Theta \subseteq F^\perp$.

### `blob:trimming`: App. B.7, Trimming, Trimming ellipsoids and trimmed sets (definition)

For a lattice $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^I$ of rank $d \in [3]$, with $|I| \le N$ and a basis matrix $B$, let $\mathcal{T}_\Theta$ be the trimming ellipsoid of the $y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ with $(y - y_\Theta)^\top \Sigma_\Theta^{-1} (y - y_\Theta) \le R_{\mathrm{tr}}^2$, where $y_\Theta := \frac{g+3}{2} B^\top \mathbf{1}$, for the all-ones vector $\mathbf{1}$, is the mean of $B^\top r_I$ and $\Sigma_\Theta := \frac{g^2}{12} B^\top B$. A set $F$ of points of a box on indices containing $I$ is trimmed by $\Theta$ if $B^\top r_I \in \mathcal{T}_\Theta$ for all $r \in F$, and $F$ trimmed by $\Theta$ is the set of these $r \in F$. Neither depends on $B$: replacing $B$ by $BU$ replaces $B^\top r_I - y_\Theta$ by $U^\top (B^\top r_I - y_\Theta)$ and $\Sigma_\Theta$ by $U^\top \Sigma_\Theta U$.
