# Praxis with a dense rank-one inverse condition

## Amendment Ncmp

For the variable \(u\) used by the bound \(E\), set
\[
N_{\mathrm{cmp}}(u)=\frac{u(u+10)}8 .
\]
The remaining formula for \(E\), the values of \(u=2Q+2\) used by the proof, and the two tests in (P5) are as specified in the protected model. The new count applies to the bad events that can pay a \(\theta\) charge. The other pair and entry terms continue to pay for the full collection of possible coincidences.

### Coupling and bad events

Fix the uniform matrix realization \(M\) for the expressions, and fix the adversary's call budget \(Q\). The run up to the first query at the target label has the same law as the run with a uniform string in place of the masked message up to that query without its answer, by the coupling of the protected symbolic-game block. In the latter run the oracle answers and the uniform string can be chosen independently of the valid encryption coins and the search vector. The symbolic simulation with formal equality, including its label images, can therefore be sampled before evaluating the expressions at those coins. An entry introduced on a guessed operand is a new formal variable of its group. The simulation uses at most three new entries per call, for two operands and one result. Received entries are present at initialization. If a result is already formally equal to an entry, the simulation returns that label rather than inserting a new entry.

Evaluate the symbolic simulation at the independent coins and uniformly independent guess-variable values. Within a group a collision means equality of evaluated values for two formally distinct entries. One can couple the evaluated symbolic and random-mask runs until the first such collision. In the real run conditional on the label images, each unused label in any of the three groups, when guessed, has a uniform exponent among the field values not already assigned in its group, and each evaluated result with a new value receives a uniform unused label. Use the same fresh labels in the symbolic and real simulations for new values. Use the uniform guess-variable value for the real guess if it is new in that group, and redraw from the unused field values if it is already assigned. This has the required uniform unused law and disagrees only at a collision. Inductively, before a collision all assigned labels and their expressions agree between the two simulations, and calls and oracle queries agree because the adversary sees the same transcript. The first call on which a collision occurs is common to both runs, even when the answers at that call differ.

The initial collisions are an \(\mathsf{init}\) mark. Suppose a non-initial collision occurs first, before a target-label query has set a mark. If the collision is good in the protected symbolic-game sense, its two entries lie in the target group and their difference has no guess variable. Its later entry cannot be a guessed operand, because that would introduce a new formal variable absent from the earlier entry. It is a result. The real result takes the earlier label, so the monitor sets a good difference mark. Thus a collision that gives a charged first mark must be a bad event. If a target-label query sets a charged first mark before a collision, the queried label has an entry, since otherwise the mark is \(\mathsf{guess}\). This entry has the target value and its expression is bad by the monitor definition. If the first evaluated-symbolic collision instead triggers a redraw of a guessed operand in the real simulation, that collision already contains the fresh guess variable and is bad. It covers any later charged mark even if the real monitor made no mark at the redraw. Hence a charged first mark is covered by the bad collisions after initialization and the bad key-value events in the symbolic simulation.

For a fixed simulation a collision uses the difference expression \(D\) and \(\chi=0\), and a key-value event uses the entry \(D\) and \(\chi=1\). We bound the probability of a bad event over its evaluation. In either source group, if the difference has no guess variable, the source-group coincidence estimate of the protected view lemma bounds its probability by \(\varepsilon_f\). If the expression contains a guess variable in any group, there is a nonzero formal coefficient of a guess monomial. Such a coefficient is either a nonzero field constant or a formally nonzero expression in one of the source groups, since products of guesses arise only through a pairing of source-group expressions. In the latter case for every fixed \(r\) it is nonzero on some valid encryption coins by the source-group coincidence estimate and \(\varepsilon_f<1\). Thus after clearing the factor \(\varpi\), the event equation is a nonzero polynomial in coins and guess values for each fixed \(r\). Its total degree is at most \(d_f+1\). The terms without guesses have degree at most \(d_f\) by the degree lemma. A term with one guess multiplying a source-group expression adds one degree to the cleared source expression, and terms with two guess variables and no other exponents have degree at most seven after clearing. All independent sampled field coordinates have support size at least \(q-D\). The polynomial root bound gives probability at most \((d_f+1)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\).

The remaining case is a guess-free target-group difference or key-value expression. For any \(r\) at which \(D\) is not identically \(\chi\kappa\), the probability of coincidence over coins is at most \(\varepsilon_f\) by the view lemma. For an \(r\) at which it is identical, the mark stack is nonzero. For a collision this follows since the two entries were formally distinct, and for a key-value expression it follows from the no-formal-key conclusion of the view lemma. The view lemma also places every such \(r\) in a single fiber of the fixed stack, specified by the stack's fixed vectors \(u_c\). Thus the event in this case is bad only for a light stack, and the probability over \(r\) is at most \(\theta\). The combined bound on the bad event is \(\theta+\varepsilon_f\).

### Counting the light-stack charges

Only the last case uses the \(\theta\) summand. At call \(j\) a collision in this case must have a result as its later entry, since an operand entering as a fresh guessed entry has its own new guess variable. Among earlier target-group entries, the received ones number at most two and results of earlier calls at most \(j-1\). All remaining earlier target-group entries are single guessed variables introduced as operands. For a fixed result expression the part involving guess variables can be exactly one of those variables at most once. Thus the result can have guess-free difference with at most one entry of this last type, even if one or both operands of call \(j\) have just been guessed. Indeed if it had guess-free difference with two distinct such entries, their subtraction would be guess-free by linearity, whereas it is the difference of two distinct formal variables. This gives at most \(j+2\) candidate target-group comparisons at call \(j\). The possible guess-free key-value entries in the target group number at most \(Q+2\) since they must be received elements or results; any fresh guessed entry contains its guessed variable. Therefore the total number of events that can incur a \(\theta\) charge is at most
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^Q(j+2)+Q+2
 = \frac{Q(Q+1)}2+3Q+2
 = \frac{u(u+10)}8-1 .
\]
The full count for all other bad events is bounded by the number of pairs plus the number of entries in a table of at most \(L+3Q\) entries, and hence by \(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\). The pair count may overcount comparisons between different groups, which only increases the bound. Combining the union bounds establishes the amended charged-mark estimate
\[
 \Pr[\text{charged first mark}]
 \le N_{\mathrm{cmp}}(u)(\theta+\varepsilon_f)
       +\left(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\right)\varepsilon_f .
\]
This argument holds for every realized \(M\), independent of the form packing.

### Generic ledger and scope of the amendment

The generic ledger has the same implication from (P5). In the amended \(E\), all coefficients as a polynomial in \(u\) are nonnegative and its degree is two. Here \(L\ge1\), \(N_{\mathrm{free}}>0\) by (P2), and \(\binom{L+3u}{2}\) expands with nonnegative constant, linear and quadratic coefficients. The new \(N_{\mathrm{cmp}}\) has nonnegative coefficients. For real \(Q\) on \([0,2^\lambda-1]\), the function \(Q\mapsto E(2Q+2)-(Q+1)/2^\lambda\) is convex because its quadratic coefficient is nonnegative. At the first endpoint it is at most zero by the first P5 test. At the second it is \(E(2^{\lambda+1})-1\), at most zero by adding the P5 tests. By convexity it is at most zero throughout. The threshold side conclusion of the generic ledger becomes \(\theta<2^{1-2\lambda}\) for \(\lambda\ge1\). Indeed putting \(U=2^{\lambda+1}\), we have \(N_{\mathrm{cmp}}(U)-N_{\mathrm{cmp}}(2)=U^2/8+5U/4-3>2^{2\lambda-1}\). Every other term in \(E(U)-E(2)\) is nonnegative, so the second P5 bound implies the stated strict threshold limit.

The changed count enters the admissibility block only through the value of \(E\) in P5. The expression, monitor and symbolic-game blocks are independent of the count: they specify the same formal polynomial expressions, oracle label sampling and classifications of marks and events. The view lemma uses degree, coin ranges, algebraic identities of the factors and polynomial root bounds, all independent of this count. The implication \(\varepsilon_f<1\) used by the comparisons follows as before because \(E(2)\ge24\varepsilon_f\) from its fourth term and (P5) remains in force. The factor-one-half lemma concerns the uniform-mask coupling and the two actual failure probabilities, so its argument has no count or bound \(E\) as a premise. The initial and guessed-label bounds use only the distinct received-expression comparisons and the uniform as-yet-unassigned target label respectively. The reduction lemma uses the private polynomial degree, gradient distribution, rank and short-vector properties, with no \(\theta\)-charge count. Its success probability refers to the same run and the same instance. Thus those results in the background list of the bound block apply with the same statements and arguments. The charged-mark and generic-ledger results are proved above, and extraction is derived in Targets.

The extractability-error corollary follows with its original statement from Targets and the factor-one-half lemma. For allowable budgets, in the combined bit experiment with uniform \(b\), put \(f_i=\Pr[\mathsf{Tgt}\wedge\mathsf{Fail}\mid b=i]\). The probability of both a correct bit and extraction failure is at most \(1/2+(f_0+f_1)/4\). The target-query bound in Targets gives \(f_i\le (Q+1)/2^\lambda+s_i\) for each \(i\). Both reductions run in time bounded by \(t_R=t+O(Q+d_R Q_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\), including fixed description and advice, so each \(s_i\) is bounded by \(\operatorname{Adv}^{\mathrm{MSIS}}(t_R)\) by its defining maximum. Substitution gives extractability error at most \((Q+1)/2^{\lambda+1}+\operatorname{Adv}^{\mathrm{MSIS}}(t_R)\) at the same extractor time. Outside the stated budgets, the trivial probability upper bound 1 yields error \(1/2\) as in the corollary. This accounts for every result in the background list of the bound block.

## Amendment I4

Replace (I4) by the two inequalities
\[
 g^2\mu_{\rm tr}\ge 2^{32},\qquad
 \Gamma_1\ge\frac{1048577}{1048576}=1+2^{-20}.
\]
They restrict the theorem to a regime where no rank-two or rank-three lattice can have the required fiber density. The other inverse conditions and the meanings of all variables remain those in the model. We prove the regularity and inverse conclusions directly under the displayed condition.

### Fiber density and rank-one regularity

For any rank-\(d\) lattice on integer box coordinates, its largest fiber density is at most \(g^{-d}\). Choose \(d\) rows of an integer basis matrix with nonzero determinant. Conditional on all other coordinates, the \(d\) equations specifying a fiber then determine the selected \(d\) coordinates if a solution exists. Their independent uniform distributions give conditional probability at most \(g^{-d}\), hence the same unconditional bound. This also applies to any normal lattice of a set contained in the box. Since \(g^2\mu_{\rm tr}\ge 2^{32}\), one has \(g^{-2}<\mu_{\rm tr}\). Thus if \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}\) and the rank is positive, the rank is one.

For a rank-one saturated lattice \(\Theta\), order the largest row minor first and use the prefix-lattice statement (a) and density bound in (b). These statements take the integer basis, its successive row-group indices, and \(g\); they do not use (I4). Initially the height is 1 and the density is \(1/g\). The nontrivial integral step orders \(s_1,\ldots,s_a\), each at least 2, multiply to \(h(\Theta)\). The density satisfies
\[
 m(\Theta)\le \frac1g F,\qquad
 F=\prod_{j=1}^a \frac{\lceil g/s_j\rceil}{g}.
\]
An omitted step order of 1 has density factor exactly 1. In particular, if \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}\), then \(F\ge g\mu_{\rm tr}>2^{-254}\) because \(g^2\mu_{\rm tr}\ge2^{32}\) and \(g<2^{254}\). Each of the \(a\) factors is at most \(1/2+1/g\le 1/2+1/128<2^{-1/2}\) by \(g\ge128\). Therefore \(a<508\) whenever \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}\).

Each factor is also at most 1, so \(\lceil g/s_j\rceil/g\ge F\ge g\mu_{\rm tr}\) for any \(j\). Since \(\lceil g/s_j\rceil/g\le 1/s_j+1/g\), this yields
\[
 \frac{s_j}{g}\le\frac1{g^2\mu_{\rm tr}-1}\le\frac1{2^{32}-1}.
\]
Multiplying the density upper bound by \(h(\Theta)=\prod_j s_j\), we obtain
\[
 m(\Theta) h(\Theta)\le\frac1g\prod_{j=1}^a(1+s_j/g)
       \le\frac1g(1+1/(2^{32}-1))^{508}.
\]
The last power is at most \(1/(1-508/(2^{32}-1))<1+2^{-20}\): expand the binomial and bound the coefficient of its \(i\)-th term by \(508^i\), then sum the geometric series. The strict comparison follows from \(508\cdot 2^{20}<2^{32}-1-508\). Consequently \(m(\Theta)h(\Theta)\le \Gamma_1/g\), exactly rank-one regularity. This includes a prefix with no nontrivial steps by the empty product. In particular \(h(\Theta)\le H_1=\Gamma_1/(g\mu_{\rm tr})\) for a lattice at the required density.

The rank-three part (c) of the prefix lemma has premise \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}\) with rank three, which is impossible by the fiber bound. It therefore retains both conclusions under this amended condition. The regularization lemma also retains its whole statement: in ranks two and three its premise is impossible, and in rank one any saturated input with \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}+2\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\) is regular by the preceding argument. Taking that input itself gives the required containment, rank and density.

### Inverse theorem under the amended condition

Let \(v\ne0\) in \(\mathbb F_q^{N+1}\) with \(v_0=0\) and \(\operatorname{gp}(v^\top)>\theta\), as in the inverse-theorem statement. Remove its zero coordinates and enumerate the nonzero ones as \(v_1,\ldots,v_{N_0}\). All the corresponding search coordinates are independently uniform on the integer box. For \(1\le i\le N_0\), let \(p_i\) be the maximal density of a modular fiber of \(v_{[i]}^\top\). Conditioning on the next box entry gives \(p_{i+1}\le p_i\), and the first coordinate gives \(p_1=1/g\) because \(v_1\ne0\) and \(g+1<q\). Therefore \(p_i\ge p_{N_0}>\theta\) for each \(i\).

We construct a rank-one regular saturated lattice \(\Theta\) on each prefix such that \(v_{[i]}\) lies in its reduction and \(m(\Theta)\ge p_i-\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\). For \(i=1\), take \(\mathbb Z\), which has \(m=1/g\) and \(h=1\) and is regular since \(\Gamma_1\ge1\). Assume the construction at \(i<N_0\). Let \(B\) be its single integer basis column. Its maximum absolute entry is \(h(\Theta)\le H_1\), using the density condition \(m>\theta-\varepsilon_{\rm tr}>\mu_{\rm tr}\) and regularity. There is a \(c'\in\mathbb F_q\) with \(v_{[i]}=Bc'\bmod q\).

Take a densest modular fiber of the next prefix and trim it by \(\Theta\). The trimming lemma, which depends on the original exponential loss condition and is unchanged here, leaves a set \(F_*\) of density at least \(p_{i+1}-\varepsilon_{\rm tr}>\mu_{\rm tr}\). Let \(\Phi(r)=(B^\top r_{[i]},r_{i+1})\) and \(c=(c',v_{i+1})\). Its image lies in a coset of the index-\(q\) subgroup \(c^\top z=0\bmod q\) because \(v_{i+1}\ne0\). If that image had affine dimension two, the protected sparse-count lemma (a) with the unchanged \(\Lambda_1\) would give
\[
  \Pr[F_*]\le\Lambda_1 g h(\Theta)m(\Theta)/q
            \le\Lambda_1\Gamma_1/q<\mu_{\rm tr}
\]
by regularity and (I5). Thus it has affine dimension at most one.

Let \(\Delta_*\) be the integer matrix whose columns are differences of image points from a fixed image point. The entry differences in its last row are at most \(g-1<T_{\rm tr}\). In its first row, the trimming ellipsoid bounds the difference by \(2R_{\rm tr}(g/\sqrt{12})\|B\|_2\): the one-dimensional covariance in the ellipsoid is \((g^2/12)B^\top B\). Since \(\|B\|_2\le\sqrt N\,h(\Theta)\), this is at most \(T_{\rm tr}h(\Theta)\). If the rank is one, its \(1\)-row minors are therefore bounded by \(H_1T_{\rm tr}<q\) by (I5). We have \(c^\top\Delta_*=0\bmod q\). By the integer-lattice lemma (c), if the rank is one there is an integral \(u\equiv c\bmod q\) with \(u^\top\Delta_*=0\); if the rank is zero the same conclusion holds for any lift of \(c\). Thus \((Bu_1,u_2)\) is an integral normal to \(F_*\) that reduces to \(v_{[i+1]}\) and is nonzero. The normal lattice \(F_*^\perp\) is saturated since the defining normal equations are integer-linear, and has rank at most one because the fiber containing \(F_*\) has density above \(g^{-2}\). It has rank at least one from this vector and \(m(F_*^\perp)\ge\Pr[F_*]\ge p_{i+1}-\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\). It is regular by the new rank-one estimate and gives the next prefix. The induction is proved.

In the final lattice let its single basis column be \(B_*\). It is nonzero, \(v_{[N_0]}\) lies in its reduction, and \(\|B_*\|_\infty=h(\Theta)\le H_1\le\beta\) by (I6). The reduction of \(B_*\) is nonzero and spans the line of \(v_{[N_0]}\), since the latter is nonzero. Extending \(B_*\) by zeros in the removed positions, including the leading position, gives an integer short multiple \(x\) with \(x_0=0\), \(0<\|x\|_\infty\le\beta\), and \(x\bmod q\) a nonzero multiple of \(v\). This proves the inverse theorem with the stated replacement of (I4), and in particular the conclusion needed for the original norm-bounded SIS reduction.

### Scope of the proof

The admissibility block changes only through (P3), which now uses the proved rank-one inverse condition. The lattice block defines boxes, fibers, heights, saturation and regularity in terms of the actual basis, \(g\) and the numerical regularity constants, with no dependence on the old inequalities relating those constants. The trimming block and its lemma use the same covariance and radius and require the original exponential inequality of (I2). The sparse-count lemma uses volumes, minors, densities and the geometric constants in (I3); its hypotheses and proof do not use the old (I4), including in clause (b). The prefix claims (a) and (b) have unchanged arguments as explained above, and clause (c) and regularization have been justified under the new hypothesis. This accounts for the inverse-condition block and its listed dependencies. The extraction proof uses the amended inverse theorem only for a short multiple, while the norm, reduction target and budget keep their original meanings.

### Correctness with error \(2/q\)

Fix a witness \(w\). Conditional on the seed and the entries \(M_{i,j}\) with \(j\ge1\), the \(M_{i,0}\) are independent uniform ring elements because they are answers of the random oracle at distinct inputs. Since \(w_0=1\), adding \(\sum_{j\ge1}M_{i,j}\hat w_j\) to each \(M_{i,0}\) preserves this joint uniform distribution. In the monomial representation of the ring, its \(d_R\) coordinates are therefore independent uniform field elements, across all rows. Hence \(z(w)=1+\sum_{e=d_R}^{n-1}a_e T^e\) with \(kd_R\) independent uniform \(a_e\). The key weight is the degree-zero coefficient in the representative of \(z(w)\sigma^{n/2-1}(z(w))\). It is a polynomial of total degree at most 2 in the \(a_e\), as Frobenius substitutes the scalar multiple \(-T/\omega\) for \(T\) and taking the coefficient after reduction modulo \(T^n-c_0\) is a linear map. Its value at every \(a_e=0\) is 1, so the polynomial is nonzero.

A nonzero polynomial of total degree \(d_*\) in independent uniform field elements vanishes with probability at most \(d_*/q\). For completeness, in the final variable write it as a polynomial of degree \(j_*\) with nonzero leading coefficient of degree at most \(d_*-j_*\) in the earlier variables. By induction the leading coefficient vanishes with probability at most \((d_*-j_*)/q\). Where it is nonzero, at most \(j_*\) values of the final variable are roots. The one-variable bound follows by dividing out a linear factor for each root, and the constant case has no roots. This gives \(\Pr[\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0]\le2/q\).

If \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0\), the decryption algorithm returns \(\bot\). Otherwise the protected honest-combination lemma for this unchanged encoding yields \(\Lambda'=(r(w)-c_w)\Psi^\alpha\), and the unchanged range lemma gives \(|r(w)-c_w|\le B\). If \(\Psi^\alpha\ne0\), it has order \(q\), so \(2B+1<q\) gives a unique matching integer in \([-B,B]\), namely \(r(w)-c_w\). If \(\Psi^\alpha=0\), also \(\Psi=0\) because \(\alpha\ne0\), and the least absolute matching integer is zero. In both cases \(\Lambda-(c_w+t)\Psi=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa]_T\), hence after dividing by \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)\) the key is \([\kappa]_T\). The final XOR returns the original message. This proves precisely the failure event and the error bound of Theorem 5.6.

### Extraction and the reduction

Let \(\mathcal A\) have the budget \(Q<2^\lambda\) of generic group calls and at most \(2^\lambda\) queries to each oracle. Fix one bit \(b\) and messages \(m_0,m_1\), and run the extraction experiment with \(m_b\). The extractor uses the two-sector sketches in the protected extractor block. It samples three independent probe vectors for each cell and sector, uses the linear combinations of source-group records and pairings to form sketches, tests the sketches of the result and the difference when there is an old record, and directly checks any candidate assignment against \(S\). In rank at most three, there are at most four assignments with first coordinate 1 to test: row echelon pivot coordinates determine the vector, pivot 0 must be present, and at most two remaining pivot choices are bits. Output is therefore always \(\bot\) or a witness. A record creation, result, or difference touches \(O(K_1N+n)\) field elements, since a source-group record uses at most \(K_1\) length-\((N+1)\) vectors or \(9n\) values, and the pairings compute six such combined sketch vectors. For a pairing, forming the inner products in the G2 record or summing per left leg and combining the G1 vectors uses \(O(K_1N+n)\) operations. A test uses \(O(N)\) for elimination with at most three rows and \(O(N+|S|)\) to generate and check the constant number of candidates. Initialization samples \(9n(N+1)\) field values, enters the \(L\) labels as records whose vectors are materialized on first use, and uses \(O(nN+L)\) time. There are at most four tests per result. Thus the extractor uses \(O((Q+1)(nN+|S|)+L)\) time because \(K_1\le n\) and \(N\ge1\).

The reduction on input a uniform \(A\in R_q^{k\times\ell}\) samples the seed, programs the seed oracle at that seed on matrix columns 1 through \(\ell\) using \(A\), and samples the other answers lazily, including the independent column 0. It samples encryption coins as specified, produces the ciphertext for \(m_b\), and simulates all generic labels and names and random oracles by the protected lazy tables. It runs \(\mathcal A\) once and uses its oracle transcript without using its output. Because the programmed entries are jointly independent uniform ring elements for uniform \(A\), all oracle answers including entries at the selected seed have exactly the joint law of the experiment. Private evaluation points are sampled independently of the run.

The reduction keeps records evaluated at its private test point, using the protected rules for guessed operands and for existing records. It tries the first GT result whose label has an existing record of different private value, using the difference expression, and also the target label's record if present. For each tried expression it computes three independent gradient vectors in each sector at private recovery points, tests their span for a kernel vector, using the plain sector first, and applies the unchanged LIFT algorithm if there is such a vector. An output from LIFT is a short multiple of that kernel vector. The coefficient of \(T^0\) in \(z(v)\) is \(v_0\). All \(kd_R\) remaining row coefficients give the equations of \(A\) on the packed input once \(v_0=0\). Thus a nonzero kernel vector has \(v_0=0\) and satisfies \(A\hat v=0\). Its short multiple has \(x_0=0\) since \(2\beta<q\). Reduction and packing commute, and packing preserves the other \(N\) coordinates and adds only zeros. This packs the output into a nonzero norm-at-most-\(\beta\) solution to \(A\hat x=0\), exactly for the challenge whose rank, columns, bound, and norm are costed by M1. If no such output is found the reduction returns \(\bot\). Denote its success probability by \(s_b\). The cost is \(t+148Q+(d_R+5)Q_H+C_R+2T_{\rm LIFT}\) with \(Q_H\) the maximum per-oracle query count, \(C_R=O((N+n)D+\mu)\) for the fixed simulation setup and postprocessing, and \(T_{\rm LIFT}\) the time of the lifting step. This is the protected reduction-time proposition for the unchanged construction. The proof of the proposition uses group and field record operations, matrix dimensions, seed query size, and the number and runtime of LIFT attempts. Those are unaffected by the replacement of I4 and the comparison bound. LIFT's running-time result applies by \(2\beta<q\) and (P4). In particular the cost is \(t+O(Q+d_R Q_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\) using \(n,N\le D\).

Here is how the first-mark bounds apply to these algorithms. The protected degree, view, and record arguments apply to the original encoding. A good mark's stack is usable and has rank at most three. Indeed guessing probability above \(\theta\) and the rank bound give rank below four since \(g^{-4}<\mu_{\rm tr}<\theta\). A kernel vector in its row space has coordinate 0 equal to zero, and its one-dimensional guessing probability is at least the stack's, by the protected guessing-probability lemma. The amended inverse theorem gives a short multiple, so LIFT succeeds on it.

The first good difference mark is recognized by the private record difference except when the nonzero cleared expression vanishes at the independent private point. The degree bound and the uniform valid coin ranges give probability at most \(\varepsilon_f\) for this failure. For a first good key mark the target record already has its expression by the record invariant. For a recognized good mark, the gradient identity of the protected reduction lemma places all vectors in its sector row space and puts the plain vectors at zero in the alpha-mark case. For any proper subspace of the mark row space choose a linear form zero on the subspace and nonzero on the row space. Its value on one gradient, divided by \(\alpha_{\mathsf s}\pi\), is a polynomial of degree at most \(n+2\) which is nonzero by the distinct monomials \(s_X\upsilon^{k_c}y_i\). The valid values for the independent coin coordinates have sizes at least \(q-D\), by the original sampler and \(K_1\le n\le D\). The polynomial root bound, with \(q-D\) in place of \(q\) in its proof, bounds the chance of failing to increase the span while it is proper. Three independent points and rank at most three give failure at most \(3(n+2)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\), by \(n\le D\) and the formula for \(\varepsilon_f\). Thus on a good first mark with a kernel vector, except for the two exceptional events just bounded, the reduction finds a kernel vector in the appropriate span, and produces the MSIS output. The unconditional probability of such a mark is at most \(s_b+2\varepsilon_f\).

For a good first mark with no kernel vector, the unchanged classification lemma gives a witness in its row space. In that sector the linear map from a tuple of probes to \(\sum_c W_cv_c\) has image the row space, as each matrix is symmetric and the sum of its images is the row space of the stack. Uniform independent probe tuples give three independent uniform vectors in this space, even conditional on the run. In dimension \(a\le3\), the chance of failing to span is bounded by \(\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}q^{j-3}<2/q\): equivalently the \(a\) rows of the random \(a\)-by-3 coordinate matrix are independent uniform three-dimensional vectors and the next row falls in the span of \(j\) independent previous rows with probability \(q^{j-3}\). At a difference mark the extractor tests the record difference. At a key mark it tests the record of the result that obtained the target label. An initially received record has zero stack and thus is no good mark. These facts from the record invariant show that if the sampled vectors span, the witness is among the assignments tested. So the probability of a good first mark with no kernel vector and with extractor failure is at most \(2/q\).

Let \(u=2Q+2\). Every target query run has a first mark. The protected initial and guessed-mark estimates and the charged-mark estimate of Amendment Ncmp bound their three probabilities by
\[
\left(\binom{L_1}{2}+\binom{L_2}{2}+\binom{L_T}{2}\right)\varepsilon_f,\quad
N_{\rm cmp}(u)(\theta+\varepsilon_f)
+\left(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\right)\varepsilon_f,\quad
2^\lambda/N_{\rm free},
\]
respectively. These estimates concern the same sampler, oracle and record laws and use only the already checked view and degree properties, budget, and label counts. Together with the two good-mark cases they cover the event of a target key query and no witness. Their sum is at most \(s_b+E(u)\): the good-mark excess is \(2\varepsilon_f+2/q\le (8u+8)\varepsilon_f\), using \(\varepsilon_f\ge1/q\); the remaining terms of \(E\) are nonnegative and its guess term contains \(2^\lambda/N_{\rm free}\). By (P5) and the generic-ledger implication of Amendment Ncmp \(E(2Q+2)\le(Q+1)/2^\lambda\). Hence for each \(b\) the target-query and no-witness probability is at most \((Q+1)/2^\lambda+s_b\), with exactly the stated oracle budgets and one MSIS instance. This proves Theorem 5.9 with its extraction time and soundness as above.
