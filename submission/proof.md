## Amendment C7

The amended grid condition is \(K_1=1\) and \(K_2=1\). The construction uses exactly one left leg, one right leg and one cell, indexed by \((0,0)\). This cell uses \(\xi(w)=1-c_*U_0(w)^2\) with \(U_0\) computed from the original form polynomial and \(c_*\) a fixed public nonsquare in \(\mathbb F_q^*\). Such an element exists since \(q\) is odd and the nonzero squares occupy only half the group. If \(U_0(w)=0\) the weight is one. Otherwise \(c_*U_0(w)^2\) is a nonsquare, so cannot be one. Thus \(\xi(w)\ne0\) for every \(w\). The other original weights and extension-field identities can still be defined as algebraic objects from the matrix, but do not index cells of this construction. Define the revised cell tie on a stack containing \(W_{\rm cell}\) to be \(W_{\rm cell}=W_{\rm cell}^{\top}\). The subscript \(00\) below refers to a corner entry of a matrix. The honest stack \(\xi ww^\top\) satisfies this tie because \(ww^\top\) is symmetric. The revised view below proves that every identity produces a tied stack, together with the passing, search fiber and key corner statements.

For the single-cell ciphertext, the classification used in extraction is the following: every nonzero rank-one passing tied stack is a single scaled witness moment matrix. Its proof uses symmetry and the protected rank-one theorem, which concerns one passing matrix regardless of any grid or tie. Specifically that theorem gives \(W=a ww^\top\) with \(a=W_{00}\ne0\) (this time the corner of the matrix), \(w_0=1\), and \(w\) a witness. The absence of a kernel vector in its row space follows since \(z(w)\) has constant coefficient 1. As \(\xi(w)\ne0\), this is \(a/\xi(w)\) times the new honest stack of \(w\). For the extraction estimate the extractor checks the witness equations directly. The amended ledger pays for all identity stacks of larger rank, including those whose ordinary heavy-stack test succeeds.

For the original abstract classification about matrices with the three protected extension-field ties, its hypotheses are on the matrices, their coefficient polynomials in \(C_U,C_V\), and the full form map. It does not use the rectangular sharing of factors: the tensors are obtained from those coefficient polynomials using the consecutive conjugates of the forms, and witness recognition checks the linear tests and their values on the resulting tensor. The three polynomial ties and the original algebraic classification on those objects may still be stated on the separate \(C_U,C_V\) index sets of the protected weight definition. In this construction the one-cell tie, honest stack and rank-one extraction statement replace their use on the transmitted grid. ### Preserved abstract classification for the protected three ties

The protected classification is a separate algebraic assertion about the original form coefficient sets and original three ties. It does not use the exponent lists, the construction's value of \(K_1K_2\), or the designation of a cell as transmitted. Here is its proof with the objects identified explicitly. In this assertion put \(m\) for the original extension degree, \(F=\mathbb F_{q^m}\), and \(f=z\) for the original form vector regarded over \(F\). The original case uses the prescribed even extension degree. If the construction uses a different number of polynomial indices, the abstract assertion also holds with the same forms zero-padded to any supported even extension degree \(m\) at least their number. In particular the next power of two at least that number is at most \(2^{28}\) by (P1) and (C3), and divides \(q-1\) in this challenge. Padding preserves the kernel. This abstract proof imposes no restriction on the actual transmitted grid, and is not applied to a stack that only satisfies the construction's revised tie.

For the power-of-two choice \(m\), a primitive \(c_0\in\mathbb F_q^*\) gives an irreducible \(T^m-c_0\). To see this, if \(m=2^a\) and \(q-1=2^b s\) with \(s\) odd, a root \(\alpha_0\) of \(T^m-c_0\) has order \(d\) with \(d/\gcd(d,m)=q-1\). This forces odd part \(s\) and \(2\)-adic valuation \(a+b\), so \(d=m(q-1)\). The least \(e>0\) with \(d\mid q^e-1\) is \(m\). Indeed \(q-1\mid q^e-1\), and \(v_2(q^e-1)=b+v_2(e)\): an odd power preserves \(v_2(q-1)\) by the odd geometric sum, and each doubling adds one since \(q^j\equiv1\bmod4\). Thus \(\alpha_0\) has degree \(m\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). This proves existence in the optional padded setting. In either setting \(\sigma\) is the actual Frobenius on \(F\).

Let \(W_U,W_V\) be the matrix polynomials of the original \(U,V\) coefficient sets, reduced in \(F\), and write \(I=\{m/2-1,m/2,m/2+1\}\) with indices on Frobenius taken modulo \(m\). Put \(W_s=W_U,W_V,\sigma^{m/2+1}(W_U)\) in that order, and \(f_s=\sigma^s(f)\). The protected ties are exactly \(W_s f_t=W_t f_s\) for \(s,t\in I\). In addition \(W_s=\sigma^s(W_{m-s})\) for \(s=m/2-1,m/2\), since the \(V\)-coefficients use only even monomials. Suppose these coefficient matrices are symmetric, pass the Boolean tests, span a row space \(\mathcal V\) of dimension \(d\le3\), and \(\mathcal V\) has no kernel vector. If \(d=0\) the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise let \(P_*\) be a base-field column basis of \(\mathcal V\), with rows \(u_i^\top\), and put \(h_s=P_*^\top f_s\). The entries of \(h_0\) are independent over \(\mathbb F_q\) by the kernel hypothesis. Consecutive Frobenius twists of \(h_0\), in a list of length \(d\), are independent over \(F\): a dependence would give a nonzero polynomial \(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}b_j X^{q^j}\) vanishing on all \(d\) independent entries of \(\sigma^s(h_0)\). Its roots form an \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspace by the power maps, so there would be at least \(q^d\) roots despite degree at most \(q^{d-1}\). Hence the three \(h_s\) in \(I\) span \(F^d\).

By symmetry and the shared row space, there are unique symmetric matrices \(\Xi_s\) with \(W_s=P_*\Xi_s P_*^\top\), obtained by applying a left inverse of \(P_*\) on both sides. The ties imply \(\Xi_s h_t=\Xi_t h_s\). If \(\sum a_s h_s=0\), then \((\sum a_s\Xi_s)h_t=\Xi_t\sum a_s h_s=0\) for every \(t\), so \(\sum a_s\Xi_s=0\). There is therefore an \(F\)-linear map \(\Xi\) with \(\Xi(h_s)=\Xi_s\). Its tensor \(\mathcal T(h,x,x')=x^\top\Xi(h)x'\) is symmetric in the last two arguments by symmetry of \(\Xi\); on the spanning \(h_s\) the ties give symmetry of the first and third arguments, hence full symmetry. The Boolean tests imply \(\mathcal T(h,u_j,u_j-u_0)=0\) for \(j>0\), first at \(h_s\), then at all \(h\).

If \(u_0=0\), select a basis of \(d\) rows \(u_j\) with \(j>0\). Any basis triple with a repeated index has value zero by the Boolean tensor identities and symmetry. For \(d<3\) this makes the tensor zero. For \(d=3\) the only possibly nonzero component is the common value \(c\) of the three-distinct-index triples. In that basis \(\mathcal T(h_s,l_b,l_c)\) for distinct indices \(b,c\) is \(c\) times the coordinate of \(h_s\) on the remaining basis vector. The identities \(\Xi_s=\sigma^s(\Xi_{m-s})\) for \(s=m/2-1,m/2\) therefore give \(c h_s=\sigma^s(c)h_0\) in all three coordinates. If \(c\ne0\), two independent consecutive conjugates \(h_{m/2-1},h_{m/2}\) would both lie in the line of \(h_0\). Thus \(c=0\), and the stack is zero.

If \(u_0\ne0\), extend \(l_0=u_0\) by basis rows \(l_j=u_{i_j}\) for \(1\le j<d\). For each \(\delta\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\), choose the unique \(p_\delta\in\mathbb F_q^d\) with \(l_0^\top p_\delta=1\) and \(l_j^\top p_\delta=\delta_j\). The normalized potential assignments in \(\mathcal V\) are \(w_\delta=P_*p_\delta\); every Boolean assignment with coordinate zero one in this space is among them. By the Boolean tensor identities, two or three copies of \(l_j\) with \(j>0\) can be replaced by one copy of \(l_j\) and the required number of \(l_0\)'s in a triple, without changing its value. Components of \(\mathcal T\) therefore depend only on the subset of nonzero indices present. There are at most two such indices. The cubes \(p_\delta^{\otimes 3}\) have values \(\prod_{j\in A}\delta_j\) on the subsets \(A\). The square matrix of these values, with rows and columns indexed by subsets, has entries \(1_{A\subseteq B}\), and is triangular with ones on the diagonal when ordered by cardinality with any tie order. Consequently the cubes span these tensors. Their squares \(p_\delta p_\delta^\top\) are independent by the same matrix argument on pairs of basis rows, which can realize every subset when \(d-1\le2\). Thus
\[
 \Xi(h)=\sum_\delta c_\delta(h^\top p_\delta)p_\delta p_\delta^\top
\]
for \(c_\delta\in F\). Write \(f_\delta=h_0^\top p_\delta\ne0\). Comparing square coefficients in \(\Xi_s=\sigma^s(\Xi_{m-s})\) gives \(c_\delta\sigma^s(f_\delta)=\sigma^s(c_\delta)f_\delta\) for \(s=m/2-1,m/2\). Their exponents differ by one, so \(c_\delta/f_\delta\) is fixed by \(\sigma\), hence is \(\chi_\delta\in\mathbb F_q\). We obtain \(W_s=\sum_\delta\chi_\delta f_\delta\sigma^s(f_\delta)w_\delta w_\delta^\top\); this is the original honest-stack expansion coefficient by coefficient in \(U,V\).

Finally for any base-field linear test \(J_*\) vanishing on the coefficient matrices, its vanishing on each \(W_s\) says \(h_0^\top A_* h_s=0\), with
\[
 A_*=\sum_\delta \chi_\delta J_*(w_\delta w_\delta^\top)p_\delta p_\delta^\top
 \quad\text{over }\mathbb F_q.
\]
Since the \(h_s\) span, \(h_0^\top A_*=0\). The \(\mathbb F_q\)-independence of the entries of \(h_0\) gives \(A_*=0\); independence of the squares then gives \(\chi_\delta J_*(w_\delta w_\delta^\top)=0\) for every \(\delta\). Applying this to all Booleanity tests makes the supported \(w_\delta\) Boolean, and applying it to every constraint test makes them witnesses when the stack passes every constraint. The same test calculation applies to any such expansion because all its normalized Boolean points lie among the same \(w_\delta\). This proves both the protected dimension-three classification and its support conclusion for the actual objects in its statement. All operations in this proof are on the abstract coefficient matrices and the form map; there is no use of the amended factor grid or operation count.


The abstract sketch implication used with that original classification also has the same full scope. For symmetric matrices with row space \(\mathcal V\), the map \((v_c)_c\mapsto\sum_c W_cv_c\) is onto \(\mathcal V\), since each matrix's image is its row space and their sum is the stack row space. Three independent uniform inputs give three independent uniform vectors in \(\mathcal V\). If \(d=\dim\mathcal V\le3\), the chance they fail to span is at most \(\sum_{j<d}q^{j-3}\le2/q\) by the row-rank test on their \(d\)-by-3 coordinate matrix. The full classification just proved supplies a witness in the span in the no-kernel case. The sketch test in Targets enumerates and checks it from any such span. This shows independence of the original abstract sketch conclusion from the revised publication arrays.

### Full asymptotic cost specialization

Here is the analogue of the original cost corollary with its family specified. For this cost statement take linear-size systems with \(N\ge\lambda^2\), \(\log q=\Theta(\lambda)\), \(\mu,\ell_{\rm seed}=O(\lambda)\), \(d_R=\Theta(\lambda)\), \(K_1,K_2=O(\lambda)\), \(n=O(\lambda^2)\) with \(n\le D\), and \(J=J'=1,\Delta=D\). Use the original evaluation-point placement in \(\mathcal H\) and the same group arithmetic costs in field operations. Constant leg counts, as in a single cell, meet these conditions. This is a cost family, independently of feasibility of all security conditions at every \(\lambda\).

As \(\nu\) can be the least power of two above \(F_{\max}\), the linear-size constraint bound gives \(D=N+2+\nu n_{\mathsf c}=O(N)\), and \(\log D=O(\lambda)\) by \(D<q\). The statement files listing nonzero terms have length \(O(\lambda N)\). With the displayed element formulas, every factor array contributes \(O((K_1+K_2+1)(N+1))=O(\lambda N)\), and chunk and cancellation arrays contribute \(O(D+n)=O(N)\) for these chunk counts. The GT element, seed and mask contribute \(O(1)\) field elements as \(\log q=\Theta(\lambda)\). Thus ciphertext size is \(O(\lambda N)\) field elements.

All QAP procedures, including evaluation of the original interpolation input at \(\tau\) during encryption and the witness quotient/remainder computation in decryption, use the protected QAP algorithm with the same evaluation data. They do not inspect cell weights, group structures or operation counts. The \(O(D\log D+|S|)\) field bound for those unmodified procedures therefore applies, as does the original setup bound \(O(|S|+D\log D+\log q)\). Form work uses \(k(\ell+1)\) ring samples or products, with \(k\le n/d_R=O(\lambda)\) and \((\ell+1)d_R=O(N+d_R)=O(N)\). A product in \(R_q\) uses \(O(d_R\log(2d_R))\) field operations. Evaluating at the odd powers of a primitive \(2d_R\)-th root reduces to a coefficient scaling and a radix-two transform with \(O(\log(2d_R))\) layers of \(O(d_R)\) butterflies. Pointwise multiplication and the inverse transform implement the product. When encryption needs the entire linear coefficient vector of a masked form at a specified evaluation point, for each ring entry apply the transpose of this fixed-entry multiplication map to the vector of its \(d_R\) monomial values. Transposing the linear scaling, butterfly and diagonal operations reverses their order and keeps the same operation count. This yields the same \(O(k(\ell+1)d_R\log(2d_R))=O(\lambda N\log\lambda)\) upper bound. Computing the constant coefficient of the original weight product, or the polynomial cross terms of linear weights, fits \(O(n\log(2n))\): use radix-two multiplication, splitting each input into at most two pieces of length at most \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) if needed so the individual product transforms fit \(\mathcal H\). Reduction modulo the original binomial, when used, folds degrees at least \(n\) into lower coefficients in one pass. Array scalars and powers cost \(O(n_1+n_2+D+n)\). These field terms sum to \(O(\lambda N\log\lambda)\).

For encryption's fixed-base operations on \(O(\lambda N)\) exponents, use radix \(2^w\) with \(w=\lfloor\log_2\lambda\rfloor\) for \(\lambda\ge2\). In each group and each digit position tabulate the multiples of that position's shifted generator by successive additions, including the cost of shifting the generator. There are \(O(\lambda/\log\lambda)\) positions and at most \(2^w=O(\lambda)\) entries in each. Thus precomputation costs \(O(\lambda^2/\log\lambda)\) group operations, and every output exponent uses \(O(\lambda/\log\lambda)\) additions of selected entries. This gives \(O(\lambda^2N/\log\lambda)\) field operations for encryption, including the preceding field work.

For decryption outside search, the proved addition bounds are \(O(\lambda N)\), the total MSM lengths are \(O(n+D)=O(N)\), and there are \(O(K_2+1)=O(\lambda)\) pairings. Even computing each MSM term separately costs \(O(\lambda)\) group operations per point by binary scalar multiplication, for \(O(\lambda N)\) field operations. Pairings cost \(O(\lambda)\) field operations apiece, hence \(O(\lambda^2)\) altogether, dominated by \(O(\lambda N)\). The fixed number of final GT scalings costs \(O(\lambda)\), so with the field work decryption outside search costs \(O(\lambda N\log\lambda)\).

To count the search, take baby-table width \(b_*=\lceil\sqrt g\,N^{1/4}\rceil\). There are \(O(b_*)\) baby additions, and the outward giant steps need \(O(1+|t|/b_*)\) additions if the answer is \(t\), by covering successive intervals of width \(2b_*-1\). The unchanged centered sampler gives \(\mathbb E|t|\le(\mathbb E t^2)^{1/2}=O(g\sqrt N)\). Hence the expected field operation bound is \(O(\sqrt g\,N^{1/4})\). With the original cost-family choice \(g=\Theta(2^{\lambda/2})\) this is precisely \(O(2^{\lambda/4}N^{1/4})\). For a family using any larger range the displayed bound in terms of \(g\) applies instead; in particular it is \(O(2^{\lambda/3}N^{1/4})\) at \(g=O(2^{2\lambda/3})\) and \(O(2^{\lambda/2}N^{1/4})\) at \(g=O(2^\lambda)\). No security inference from the illustrative smaller cost range is needed.

Indeed the coefficients of \(E(u)\) are nonnegative and it is quadratic, by expansion of each summand and \(L\ge1,N_{\rm free}>0\). Thus \(Q\mapsto E(2Q+2)-(Q+1)/2^\lambda\) is convex. \[
 N_{\rm cmp}(U)-N_{\rm cmp}(2)
   = U^2/2+(U-2)L+(U-6)/2>U^2/2.
\]
The strict inequality follows from \(U\ge4,L\ge5\). Therefore the second endpoint test gives \(\theta<2^{-(2\lambda+1)}\), as in the original ledger. We have \(L_1\ge n_1+1\), \(L_2\ge2n_2+1\) from the definitions, and the construction has more than five received labels in the counting convention. Thus \(L\ge5\).

for \(u\ge0\). The extra guard gives \(g^{-2}\le4\theta\). In particular

This is why the actual rank-sensitive probabilities below fit the original ledger. ### Degree, single-cell view, and gradients

Fix the matrix \(M\). Source expressions are linear combinations of the input lists of Amendment n1 and the source generators. A target expression is a linear combination of their pairwise products and the separate \(D_K\alpha\kappa\) term. Separate each expression by its degree in the coin \(\alpha\). Degree zero is the plain sector and degree one is the alpha sector, with respective factors \(a_{\rm plain}=1\), \(a_\alpha=\alpha\). Degree two is also possible from multiplying two scaled leaves. The only G2 leaves with an alpha factor are search, quotient and remainder bases, which do not depend on \(r\), so degree two never depends on \(r\). In sector \(\mathsf s\), \(W\) denotes the coefficients pairing \(F_{i,1}\) of that sector with shared \(H_{j,1}\). Define \(u_i\) as the negative sum of all coefficients pairing a first left coordinate indexed by \(i\) with a search base whose total alpha degree is that sector. At degree one, one alpha factor can be on either input, and both products have exponent \(\alpha\gamma y_i\). This is the sole cell in each sector. We show that an identity \(D=\chi\kappa\) has symmetric passing matrices, \(Wr=u\) in each of the two sectors and \(W_{00}=\chi\) in the plain sector, followed by the locus, coincidence and formal key properties. Symmetry is precisely the revised cell tie of Amendment C7. The derivative and matrices have no contribution from degree two.

Use the original clearing factor \(\varpi=\Omega_{22}\zeta\zeta'\zeta''\varepsilon\), which is nonzero on valid coins. G1 has no denominators and has degree at most \(d_1=\max\{D+3,\Delta\}\): factors use at most three degrees for their scalar \(a_{\mathsf s}\pi_*\) and at most \(D\) further degrees for the hidden-basis row, while the largest other degree is at most \(\max(\Delta,\Delta')\) with \(\Delta'\le D\). The cleared G2 degree, including the alpha sector, is at most \(d_2=D+8+(J-1)\Delta\). Indeed, a quotient base has degree at most \(D+3+4+1+(J-1)\Delta\), respectively from \(Z_*,\gamma,\varpi/\zeta,\alpha,\tau^{(J-1)\Delta}\). A remainder base has degree at most \(N+2+3+4+1+(J'-1)\Delta'\le D+8\). Each cleared right factor has degree at most \(D+5\): at most \(D-1\) from the QAP polynomial or one from a vector entry, one from \(s\) and five from clearing (the ratio \(\Omega_{12}/\Omega_{22}\) has net degree zero). The search base has cleared degree at most seven. Generators and the two cleared key exponents have degrees at most \(D+8\) as well. Since \(J\Delta\le D+n\), either \(\Delta=d_1\) and \(d_1+d_2\le2D+n+8\), or \(D+3=d_1\) and \(d_1+d_2\le3D+n+10\) using \((J-1)\Delta\le D+n-1\). Both bounds are at most \(d_f=3D+2n+2K_1+6\), using \(n\ge2\), \(K_1\ge1\), \(D\ge n\). The source expressions separately have degree at most \(\max\{d_1+5,d_2\}\le d_f\) after clearing. These are the protected degree upper bounds, with the removed types absent.

All scalar coordinates of the coins are independent and have supports of size at least \(q-D\) by the original sampling sets. For \(\varepsilon\) use \(1+K_1\le D\), and for \(\tau\) use \(|\mathcal P|=D\). The first endpoint of Amendment P5 gives \(24\varepsilon_f\le E(2)\le2^{-\lambda}\) and \(d_f<q-D\). For fixed \(r\), the product-set root bound in Targets then implies that any identity on the valid sampling sets has zero cleared polynomial, and any nonidentity has coincidence probability at most \(d_f/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\). Thus coefficient comparisons for an identity can be made as Laurent polynomial comparisons with the scalar coins as indeterminates.

The two shared right exponents are
\[
 s(r_j+A_j(\tau))-\Omega_{12}s(B_j(\tau)-y_j)/\Omega_{22},
 \qquad s(B_j(\tau)-y_j)/\Omega_{22}.
\]
Write \(\tilde a\) for the exponent, with any factor of \(\alpha\) removed, of a G1 input at \(\Omega_{22}=0\). For \(F_{i,1}\) this is \(\pi_*y_i\), and for \(F_{i,2}\) it is \(\Omega_{12}\pi_*y_i\). Suppose \(D=\chi\kappa\) identically at \(r\). Isolate the terms at \(\Omega_{22}^{-1}\) with positive degree in \(\Omega_{12}\), since the key exponents do not involve the basis. Only the shared right factors have negative degree in \(\Omega_{22}\). From their first coordinates these terms contain \(-\Omega_{12}\tilde a\) for every \(a\). From the second right coordinates only \(F_{i,2}\) contributes, with \(\Omega_{12}\tilde F_{i,1}\). Divide by the common Laurent monomial \(-\Omega_{12}/\Omega_{22}\). For each \(j_0\) differentiate the result in \(y_{j_0}\) and substitute \(y=B(\tau)\). This kills the terms from differentiating \(\tilde a\), and the remaining identity, up to a minus sign, is
\[
 \sum_{\mathsf s,a}\delta_{\mathsf s,a,j_0}\,
        a_{\mathsf s}s\,\tilde a\big|_{y=B(\tau)}=0
\]
where \(\delta_{\mathsf s,a,j_0}\) is the coefficient of \(a,H_{j_0,1}\) in that sector, less that of the corresponding \(F_{i,2},H_{j_0,2}\) when \(a\) is of type \(F_{i,1}\). The types \(1,\zeta\tau^v,\zeta'\tau^v,\pi_* B_i(\tau),\Omega_{12}\pi_* B_i(\tau)\) have disjoint multidegrees in \(\zeta,\zeta',\pi,\Omega_{12}\); \(\alpha\) separates the sectors. Within each power type the monomials are distinct. Within each factor type, divide by the nonzero polynomial common to it and use \(B(d_j)=e_j\) to see that the \(B_i\) are linearly independent. Consequently every \(\delta\) is zero. The paired coefficients of the two matching factor coordinates agree, and only first left coordinates can pair with first right coordinates with nonzero coefficient.

Next isolate the Laurent degree class of the key, with degree one in \(\pi\), zero in \(\zeta,\zeta'\) and joint basis degree zero in \((\Omega_{12},\Omega_{22})\). The possible pairings in this class are first left coordinates with first right coordinates, either search base, or the G2 generator, second left coordinates with second right coordinates, and matching quotient and remainder powers with their bases. This list follows by matching the complementary degrees of the G1 and G2 types. The G1 generator has degree zero, and no G2 input alone has the key class. Separate degree zero and one in \(\alpha\); the higher coefficients of a Laurent identity must vanish independently. Move \(D_K\alpha\kappa\) to the right and write \(\chi_{\rm plain}=\chi\) and \(\chi_\alpha=-D_K\). In either degree the generator terms are independent of \(s\). The matched factor coefficients cancel the hidden basis. Taking degree one in \(s\) and dividing out the common \(a_{\mathsf s}\gamma\) yields
\[
 y^\top(Wr-u)+y^\top(W-W^\top)A(\tau)
       + P_W(\tau)+Z_*(\tau)h_*(\tau)+Z_0(\tau)R_*(\tau)
       =\chi_{\mathsf s}L_\rho(\tau).
\]
Here the quotient coefficients assemble a polynomial \(h_*\) from the quotient powers; the remainder powers give an allowed polynomial \(R_*\) because the shifts \(j\Delta'\) preserve the allowed residues modulo \(\nu\). The linear coefficient in \(y\) at \(d_0\) gives \(Wr=u\), as \(A(d_0)=0\). At \(\rho,d_1,\ldots,d_N\), the \(A\)-values \(e_0,e_j-e_0\) span the space. Hence \(W-W^\top=0\), which proves the stated tie in each sector. The constant term at \(\rho\) gives \(W_{00}=\chi_{\mathsf s}\). The remaining constant identity gives precisely the protected QAP criterion with \(-h_*,-R_*\), so \(W\) passes.

Only the shared first right coordinates depend on \(r\), so this dependence lies in degrees zero and one in \(\alpha\). The vanishing result shows that the value difference from replacing \(r\) by \(r'\) is \(\sum_{\mathsf s}a_{\mathsf s}\gamma y^\top W^{\mathsf s}(r'-r)\). If both matrices satisfy their equations at \(r'\), the identity persists. If the matrices vanish, the identity is formal and \(\chi=0\) by its corner. An identity formal for any \(\chi\) requires \(Wr=u\) for every field vector, so the matrices vanish and \(\chi=0\). This proves the locus and no-formal-key claims. Pairing a source-expression difference with the opposite generator yields a target expression with zero matrices. An identity at any \(r\) would hold formally by the locus just proved and imply formal equality of the source expressions. Therefore formally distinct source expressions have coincidence probability at most \(\varepsilon_f\) at every \(r\) by the degree estimate. Likewise formally equal target expressions have the same matrices, because the difference is a formal identity at \(\chi=0\).

For a fixed identity, the coefficient vanishing persists at all private recovery points, since it is a statement about its fixed coefficients. Its sector gradient in \(r\) there is \(a_{\mathsf s}\gamma (W^{\mathsf s})^\top y\). This is in the sector row space and is zero when that matrix is zero. For a rank-one mark matrix it is uniform in that row space at a private point by uniform \(y\) independent of the nonzero \(a_{\mathsf s}\gamma\). For the extractor the sector sketch is \(W^{\mathsf s}v\), which also ranges uniformly over the row space by symmetry and uniform independent \(v\). Thus the three independent probes or points span a rank-one mark space except with probability \(q^{-3}\).

### Symbolic alternatives and their probabilities

Use the random-mask coupling of the protected symbolic-game block, so the transcript up to a first target query without its answer has the same law as in the real message experiment, with the same label and cipher coin samples. Fix the matrix and seed and sample the symbolic game with formal equality before evaluating the coins, because the symbolic answers and random mask do not depend on the valid field coins or \(r\). The generic label inverses on used strings in all three groups can be sampled lazily. When a fresh guessed operand is a label, its field exponent in the real game is uniform among those not already assigned in its group. Use the symbolic guess variable's independent uniform evaluation if it is unused, and otherwise redraw from the unused exponents. With \(m\) used exponents, each unused value has probability \(1/q+(m/q)/(q-m)=1/(q-m)\). Label membership tests use the same sampled label images, including the valid guessed operands of rejected calls specified by (M2). For a fresh result whose real exponent is new, use the same fresh label in both games. Before the first collision of distinct formal entries these choices agree, and hence the adversary makes the same calls and oracle queries, because the answers were the same up to that point. A collision occurs upon the creation of the later formal entry, even if a real redraw on a guessed operand leaves no mark at that time. Thus the first symbolic collision can be used to bound the actual target-query failure event. If it is earlier than the target query, the games agree through its call up to the collision. If the target query occurs earlier, the query has the same transcript prefix as in the symbolic evaluation.

Define a *recoverable* event to be a collision or key-value event involving a guess-free target-group expression \(D\) that is identically \(\chi\kappa\) at the sampled \(r\) and whose mark stack has rank one. Here \(D\) is the formal difference of the two entries with \(\chi=0\) for a collision, and the target entry itself with \(\chi=1\) for a key value. The mark stack is the plain stack if nonzero, and the alpha stack otherwise, as in the protected monitor. Call all other collisions or key-value events *charged for this calculation*. This terminology applies to the present partition of events.

Fix the symbolic game, a collision pair of formally distinct entries, or a candidate single target-group key-value entry. In a guess-free comparison in a source group, the chance of equal values is at most \(\varepsilon_f\) by the source-group estimate just proved. For a comparison or key-value expression with a guess variable, there is a formally nonzero coefficient of some guess monomial. It is either a nonzero field constant or a formally nonzero expression in a source group: products of guesses can arise only from pairings of source expressions, and direct guesses in GT are linear. For fixed \(r\) in the latter case the coefficient cannot vanish on all valid encryption coins by the source-group estimate and \(\varepsilon_f<1\). Thus the cleared event equation is a nonzero polynomial after fixing \(r\). The terms without a guess have degree at most \(d_f\) by the degree lemma. A guess multiplying a source expression adds one degree to the cleared source expression, while a product of two guesses has degree at most seven after clearing the five factors of \(\varpi\). The total degree is at most \(d_f+1\). The independent evaluated guess variables are uniform in the field, and every valid coin coordinate has support size at least \(q-D\). The independent-coordinate polynomial root bound gives probability at most \((d_f+1)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\). Its elementary conditional-variable proof is given in Targets.

Consider now a guess-free comparison or key-value expression \(D\) in the target group. At any \(r\) where the identity in the coins fails, equality occurs with probability at most \(\varepsilon_f\) by the view lemma. If it holds, the view lemma gives passing symmetric stacks and the fiber equations \(W_c r=u_c\) for every cell. At least one stack is nonzero: for a collision, zero stacks would make \(D\) formally zero, contradicting distinctness of the entries, and for the key value their zero key corner would contradict \(\chi=1\). The stack used for the mark is fixed by \(D\). For the nonrecoverable identity case it has rank at least two, since rank one is recoverable. Its fibers under the centered sampler have probability at most \(g^{-2}\) by the protected stack rank lemma. All \(r\) for which the identity holds obey the same fixed fiber equations, so the total probability of an event charged for this calculation in the guess-free target-group case is at most \(g^{-2}+\varepsilon_f\).

We count the events that use this last bound. At call \(j\) a guess-free difference must have a result as the later entry, since a newly guessed operand introduces a new variable absent from the earlier entry. In GT the two received entries and at most \(j-1\) earlier results are eligible. The remaining earlier entries in that group were inserted as pure guessed variables. A fixed result can have guess-free difference with at most one of them. If it had two, subtracting the two differences would make the difference of two distinct guess variables guess-free. This holds also for the operands newly guessed at call \(j\). Thus there are at most \(j+2\) candidate pairs at call \(j\). A guess-free key-value entry is one of at most \(Q+2\) received entries and results. The total for this bound is therefore at most
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^Q(j+2)+Q+2
     =\frac{Q(Q+1)}2+3Q+2
     = C_*(2Q+2)-1 .
\]
The remaining events, each bounded by \(\varepsilon_f\), number at most the number of pairs plus the number of entries in the at-most-\(L+3Q\) lists, hence at most \(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\) for \(u=2Q+2\). These counts apply conditional on the symbolic game, and their union bound is its contribution to \(E(u)\).

### Recovery from the rank-one alternatives

For any recoverable expression the chosen stack is nonzero, passing and of rank one. One of its matrices is nonzero, hence of rank one since its rows lie in the stack row space. By the protected rank-one passing-matrix lemma, it is \(a ww^\top\) for \(a\ne0\) and a witness \(w\) with \(w_0=1\). In particular the row space of the mark stack is the line through \(w\). On a recoverable collision, the later entry is a result and the real game at that call assigns its label to the earlier equal value. In the real monitor, the first mark is the difference with expression \(D\). It has that expression even though the threshold classification of the mark is unchanged. At a recoverable key query, the earlier record of the entry is the expression \(D\). It cannot be an initially received GT element, whose stack is zero. In the collision case the extractor tests the difference of the two records when processing the result. If the earlier entry is a pure guess, the guess-free difference implies that the result contains that same variable with coefficient one. The label was therefore used in an earlier answered call or is an operand of this call. Its zero record exists by the time the difference is tested. In the known-key case the extractor tested the result record when that entry was first created. Records of used operands without a record are zero, which agrees with setting the guess variables to zero. Setting guesses to zero commutes with both the linear operations and pairing, and before the first mark, any reuse of a label preserves formal equality. Formal equality implies equal matrices by the view proved above. Induction over the calls therefore gives the sketches of \(D\) among the tested records in each case. This argument requires the rank-one identity and the absence of guesses, and holds independently of classifying the mark as heavy.

For each of the three probes in the mark sector, the linear map \((v_c)_c\mapsto\sum_c W_cv_c\) has image equal to the row space of the stack. This follows because the matrices are symmetric and the sum of their column spaces is then the sum of their row spaces. Independent uniform probes give independent uniform vectors on that one-dimensional image. Hence the probability that they all vanish is \(q^{-3}\), which is at most \(2/q\). Otherwise they span the row space and the test of that span finds a witness because the normalized \(w\) is among the enumerated candidates. The extractor's sampling is independent of the adversary's transcript and the events used to select the earliest collision or target query. Thus the conditional bound holds given that earliest event whenever it is recoverable. If the extractor had found a witness earlier, it already succeeded. This estimate is paid only once per run.

Now the first possibilities covering a target query and no witness are an initial coincidence, a first collision charged for this calculation, a target query for a label with no entry before a collision, a target query with a known entry charged for this calculation before a collision, and a recoverable alternative with extractor failure. The initial allowance, comparisons, guess and recovery terms of the protected \(E\) pay for these events. Precisely, the initial probability is bounded by \(\sum_s\binom{L_s}{2}\varepsilon_f\), the collision and known-entry alternatives by the two union bounds above using \(C_*(u)(g^{-2}+\varepsilon_f)\le N_{\rm cmp}(u)(\theta+\varepsilon_f)\), the unknown-target case by \(2^\lambda/N_{\rm free}\), and the recoverable failure by \(2/q\). A first symbolic collision that would require a redraw on a guessed operand is already among the comparison cases containing a guess variable, even if the real monitor has no mark at that call. The fourth term \((8u+8)\varepsilon_f\) dominates \(2/q\) since \(\varepsilon_f\ge1/q\), and the fifth term dominates the guessing probability. The remaining terms are nonnegative. The probability of target query and extractor failure is consequently at most the amended \(E(2Q+2)\). The extraction proof in Targets uses this estimate with the same explicit reduction and adversary model.

### Correctness with error \(2/q\)

For every witness \(w\) the amended key weight is nonzero by Amendment C7. The following product-set polynomial estimate is also used in the expression analysis above.

A nonzero polynomial of total degree \(d_*\) in independent variables sampled uniformly from respective field subsets of size at least \(m\) vanishes with probability at most \(d_*/m\). In the final variable write it as a polynomial of degree \(j_*\) with nonzero leading coefficient of degree at most \(d_*-j_*\) in the earlier variables. By induction the leading coefficient vanishes with probability at most \((d_*-j_*)/m\). Where it is nonzero, at most \(j_*\) values of the final variable are roots, giving conditional probability at most \(j_*/m\). The one-variable bound follows by dividing out a linear factor for each root, and a nonzero constant has no roots.

If \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0\), the decryption algorithm returns \(\bot\). Otherwise the honest combination proved in Amendment n1 yields \(\Lambda'=(r(w)-c_w)\Psi^\alpha\), and the unchanged range lemma gives \(|r(w)-c_w|\le B\). If \(\Psi^\alpha\ne0\), it has order \(q\), so \(2B+1<q\) gives a unique matching integer in \([-B,B]\), namely \(r(w)-c_w\). If \(\Psi^\alpha=0\), also \(\Psi=0\) because \(\alpha\ne0\), and the least absolute matching integer is zero. In both cases \(\Lambda-(c_w+t)\Psi=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa]_T\), hence after dividing by \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)\) the key is \([\kappa]_T\). The final XOR returns the original message. The failure event is empty on witness inputs, so the correctness error is zero, in particular at most \(2/q\).

### Extraction and the reduction

Let \(\mathcal A\) have the budget \(Q<2^\lambda\) of generic group calls and at most \(2^\lambda\) queries to each oracle. Fix one bit \(b\) and messages \(m_0,m_1\), and run the extraction experiment with \(m_b\). The extractor makes no oracle calls and observes only the answered generic transcript. It uses three independent uniform probes \(v\) per sector in the only transmitted cell, or equivalently the protected sketch algorithm with all other arrays zero. G1 records hold the two coefficient vectors on first left coordinates, one plain and one alpha-scaled. G2 records hold the six inner products of the shared first right-coordinate coefficients against the three probes for each of the two sectors. GT records contain six length-\((N+1)\) sketch vectors. Initialize a source basis record as a single unit coordinate or six precomputed probe entries where appropriate, otherwise zero. Linear operations use the corresponding linear operations on records. For a pairing, if a sector's G1 coefficient vector is \(a\) and the shared G2 coefficient vector is \(b\), its matrix is \(ab^\top\) and each sketch is \(a\langle b,v\rangle\). Only terms using the shared right factors have such a matrix, so exponent degree two in \(\alpha\) does not enter the sketch. On each GT result, including a name negative, test the result in each sector and also its difference against the old label record if one exists. Keep only the first record for a label and give a previously unrecorded operand record zero. The tests compute row echelon form of three sketches, discard a span with no pivot at 0, and try the vector with pivot 0 set to 1 and every choice of bits at the other at most two pivots. There are at most four candidates. Check every witness condition of \(S\) before outputting one, so every output is a witness or \(\bot\).

The extractor uses \(O(N+L)\) initialization for the probes and sparse initial records. Each operation on records, including a pairing, uses at most a constant number of vectors of length \(N+1\). Each test costs \(O(N+|S|)\) by elimination with three rows and evaluation of the at most four candidates. There are at most four tests per result. Thus the extractor uses \(O((Q+1)(N+|S|)+L)\) time, at most the required \(O((Q+1)(nN+|S|)+L)\) bound.

The reduction on input a uniform \(A\in R_q^{k\times\ell}\) samples the seed, programs the seed oracle at that seed on matrix columns 1 through \(\ell\) using \(A\), and samples the other answers lazily, including the independent column 0. It samples encryption coins as specified, produces the ciphertext for \(m_b\), and simulates all generic labels and names and random oracles by the protected lazy tables. It runs \(\mathcal A\) once and uses its oracle transcript without using its output. Because the programmed entries are jointly independent uniform ring elements for uniform \(A\), all oracle answers including entries at the selected seed have exactly the joint law of the experiment. Private evaluation points are sampled independently of the run. The private monitor, gradients and LIFT steps use no random oracle query transcript. The lazy oracle tables implement exactly the law of the adversary querying the oracles directly.

The reduction keeps records evaluated at its private test point, using the protected rules for guessed operands and for existing records. It tries the first GT result whose label has an existing record of different private value, using the difference expression, and also the target label's record if present. For each tried expression it computes three independent gradient vectors in each sector at private recovery points, tests their span for a kernel vector, using the plain sector first, and applies the unchanged LIFT algorithm if there is such a vector. An output from LIFT is a short multiple of that kernel vector. The coefficient of \(T^0\) in \(z(v)\) is \(v_0\). All \(kd_R\) remaining row coefficients give the equations of \(A\) on the packed input once \(v_0=0\). Thus a nonzero kernel vector has \(v_0=0\) and satisfies \(A\hat v=0\). Its short multiple has \(x_0=0\) since \(2\beta<q\). Reduction and packing commute, and packing preserves the other \(N\) coordinates and adds only zeros. This packs the output into a nonzero norm-at-most-\(\beta\) solution to \(A\hat x=0\), exactly for the challenge whose rank, columns, bound, and norm are costed by M1. For the asymptotic reduction time, scalar evaluation of a retained operation at a private point takes a bounded number of operations: linear group operations are field linear combinations, pairing is one product, and a name negative is negation. For each selected target expression, store the arithmetic circuit of its record with one node per answered operation and leaves for input exponents and zeroed guesses. Set all other leaves to zero, since they cannot contribute to the selected derivative. In particular, retain the G1 generator and unscaled powers in degree zero, even on expressions that are not identities. This preserves the selected gradient since every target product uses exactly one G1 leaf and one G2 leaf after expansion, and only a shared first right-coordinate leaf depends on \(r\).
