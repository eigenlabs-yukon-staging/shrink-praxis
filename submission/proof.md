# Key-cell Praxis with shared right factors

## Amendment I6

The amended condition is
\[
 \beta\ge1,\qquad 2\beta<q,\qquad \theta g\ge1,\qquad \theta<1 .
\]
This condition restricts the inverse theorem to a regime in which its short-vector premise is empty. Let \(v\ne0\) have \(v_0=0\), as in the statement of the inverse theorem. There is an \(i\in[1,N]\) with \(v_i\ne0\). Conditional on every search coordinate other than \(r_i\) among coordinates 1 through \(N\), the equation \(v^\top r=t\) is an equation for \(r_i\) with nonzero coefficient. Because \(v_0=0\), the dependence of the centered \(r_0\) on the other coordinates disappears. The \(g\) possible values of \(r_i\) are distinct modulo \(q\) by (C4), giving conditional probability at most \(1/g\) and hence the same bound for every fiber. Thus \(\operatorname{gp}(v^\top)\le1/g\le\theta\) and no such vector meets the strict premise \(\operatorname{gp}(v^\top)>\theta\). This establishes the inverse statement under the replacement of (I6). The unchanged positivity and \(2\beta<q\) conditions are sufficient, together with (P4), for the lifting lemma, which remains usable when evaluating the explicit reduction.

Here are the dependencies in the inverse-conditions block. Only (P3) in the admissibility block depends on (I6), by requiring the inverse-theorem conditions. The lattice and trimming blocks define their objects using the box, basis, \(\Gamma_d\), and trimming radius and do not use a norm limit on the final multiple. The prefix lemma proves its height and density relations by successive integer-group indices and residue-class counts, and uses (I1), (I2), (I4) for its step inequality and rank-three regularity. Trimming uses (I2) to bound the loss after applying the uniform-box exponential estimate. The sparse-count lemma uses the ellipsoid volumes and (I3). Regularization uses the prefix, trimming and sparse-count statements and the growth and rank inequalities of (I4) and (I5). Neither its induction nor its conclusion of small density times height uses the final norm requirement. Detailed proofs of the prefix, trimming, sparse-count and regularization statements under the retained conditions are included below. The inverse theorem is the result whose norm step changes, and it was proved above. In the heavy-stack lemma, part (a) follows as before from \(g^{-4}<\mu_{\rm tr}<\theta\) and the guessing-probability rank bound. In fact every nonzero stack has guessing probability at most \(1/g\), so the case of a kernel vector of a heavy stack is now empty. Part (c) keeps its original proof since the forms-to-module packing and the definition of a short multiple are unchanged. The new extraction argument uses passing rank-one matrices directly, as given next and in Targets.

### Prefix and truncation estimates without a norm condition

The following gives the dependencies of the prefix and trimming statements explicitly. Let \(\Theta\) be a saturated lattice of rank \(d\), with integral basis matrix \(B\) and a largest minor on \(J\). Order its coordinates with \(J\) first, let \(I_k\) be the first \(k\) coordinates, and let \(\Theta_k=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}(B_{I_k})\cap\mathbb Z^{I_k}\), \(k\ge d\). Let \(h_k,m_k\) be its height and largest fiber density. Its rank is \(d\) because the selected minor remains invertible. If \(Y\) is a basis of \(\Theta_k\), then \(B_{I_k}=Y C\) for an integral nonsingular \(d\)-square matrix \(C\). The fibers of the prefix are thus the level sets of \(B_{I_k}^{\top}r\). The group \(G_k=B_{I_k}^{\top}\mathbb Z^{I_k}=C^\top\mathbb Z^d\), by saturation and the integer-lattice lemma (b), has index \(|\det C|\) in \(\mathbb Z^d\). The \(d\)-row determinants of \(B_{I_k}\) are multiplied from those of \(Y\) by \(\det C\). As their largest absolute value is the original \(h(\Theta)\), this gives \(h_k [\mathbb Z^d:G_k]=h(\Theta)\) and shows that \(J\) is still a largest minor for \(\Theta_k\). At \(k=d\), the column span is all of \(\mathbb R^J\), so \(\Theta_d=\mathbb Z^J\), \(h_d=1\) and \(m_d=g^{-d}\). At the full set of coordinates the saturated lattice is \(\Theta\).

On adding the next row to \(B_{I_k}\), let \(s_k\) be the order of that row as a column in the quotient \(\mathbb Z^d/G_k\). Adjoining it multiplies the size of the subgroup modulo \(G_k\) by \(s_k\), so \(h_{k+1}=s_k h_k\) by the index identity. For a fixed value of \(B_{I_{k+1}}^\top r\), the new coordinate is in at most one residue class modulo \(s_k\); its allowed values in an interval of \(g\) integers number at most \(\lceil g/s_k\rceil\). For each such value the remaining prefix has fiber density at most \(m_k\). Consequently \(m_{k+1}\le m_k\lceil g/s_k\rceil/g\). If \(s_k\ge g\) this also makes the new coordinate constant on the fiber. If \(s_k=1\), the power inequality \(m_{k+1}^b h_{k+1}^{b-1}\le m_k^b h_k^{b-1}\) is immediate. For \(2\le s=s_k<g\), use \(\lceil g/s\rceil/g\le 1/s+1/g\le s^{-1+1/b}\). To verify the second inequality, if \(s\ge2^b\) then \(1/s+1/g<2/s\le s^{-1+1/b}\). Otherwise \(g\ge2^b\) and the concave function \(x^{1/b}-1-x/2^b\) is nonnegative on \([2,2^b]\): it is zero at \(2^b\), and at \(2\) it is at least \((\log 2)/b-2^{1-b}>0\) for \(b\ge7\). The last strict inequality follows from \(\log 2>1/2\) and \(2^{b-2}>b\) for integers \(b\ge7\). This proves the power inequality in all its cases.

For the rank-three conclusion of the prefix lemma, let the nontrivial step orders be \(s_1,\ldots,s_a\ge2\); their product is \(h=h(\Theta)\). The density satisfies \(m(\Theta)\le g^{-3}\prod_j(1/s_j+1/g)\). The product expands as \(1/h\) plus terms whose sum is at most \((1/2+1/g)^a-(1/2)^a\) since \(1/s_j\le1/2\). The difference is zero if \(a=0\), and otherwise is at most \(a(1/2+1/g)^{a-1}/g\le(1+2/g)/g\). The latter sequence has maximum at \(a=2\), since the ratio \((1+1/a)(1/2+1/g)<1\) for \(a\ge2\) and \(g\ge128\). Therefore \(m(\Theta)\le g^{-3}\xi_{\rm tr}(1/h+1/g)\). If \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}\), solving gives \(h\le H_3\), and \(m(\Theta)h\le g^{-3}\xi_{\rm tr}(1+H_3/g)\le g^{-3}\Gamma_3\) by the retained (I2) and (I4). This proves the rank-three regularity statement. Every assumption used here is still in (I1), (I2), or (I4).

For the trimming lemma, a centered uniform box entry can be added to a uniform independent variable on \([-1/2,1/2]\) to get the continuous uniform variable on \([-g/2,g/2]\). The added variable's moment generating function is at least one by convexity, so the centered box entry has moment generating function bounded by \(\sinh(x)/x\) with \(x=tg/2\) at argument \(t\). Its series is bounded by \(e^{x^2/6}\) because \((2j+1)!\ge6^j j!\) for \(j\ge0\), by induction on \(j\). Let \(Y=B^\top r-\mathbb E[B^\top r]\) and \(\Sigma=(g^2/12)B^\top B\). Independence of the box entries gives \(\mathbb E e^{u^\top Y}\le e^{u^\top\Sigma u/2}\). With \(Z\) an independent standard \(d\)-dimensional Gaussian, the Gaussian moment generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E e^{(99/200)\|\Sigma^{-1/2}Y\|_2^2}
 =\mathbb E e^{\sqrt{99/100}\,Z^\top\Sigma^{-1/2}Y}
 \le \mathbb E e^{(99/200)\|Z\|_2^2}=10^d .
\]
The inequality conditions on \(Z\); the last equality follows by scaling each normal density in the integral. By Markov's inequality the probability of being outside the trimming ellipsoid is at most \(10^d e^{-99R_{\rm tr}^2/200}\le\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\) by (I2) and \(d\le3\). Trimming any subset removes at most that absolute density. This establishes the trimming conclusion without a norm assumption.

### Sparse-count estimate with the retained geometric constants

The claim of `lem:sparse` is as follows. Let \(B\) be a basis of an integral lattice \(\Theta\) on a coordinate set \(I\) with \(|I|\le N\), of rank \(d\in[3]\). Let \(F\ne\varnothing\) be a set of points of the integer box on \(I\cup\{i'\}\), \(i'\notin I\), trimmed by \(\Theta\), and \(\Phi(r)=(B^\top r_I,r_{i'})\). If (a) \(\Phi(F)\) has affine dimension \(d+1\) and lies in a coset of an index-\(q\) subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\), then
\(\Pr[F]\le\Lambda_d g^d h(\Theta)m(\Theta)/q\).
If (b) \(F^\perp\) has rank \(d'\) with \(1\le d'<d\), a largest minor on \(J\subseteq I\), and every vector of \(F^\perp\) restricts to a vector in \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Theta\) on \(I\), then
\(\Pr[F]h(F^\perp)\le\Lambda_{d-d'}\,g^{d-d'}h(\Theta)m(\Theta)\).
Here \(F^\perp\) is the lattice of integer vectors normal to the differences in \(F\).

For each possible image \((y,x)\), the probability of \(B^\top r_I=y,r_{i'}=x\) is at most \(m(\Theta)/g\) by independence, so \(\Pr[F]\le|\Phi(F)|m(\Theta)/g\). We count images in \(C=\mathcal{T}_\Theta\times[2,g+1]\).

For (a), put \(s=d+1\), \(\delta=q\), \(J=\varnothing\), and let \(p\) be the identity. Write \(B=(B_1,B_2)\) with \(B_1\) empty, and use \(|\det(B_1)_J|=1\).
For (b), put \(s=d+1-d'\) and let \(Y\) be an integral basis matrix of \(F^\perp\); \(Y_J\) is invertible. The submatrix \(B_J\) has row rank \(d'\) since the columns \(Y_J\) lie in its real column span. The integral kernel of \(B_J\) is saturated in \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), of rank \(d-d'\). It has a basis that extends to an integral basis of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\): the quotient by a saturated subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\) is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence free, and lifts of a quotient basis give the extension. Using this basis change, write \(B=(B_1,B_2)\) where \((B_2)_J=0\) and \((B_1)_J\) is invertible square. The columns of \(B_2\) number \(s-1\). Trimming is basis independent since the mean and covariance transform by the same invertible change on the image coordinates.

In case (b), the injective map \(a=(a_{[d]},a_{d+1})\mapsto(Ba_{[d]},a_{d+1})\) maps the real annihilator of the differences of \(\Phi(F)\) isomorphically onto the real annihilator of the differences of \(F\), by the restriction assumption. The first annihilator has dimension \(d'\) and its integer points form a saturated lattice with basis matrix \(V\). Split its rows into \(V_1,V_2,V_3\) of lengths \(d',s-1,1\). The image of its basis is \(Y A\) for an integral nonsingular \(d'\)-square matrix \(A\), as its columns are integral independent normals. At rows \(J\), this gives
\[
(B_1)_J V_1=Y_J A .
\]
Consequently \(V_1\) is invertible and \(|\det(B_1)_J|\,\delta\ge h(F^\perp)\), where \(\delta=|\det V_1|\). Write \(y=(y_1,y_2)\) according to the basis split. Projection \(p:(y,x)\mapsto(y_2,x)\) is injective on any affine translate of the kernel of \(V^\top\), because there the first block of a difference is determined by \(V_1^\top\). It therefore preserves the affine dimension of \(\Phi(F)\), which is \(s\). The projections of differences of image points lie in the kernel of
\[
 (y_2,x)\longmapsto V_2^\top y_2 + x V_3^\top
          \pmod{V_1^\top\mathbb{Z}^{d'}} .
\]
The map is onto because \(V^\top \mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\mathbb{Z}^{d'}\) by saturation and `lem:lattice-facts` (b), while the \(y_1\) contribution vanishes in the quotient. The kernel has index \(\delta\) in \(\mathbb{Z}^s\). Thus in both cases \(p(\Phi(F))\) has full affine dimension \(s\), cardinality \(|\Phi(F)|\), and lies in \(p(C)\) and in a coset of an index-\(\delta\) lattice.

A full-dimensional finite set \(X\) of \(m\) points in such a coset has \(\operatorname{vol}_s\operatorname{conv}(X)\ge(m-s)\delta/s!\). To see this, start from any \(s+1\) affinely independent points forming a simplex. One can add the points successively while maintaining a triangulation of their hull using all inserted points as vertices. For a point outside the hull, each boundary facet simplex strictly visible to the point gives a new simplex by coning it to the point. Those simplices have disjoint interiors and fill the new region, as rays from the point first hit the old hull in its visible boundary. At least one facet is strictly visible, so at least one full simplex is added. If a point is in the hull, take the unique smallest face of the triangulation containing it. This face has positive dimension since the point is distinct from all earlier vertices. Subdivide that face by coning its boundary faces to the point and make the corresponding subdivision of every full simplex incident to it, by adjoining the complementary simplex vertices. This covers exactly those full simplices and replaces each by at least two, since the point lies in the relative interior of the face. Hence there are at least \(m-s\) full simplices. Each has volume at least \(\delta/s!\), since the determinant of \(s\) independent differences of coset points is a nonzero integer multiple of the determinant \(\delta\) of its lattice. Since \(m\ge s+1\), the region containing \(X\), if convex, has volume at least \(\delta/s!\). In particular, applying both consequences to \(p(C)\) gives
\[
 |\Phi(F)|\le s!\operatorname{vol}_s p(C)/\delta+s
             \le (s+1)!\operatorname{vol}_s p(C)/\delta .
\]

The projection of the ellipsoid \(\mathcal{T}_\Theta\) to the last \(s-1\) coordinates has volume \(v_{s-1}R_{\rm tr}^{s-1}(g/\sqrt{12})^{s-1}\sqrt{\det(B_2^\top B_2)}\), where \(v_j\) is the volume of the Euclidean unit \(j\)-ball. Indeed, write the ellipsoid as the image of a unit ball by \(R_{\rm tr}\Sigma_\Theta^{1/2}\) plus the center. The projected map has a Gram matrix on its range equal to the bottom-right block \(R_{\rm tr}^2(g^2/12)B_2^\top B_2\), which gives the product of its nonzero singular values. The interval in \(p(C)\) has length \(g-1\). In either case \((B_2)_J=0\). By expansion of \(\det(B_2^\top B_2)\) into squared row minors, and because \(|\det(B_1)_J\det(B_2)_{J'}|=|\det B_{J\cup J'}|\le h(\Theta)\) for each \(J'\subseteq I\setminus J\) of size \(s-1\),
\[
 |\det(B_1)_J| \sqrt{\det(B_2^\top B_2)}
 \le h(\Theta)\sqrt{\binom{N}{s-1}} .
\]
Combining with the counting bound and \(\Pr[F]\le |\Phi(F)|m(\Theta)/g\) gives
\[
 \Pr[F]|\det(B_1)_J|\delta
 \le g^{j}h(\Theta)m(\Theta)
      (j+2)!v_j R_{\rm tr}^{j}\sqrt{\binom{N}{j}/12^j},
 \qquad j=s-1 .
\]
Use \(\binom{N}{j}\le N^j/j!\) and \(v_1=2,v_2=\pi,v_3=4\pi/3\). The three upper bounds on the geometric factor are \(2\sqrt3 R_{\rm tr}\sqrt N\), \(\sqrt2\pi R_{\rm tr}^2N\), and \(20\pi R_{\rm tr}^3N^{3/2}/(9\sqrt2)\). They are at most the three allowed \(\Lambda_j\). In case (a), divide by \(q\) to obtain the claim. In (b), replace \(|\det(B_1)_J|\delta\) by \(h(F^\perp)\) on the left to obtain the claim.


### Regularization under the retained growth conditions

The claim of `lem:regularize` is that a saturated lattice \(\Theta\) of rank \(d\in[3]\) with \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}+(3-d)\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\) is contained in a regular saturated lattice of rank in \([d,3]\) and maximum fiber density at least \(m(\Theta)-(3-d)\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\).

Write \(\mu=\mu_{\rm tr}\) and \(e=\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\) in this paragraph and the next two. Induct downwards on the initial rank. For \(d=3\), `lem:prefix` (c) gives regularity directly. For \(d\le2\), use `lem:prefix` with a largest minor at \(J\) and \(J\) first in the coordinate order. Denote the prefixes by \(\Theta_k\), their heights and max densities by \(h_k,m_k\), and the step order by \(s_k\). The densities are nonincreasing, so each is at least \(\mu+(3-d)e\). Take a densest fiber of \(\Theta_k\) to form \(F_k\). If the preceding \(F_{k-1}^\perp\) strictly contains \(\Theta_{k-1}\), trim this fiber by the regular containing lattice \(\widetilde\Theta_{k-1}\) specified below. Otherwise leave the fiber whole. The density of each \(F_k\) is at least \(m_k-e\) by `lem:trim`. Its normal lattice contains \(\Theta_k\). If this inclusion is strict, saturation gives a rank \(d_k>d\). Its rank is at most three since a fiber of a rank-\(a\) lattice has density at most \(g^{-a}\) by fixing coordinates outside an invertible \(a\)-row minor, and \(g^{-4}<\mu\le m_k-(3-d)e\) by (I5). Its max fiber density is at least \(\Pr[F_k]\ge\mu+(2-d)e\ge\mu+(3-d_k)e\). By induction let \(\widetilde\Theta_k\) be a regular saturated containing lattice of rank at least \(d_k\), with \(m(\widetilde\Theta_k)\ge m(F_k^\perp)-(3-d_k)e\ge m_k-(3-d)e\). This constructs the trimming lattice for the next step, and if \(F_{|I|}^\perp\ne\Theta\) it gives the desired final lattice.

If \(F_{|I|}^\perp=\Theta\), let \(j<|I|\) be the last strict index, or \(j=d-1\) when there is none. For \(j<k<|I|\) the step order \(s_k\) is below \(g\). Otherwise `lem:prefix` (b) would make the new coordinate constant in the fiber of \(\Theta_{k+1}\) containing \(F_{k+1}\); its unit vector would then be in \(F_{k+1}^\perp=\Theta_{k+1}\) and vanish at \(J\), forbidden by `lem:lattice-facts` (a). Iterating the power inequality in `lem:prefix` (b) gives
\[
 \mu(m(\Theta)h(\Theta))^{b-1}
 \le m(\Theta)^b h(\Theta)^{b-1}
 \le m_{j+1}^{b}h_{j+1}^{b-1}.
\]
When \(j=d-1\), \(m_d=g^{-d}, h_d=1\). The bound on \(\Gamma_d^{b-1}\) in (I4) gives \(m_d^{b}h_d^{b-1}\le\mu(\Gamma_d/g^d)^{b-1}\).

When \(j\ge d\), let \(\tilde d\) be the rank of \(\widetilde\Theta_j\). The set \(F_{j+1}\) was trimmed by this lattice. Its normal lattice is exactly \(\Theta_{j+1}\), of rank \(d<\tilde d\), and with largest minor on \(J\) by `lem:prefix` (a). Restrictions to the first \(j\) coordinates lie in the span of \(\Theta_j\) by the definition of the prefix lattice, hence in the span of \(\widetilde\Theta_j\). Apply the proved projected count (b). Since \(\Pr[F_{j+1}]\ge m_{j+1}-e\ge m_{j+1}/A_{\rm inv}\) by (I4) and \(m_{j+1}\ge\mu\), regularity gives
\[
 m_{j+1}h_{j+1}\le A_{\rm inv}\Lambda_{\tilde d-d}\Gamma_{\tilde d}/g^d .
\]
Also \(m_{j+1}\le m_j\le\Pr[F_j]+e\le g^{-d-1}+e\le\mu P_d^{b-1}\) by the rank of \(F_j^\perp\) and (I4). For \(d=1\), (I4) allows \(2e\) in the numerator and therefore implies the same inequality. In all three rank pairs \((d,\tilde d)\in\{(1,2),(1,3),(2,3)\}\), the last growth inequalities of (I4) imply \(A_{\rm inv}\Lambda_{\tilde d-d}\Gamma_{\tilde d}P_d\le\Gamma_d\). Multiplying yields \(m_{j+1}^b h_{j+1}^{b-1}\le\mu(\Gamma_d/g^d)^{b-1}\). Now \(b>1\) and \(\mu>0\) give \(m(\Theta)h(\Theta)\le\Gamma_d/g^d\). This proves regularity and finishes the induction.

\[
 \xi\gamma\big(y(w)(r(w)+A_w(\tau))+A_w(\tau)(B_w(\tau)-y(w))\big)

### Degree, single-cell view, and gradients

Fix the matrix \(M\). Source expressions are linear combinations of the input lists of Amendment n1 and the source generators. A target expression is a linear combination of their pairwise products and the separate \(D_K\alpha\kappa\) term. Separate each expression by its degree in the coin \(\alpha\). Degree zero is the plain sector and degree one is the alpha sector, with respective factors \(a_{\rm plain}=1\), \(a_\alpha=\alpha\). Degree two is also possible from multiplying two scaled leaves. The only G2 leaves with an alpha factor are search, quotient and remainder bases, which do not depend on \(r\), so degree two never depends on \(r\). In sector \(\mathsf s\), \(W\) denotes the coefficients pairing \(F_{i,1}\) of that sector with shared \(H_{j,1}\). Define \(u_i\) as the negative sum of all coefficients pairing a first left coordinate indexed by \(i\) with a search base whose total alpha degree is that sector. We show that an identity \(D=\chi\kappa\) has symmetric passing matrices, \(Wr=u\) in each of the two sectors and \(W_{00}=\chi\) in the plain sector, followed by the locus, coincidence and formal key properties. The derivative and matrices have no contribution from degree two.

Use the original clearing factor \(\varpi=\Omega_{22}\zeta\zeta'\zeta''\varepsilon\), which is nonzero on valid coins. For \(F_{i,1}\) this is \(\pi_*y_i\), and for \(F_{i,2}\) it is \(\Omega_{12}\pi_*y_i\). Suppose \(D=\chi\kappa\) identically at \(r\). Isolate the terms at \(\Omega_{22}^{-1}\) with positive degree in \(\Omega_{12}\), since the key exponents do not involve the basis. ### Symbolic alternatives and their probabilities

Use the random-mask coupling of the protected symbolic-game block, so the transcript up to a first target query without its answer has the same law as in the real message experiment, with the same label and cipher coin samples. Fix the matrix and seed and sample the symbolic game with formal equality before evaluating the coins, because the symbolic answers and random mask do not depend on the valid field coins or \(r\). The generic label inverses on used strings in all three groups can be sampled lazily. When a fresh guessed operand is a label, its field exponent in the real game is uniform among those not already assigned in its group. Use the symbolic guess variable's independent uniform evaluation if it is unused, and otherwise redraw from the unused exponents. With \(m\) used exponents, each unused value has probability \(1/q+(m/q)/(q-m)=1/(q-m)\). Label membership tests use the same sampled label images, including the valid guessed operands of rejected calls specified by (M2). For a fresh result whose real exponent is new, use the same fresh label in both games. Before the first collision of distinct formal entries these choices agree, and hence the adversary makes the same calls and oracle queries, because the answers were the same up to that point. A collision occurs upon the creation of the later formal entry, even if a real redraw on a guessed operand leaves no mark at that time. Thus the first symbolic collision can be used to bound the actual target-query failure event. If it is earlier than the target query, the games agree through its call up to the collision. If the target query occurs earlier, the query has the same transcript prefix as in the symbolic evaluation.

Define a *recoverable* event to be a collision or key-value event involving a guess-free target-group expression \(D\) that is identically \(\chi\kappa\) at the sampled \(r\) and whose mark stack has rank one. Here \(D\) is the formal difference of the two entries with \(\chi=0\) for a collision, and the target entry itself with \(\chi=1\) for a key value. The mark stack is the plain stack if nonzero, and the alpha stack otherwise, as in the protected monitor. Call all other collisions or key-value events *charged for this calculation*. This terminology applies to the present partition of events.

Fix the symbolic game, a collision pair of formally distinct entries, or a candidate single target-group key-value entry. In a guess-free comparison in a source group, the chance of equal values is at most \(\varepsilon_f\) by the source-group estimate just proved. For a comparison or key-value expression with a guess variable, there is a formally nonzero coefficient of some guess monomial. It is either a nonzero field constant or a formally nonzero expression in a source group: products of guesses can arise only from pairings of source expressions, and direct guesses in GT are linear. For fixed \(r\) in the latter case the coefficient cannot vanish on all valid encryption coins by the source-group estimate and \(\varepsilon_f<1\). Thus the cleared event equation is a nonzero polynomial after fixing \(r\). The terms without a guess have degree at most \(d_f\) by the degree lemma. A guess multiplying a source expression adds one degree to the cleared source expression, while a product of two guesses has degree at most seven after clearing the five factors of \(\varpi\). The total degree is at most \(d_f+1\). The independent evaluated guess variables are uniform in the field, and every valid coin coordinate has support size at least \(q-D\). The independent-coordinate polynomial root bound gives probability at most \((d_f+1)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\). Its elementary conditional-variable proof is given in Targets.

Consider now a guess-free comparison or key-value expression \(D\) in the target group. At any \(r\) where the identity in the coins fails, equality occurs with probability at most \(\varepsilon_f\) by the view lemma. If it holds, the view lemma gives passing symmetric stacks and the fiber equations \(W_c r=u_c\) for every cell. At least one stack is nonzero: for a collision, zero stacks would make \(D\) formally zero, contradicting distinctness of the entries, and for the key value their zero key corner would contradict \(\chi=1\). The stack used for the mark is fixed by \(D\). For the nonrecoverable identity case it has rank at least two, since rank one is recoverable. Its fibers under the centered sampler have probability at most \(g^{-2}\) by the protected stack rank lemma. All \(r\) for which the identity holds obey the same fixed fiber equations, so the total probability of an event charged for this calculation in the guess-free target-group case is at most \(g^{-2}+\varepsilon_f\).

We count the events that use this last bound. At call \(j\) a guess-free difference must have a result as the later entry, since a newly guessed operand introduces a new variable absent from the earlier entry. In GT the two received entries and at most \(j-1\) earlier results are eligible. The remaining earlier entries in that group were inserted as pure guessed variables. A fixed result can have guess-free difference with at most one of them. If it had two, subtracting the two differences would make the difference of two distinct guess variables guess-free. This holds also for the operands newly guessed at call \(j\). Thus there are at most \(j+2\) candidate pairs at call \(j\). A guess-free key-value entry is one of at most \(Q+2\) received entries and results. The total for this bound is therefore at most
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^Q(j+2)+Q+2
     =\frac{Q(Q+1)}2+3Q+2
     = C_*(2Q+2)-1 .
\]
The remaining events, each bounded by \(\varepsilon_f\), number at most the number of pairs plus the number of entries in the at-most-\(L+3Q\) lists, hence at most \(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\) for \(u=2Q+2\). ### Recovery from the rank-one alternatives

For any recoverable expression the chosen stack is nonzero, passing and of rank one. One of its matrices is nonzero, hence of rank one since its rows lie in the stack row space. By the protected rank-one passing-matrix lemma, it is \(a ww^\top\) for \(a\ne0\) and a witness \(w\) with \(w_0=1\). In particular the row space of the mark stack is the line through \(w\). On a recoverable collision, the later entry is a result and the real game at that call assigns its label to the earlier equal value. In the real monitor, the first mark is the difference with expression \(D\). It has that expression even though the threshold classification of the mark is unchanged. At a recoverable key query, the earlier record of the entry is the expression \(D\). It cannot be an initially received GT element, whose stack is zero. In the collision case the extractor tests the difference of the two records when processing the result. If the earlier entry is a pure guess, the guess-free difference implies that the result contains that same variable with coefficient one. The label was therefore used in an earlier answered call or is an operand of this call. Its zero record exists by the time the difference is tested. In the known-key case the extractor tested the result record when that entry was first created. Records of used operands without a record are zero, which agrees with setting the guess variables to zero. Setting guesses to zero commutes with both the linear operations and pairing, and before the first mark, any reuse of a label preserves formal equality. Formal equality implies equal matrices by the view proved above. Induction over the calls therefore gives the sketches of \(D\) among the tested records in each case. This argument requires the rank-one identity and the absence of guesses, and holds independently of classifying the mark as heavy.

For each of the three probes in the mark sector, the linear map \((v_c)_c\mapsto\sum_c W_cv_c\) has image equal to the row space of the stack. This follows because the matrices are symmetric and the sum of their column spaces is then the sum of their row spaces. Independent uniform probes give independent uniform vectors on that one-dimensional image. Hence the probability that they all vanish is \(q^{-3}\), which is at most \(2/q\). Otherwise they span the row space and the test of that span finds a witness because the normalized \(w\) is among the enumerated candidates. The extractor's sampling is independent of the adversary's transcript and the events used to select the earliest collision or target query. Thus the conditional bound holds given that earliest event whenever it is recoverable. If the extractor had found a witness earlier, it already succeeded. This estimate is paid only once per run.

Now the first possibilities covering a target query and no witness are an initial coincidence, a first collision charged for this calculation, a target query for a label with no entry before a collision, a target query with a known entry charged for this calculation before a collision, and a recoverable alternative with extractor failure. The first two lines and the guess and recovery terms of \(E\) pay for these events. Precisely, the initial probability is bounded by \(\sum_s\binom{L_s}{2}\varepsilon_f\), the collision and known-entry alternatives by the two union bounds above, the unknown-target case by \(2^\lambda/N_{\rm free}\), and the recoverable failure by \(2/q\). A first symbolic collision that would require a redraw on a guessed operand is already among the comparison cases containing a guess variable, even if the real monitor has no mark at that call. The fourth term \((8u+8)\varepsilon_f\) dominates \(2/q\) since \(\varepsilon_f\ge1/q\), and the fifth term dominates the guessing probability. The remaining terms are nonnegative. The probability of target query and extractor failure is consequently at most the amended \(E(2Q+2)\). ### Correctness with error \(2/q\)

Fix a witness \(w\). Conditional on the seed and the entries \(M_{i,j}\) with \(j\ge1\), the \(M_{i,0}\) are independent uniform ring elements because they are answers of the random oracle at distinct inputs. Since \(w_0=1\), adding \(\sum_{j\ge1}M_{i,j}\hat w_j\) to each \(M_{i,0}\) preserves this joint uniform distribution. In the monomial representation of the ring, its \(d_R\) coordinates are therefore independent uniform field elements, across all rows. Hence \(z(w)=1+\sum_{e=d_R}^{n-1}a_e T^e\) with \(kd_R\) independent uniform \(a_e\). The key weight is the degree-zero coefficient in the representative of \(z(w)\sigma^{n/2-1}(z(w))\). It is a polynomial of total degree at most 2 in the \(a_e\), as Frobenius substitutes the scalar multiple \(-T/\omega\) for \(T\) and taking the coefficient after reduction modulo \(T^n-c_0\) is a linear map. Its value at every \(a_e=0\) is 1, so the polynomial is nonzero.

A nonzero polynomial of total degree \(d_*\) in independent variables sampled uniformly from respective field subsets of size at least \(m\) vanishes with probability at most \(d_*/m\). In the final variable write it as a polynomial of degree \(j_*\) with nonzero leading coefficient of degree at most \(d_*-j_*\) in the earlier variables. By induction the leading coefficient vanishes with probability at most \((d_*-j_*)/m\). Where it is nonzero, at most \(j_*\) values of the final variable are roots, giving conditional probability at most \(j_*/m\). The one-variable bound follows by dividing out a linear factor for each root, and a nonzero constant has no roots. Taking \(m=q\) gives \(\Pr[\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0]\le2/q\).

If \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0\), the decryption algorithm returns \(\bot\). Otherwise the honest combination proved in Amendment n1 yields \(\Lambda'=(r(w)-c_w)\Psi^\alpha\), and the unchanged range lemma gives \(|r(w)-c_w|\le B\). If \(\Psi^\alpha\ne0\), it has order \(q\), so \(2B+1<q\) gives a unique matching integer in \([-B,B]\), namely \(r(w)-c_w\). If \(\Psi^\alpha=0\), also \(\Psi=0\) because \(\alpha\ne0\), and the least absolute matching integer is zero. In both cases \(\Lambda-(c_w+t)\Psi=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa]_T\), hence after dividing by \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)\) the key is \([\kappa]_T\). The final XOR returns the original message. This proves precisely the failure event and the error bound of Theorem 5.6.

### Extraction and the reduction

Let \(\mathcal A\) have the budget \(Q<2^\lambda\) of generic group calls and at most \(2^\lambda\) queries to each oracle. Fix one bit \(b\) and messages \(m_0,m_1\), and run the extraction experiment with \(m_b\). The extractor makes no oracle calls and observes only the answered generic transcript. It uses three independent uniform probes \(v\) per sector in the only transmitted cell, or equivalently the protected sketch algorithm with all other arrays zero. G1 records hold the two coefficient vectors on first left coordinates, one plain and one alpha-scaled. G2 records hold the six inner products of the shared first right-coordinate coefficients against the three probes for each of the two sectors. GT records contain six length-\((N+1)\) sketch vectors. Initialize a source basis record as a single unit coordinate or six precomputed probe entries where appropriate, otherwise zero. Linear operations use the corresponding linear operations on records. For a pairing, if a sector's G1 coefficient vector is \(a\) and the shared G2 coefficient vector is \(b\), its matrix is \(ab^\top\) and each sketch is \(a\langle b,v\rangle\). Only terms using the shared right factors have such a matrix, so exponent degree two in \(\alpha\) does not enter the sketch. On each GT result, including a name negative, test the result in each sector and also its difference against the old label record if one exists. Keep only the first record for a label and give a previously unrecorded operand record zero. The tests compute row echelon form of three sketches, discard a span with no pivot at 0, and try the vector with pivot 0 set to 1 and every choice of bits at the other at most two pivots. There are at most four candidates. Check every witness condition of \(S\) before outputting one, so every output is a witness or \(\bot\).

The extractor uses \(O(N+L)\) initialization for the probes and sparse initial records. Each operation on records, including a pairing, uses at most a constant number of vectors of length \(N+1\). Each test costs \(O(N+|S|)\) by elimination with three rows and evaluation of the at most four candidates. There are at most four tests per result. Thus the extractor uses \(O((Q+1)(N+|S|)+L)\) time, at most the required \(O((Q+1)(nN+|S|)+L)\) bound.

The reduction on input a uniform \(A\in R_q^{k\times\ell}\) samples the seed, programs the seed oracle at that seed on matrix columns 1 through \(\ell\) using \(A\), and samples the other answers lazily, including the independent column 0. It samples encryption coins as specified, produces the ciphertext for \(m_b\), and simulates all generic labels and names and random oracles by the protected lazy tables. It runs \(\mathcal A\) once and uses its oracle transcript without using its output. Because the programmed entries are jointly independent uniform ring elements for uniform \(A\), all oracle answers including entries at the selected seed have exactly the joint law of the experiment. Private evaluation points are sampled independently of the run. The private monitor, gradients and LIFT steps use no random oracle query transcript. The lazy oracle tables implement exactly the law of the adversary querying the oracles directly.

The reduction keeps records evaluated at its private test point, using the protected rules for guessed operands and for existing records. It tries the first GT result whose label has an existing record of different private value, using the difference expression, and also the target label's record if present. For each tried expression it computes three independent gradient vectors in each sector at private recovery points, tests their span for a kernel vector, using the plain sector first, and applies the unchanged LIFT algorithm if there is such a vector. An output from LIFT is a short multiple of that kernel vector. The coefficient of \(T^0\) in \(z(v)\) is \(v_0\). All \(kd_R\) remaining row coefficients give the equations of \(A\) on the packed input once \(v_0=0\). Thus a nonzero kernel vector has \(v_0=0\) and satisfies \(A\hat v=0\). Its short multiple has \(x_0=0\) since \(2\beta<q\). Reduction and packing commute, and packing preserves the other \(N\) coordinates and adds only zeros. This packs the output into a nonzero norm-at-most-\(\beta\) solution to \(A\hat x=0\), exactly for the challenge whose rank, columns, bound, and norm are costed by M1. If no such output is found the reduction returns \(\bot\). The protected reduction-time proposition's \(148Q\) term is retained by the corresponding scalar record implementation. For a generic call, use 14 operations for the label and record lookups, run exponent, private test value, stored operation and record test of R3. There are six recovery passes, each with at most 19 operations per call: three for the forward value and up to eight for each of two selected expressions in the reverse gradient, four through the call and four for derivatives of received operands. The reverse update of a leaf uses its fixed derivative and coordinate, so sharing the right leaves leaves this count unchanged. Two unseen operands cost at most eight operations each, including deciding membership and sampling exponent, plus two zero records. Handling a name costs at most two more operations. These give \(14+6\cdot19+2\cdot8+2+2=148\). The fixed initialization and postprocessing term \(C_R\) remains \(O((N+n)D+\mu)\): evaluate the \(O(N)\) degree-\(<D\) QAP factor polynomials at constantly many points, iterate the needed powers through lengths bounded by \(D+n\), compute the original forms and scalar data within \(O(nD)\), and enter the \(O(N+D)\) transmitted exponent records. The tests of the constant number of width-three kernel systems use \(O(nN)\) operations apart from the two counted LIFT calls. Codes and QAP advice fit \(O((N+n)D+\mu)\) in the protected model. For the asymptotic reduction time, scalar evaluation of a retained operation at a private point takes a bounded number of operations: linear group operations are field linear combinations, pairing is one product, and a name negative is negation. For each selected target expression, store the arithmetic circuit of its record with one node per answered operation and leaves for input exponents and zeroed guesses. At each recovery point retain the G1 leaves of the chosen alpha degree and retain the shared first right-coordinate G2 leaves. Set all other leaves to zero, since they cannot contribute to the selected derivative. In particular, retain the G1 generator and unscaled powers in degree zero, even on expressions that are not identities. This preserves the selected gradient since every target product uses exactly one G1 leaf and one G2 leaf after expansion, and only a shared first right-coordinate leaf depends on \(r\). Compute its gradient using the scalar chain rule in reverse order: at a sum or scalar multiplication distribute the accumulated derivative linearly to the inputs, and at a product send each input the accumulated derivative times the value of the other input. Only a first right-coordinate leaf depends on \(r\), and its derivative has only entry \(j\) equal to \(s\). The alpha scaling at the left leaves supplies the sector factor. Summing the accumulated contributions at these leaves gives the required gradient, using \(O(Q+N)\) field operations after fixed initialization. There are a constant number of private points and at most two selected expressions. The span has at most three vectors. Testing a kernel vector in it uses evaluation of the costed form map on those vectors and linear elimination on at most three columns; this is polynomial in \(D,\log q\) since \(n,N\le D\). Each of at most two lifting calls has the protected polynomial running time by \(2\beta<q\) and (P4). Setup and encryption at these points are likewise polynomial in \(D,\log q\) and linear in the message length where it is used. Lazy generic and oracle tables use a bounded number of operations per generic call or key query, and \(O(d_R)\) for each seed oracle query. The code and the fixed statement and polynomial data fit \(\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\) in the reduction's cost model. Consequently its total time is \(t_R=t+O(Q+d_RQ_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\), the corollary's reduction-time bound.

Fix the bit \(b\) and use the exact protocol and message \(m_b\) in that reduction to define \(s_b=\Pr[\mathcal B_b\text{ outputs an SIS solution}]\). Since simulation uses the uniform \(A\) in the specified oracle locations, its ciphertext and adversary transcript have the stipulated law. Output soundness follows from the kernel packing and LIFT just proved. The bound does not need a lower estimate of \(s_b\).

The rank-gap ledger of Amendment E proves, for \(u=2Q+2\), that the probability of a target query and extractor failure conditional on \(b\) is at most \(E(u)\). The convex endpoint argument gives \(E(u)\le (Q+1)/2^\lambda\). Since \(s_b\ge0\), this is at most \((Q+1)/2^\lambda+s_b\), exactly the requested form with the explicit reduction and same costed SIS instance. All oracle budgets were those in Theorem 5.9. The extractor always tests candidate witnesses before outputting and has the stated \(O((Q+1)(nN+|S|)+L)\) running time, so this also gives its soundness and running-time clauses.

For the extractability-error corollary, let \(f_i\) be the just-bounded conditional probabilities for \(i=0,1\). The unchanged factor-one-half lemma applied to the random-mask coupling gives \(\Pr[b'=b\wedge\mathsf{Fail}]\le1/2+(f_0+f_1)/4\). Both reductions run within \(t_R=t+O(Q+d_RQ_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\), so each \(s_i\le\operatorname{Adv}^{\rm MSIS}(t_R)\). Substituting gives the stated error \((Q+1)/2^{\lambda+1}+\operatorname{Adv}^{\rm MSIS}(t_R)\) at the same extraction time. Outside the stated budgets the trivial upper bound of one gives the default error \(1/2\) as in that corollary. This establishes the original correctness and extraction targets and their dependent error statement with the same models, budget and normed SIS reduction.
