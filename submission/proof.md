# Independent extension padding and a tighter generic comparison count

## Amendment n

Let \(p=\max\{1,\lceil\mathtt{forms\_pad}\rceil\}\). The amended number of forms and extension degree is
\[
n=k d_R+p.
\]
Construction conditions (C5)–(C7) and (P1) continue to require the ring congruence, positive grid legs, positive even \(n\) dividing \(q-1\), \(K_1K_2=n/2\), and \(n\le D\). Use the same matrix \(M\) with \(k\) rows and \(\ell+1\) columns over \(R_q\), sampled from the same random oracle, and the same packing \(w\mapsto(\hat w_1,\ldots,\hat w_\ell)\) of coordinates other than 0. Define
\[
\begin{split}
\varphi_0(w)&=w_0,\\
\varphi_e(w)&=0\quad(1\le e<p),\\
\varphi_{p+(i-1)d_R+s}(w)
 &= [Y^s]\left(M_{i,0}w_0+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} M_{i,j}\hat w_j\right)
 \quad(1\le i\le k,\ 0\le s<d_R).
\end{split}
\]
The last \(kd_R\) indices are distinct and range from \(p\) to \(n-1\), as division with remainder by \(d_R\) recovers \(i-1\) and \(s\). This defines every form. It agrees with the baseline encoding at \(p=d_R\).

The field for the weights is \(\mathbb{F}_q[T]/(T^n-c_0)\) with \(c_0\) a generator of \(\mathbb{F}_q^*\). To see directly that it is a field, take a root \(t\) of \(T^n-c_0\) in the algebraic closure. Then \(t^q=\omega t\) for \(\omega=c_0^{(q-1)/n}\) of order \(n\), and \(t^{q^j}=\omega^j t\) by induction since \(\omega\in\mathbb{F}_q^*\). The orbit of \(t\) under Frobenius consists of \(n\) distinct roots of its minimal polynomial, so that polynomial has degree at least \(n\). It divides the monic polynomial \(T^n-c_0\) of degree \(n\), giving irreducibility. In this field \(\sigma f(T)=f(\omega T)\), \(z(w)=\sum_{e<n}\varphi_e(w)T^e\), and \(\omega^{n/2}=-1\). For \(h=\sigma^{n/2-1}\), \(j=\sigma^{n/2}\), \(l=\sigma^{n/2+1}\), the products \(z h(z),\,z j(z),\,z l(z)\) satisfy \(j(z j(z))=z j(z)\) and \(l(z h(z))=z l(z)\), by \(j^2=l h=1\) and commutativity. The fixed polynomial of \(j\) has only even powers since \(j(T)=-T\) and \(q\) is odd. These are exactly the field, Frobenius, degree and parity properties used in `blob:copies-ties`.

The monomial basis also gives an exact kernel identity. If \(A\) is the matrix of columns \(1\) through \(\ell\) of \(M\), then
\[
 z(v)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_0=0
             \ \text{and}\ A(\hat v_1,\ldots,\hat v_\ell)^\top=0 .
\]
The forward implication follows by setting to zero the constant coefficient and all \(kd_R\) row coefficients. The converse sets every coefficient to zero. This is the kernel criterion in `blob:kernel-short`; the definition of a short multiple uses only the infinity norm and reduction modulo \(q\).

### Ciphertext and cancellation

Encryption uses the amended forms when evaluating \(z[\cdot]\) in `blob:enc`. Decryption uses them when evaluating \(z(w)\) and the cancellation polynomials in `blob:dec`. Every other ciphertext scalar, sampling rule, coordinate or capacity in those algorithms is specified by those blocks with this \(n\) and the amended forms, including both sectors of \(ct_2\). The algorithms continue to call \(H_{\rm seed}\) for exactly the entries of the same matrix shape. In particular all exponents for the indexed factors and masks use \(k_c=2k_1+k_{\mathrm{leg}}\), which still enumerates \(0\) through \(n-1\) for the two U kinds and even exponents \(0\) through \(n-2\) for V by \(K_1K_2=n/2\). The identities and polynomial manipulations of `blob:copies-ties` and `blob:cancellation-mask` use only these exponents, the conjugation identities above, and the degree bound on \(z\).

For detail on the cancellation identity, write \(U,V\) for the polynomial representatives of \(z(w)h(z(w)),z(w)j(z(w))\), and \(U_+=U(-\omega T)\) for the representative of \(z(w)l(z(w))\). Let \(z_-,z_\circ,z_+\) in this paragraph mean the three polynomials \(\sum_i w_i z_i[-T/\omega],\sum_i w_i z_i[-T],\sum_i w_i z_i[-\omega T]\). The three differences \(U z_\circ-V z_-\), \(U z_+-U_+ z_-\), \(V z_+-U_+ z_\circ\) vanish in the field, each being a difference of two copies of the same triple product. Call the quotients by \(P(T)=T^n-c_0\) \(x_1^0,x_2^0,x_3^0\). For either U or V, let the prime-weight polynomial multiply each coefficient \(\xi_c(w)\) by \(\omega^{2k_1(c)}\); call them \(U',V'\). Let \(U''\) multiply the U coefficients by \((-\omega)^{k_{\mathrm{leg}}}\). Expand \(\pi_{k_1}\vartheta_{\mathrm{leg}}\) in the tie masks using \(\pi_{k_1}=\pi\upsilon^{2k_1}(1+\varepsilon\omega^{2k_1})\). The terms of degree zero in \(\varepsilon\) on an E or O leg are \(\pi\upsilon^{k_c}(\eta_1 z_\circ+\eta_2 z_+)-\pi(-\omega\upsilon)^{k_c}(\eta_2 z_-+\eta_3 z_\circ)\), where the three \(z\)'s are evaluated at \(\upsilon\). On V legs they are \(\pi\upsilon^{k_c}(\eta_3 z_+-\eta_1 z_-)\). Summing with the weights gives \(\pi\sum_a\eta_a P x_a^0\). Terms of degree \(+1\) have polynomials \(U' z_\circ - V' z_-, U' z_+, V' z_+\) in the coefficients of \(\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3\), respectively. Terms of degree \(-1\) only occur on E and O legs, with polynomials \(0,-U'' z_-,-U'' z_\circ\). These are \(x_a^+,x_a^-\) of `blob:cancellation-mask`. Thus the weighted mask at the witness is exactly \(\pi\sum_a\eta_a(Px_a^0+\varepsilon x_a^++\varepsilon^{-1}x_a^-)(\upsilon)\). The numerator degree is at most \(2n-2\), giving quotient degree at most \(n-2\). Each cross polynomial has degree at most \(2n-2\). The published tie and cross bases cover precisely these ranges. For an arbitrary stack the terms of degree zero in \(\varepsilon\) are the same expansion with \(W_c\) in place of \(\xi_c(w)w^\top\), giving its three tie polynomials. This derivation of the mask identities does not use the old form count.

To establish `lem:honest`, sum the pairings of the witness factors. The \(\Omega\) and \(\Omega^{-1}\) cancel in each pairing, and \(y(w)A_w(\tau)-A_w(\tau)y(w)=0\). The scalar left is the sum of \(r(w)y(w)\gamma_c\xi_c(w)\), the QAP term \(\gamma_c\xi_c(w) A_w(\tau)B_w(\tau)\), and the weighted tie mask times \(y(w)\). The pairs using \(\mathsf{P}^{\mathrm{quo}}\) and \(\mathsf{P}^{\mathrm{rem}}\) subtract the quotient and remainder terms by `lem:qap`, because \(J\Delta\ge D\), \(\nu\mid\Delta'\) and the remainder degrees fit in the \(J'\) chunks. They leave the single key-copy remainder \(\xi_{\mathrm{key}}(w)\kappa\). The pair of \(\mathsf{Y}_w\) with \(\mathsf{B}_w\) subtracts the tie mask from the expansion above, with \(\zeta''\) cancelling between the operands. The search-base pairing is \(y(w)\sum_c\gamma_c\xi_c(w)\) since the first \(\Omega\)-transformed left coordinate is \(\pi_{k_1}y(w)\). Thus with \(\psi=y(w)\sum_c\gamma_c\xi_c(w)\) we have \(\Lambda=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa+r(w)\psi]_T\) and \(\Psi=[\psi]_T\). Every pairing in the combination uses one entry of \(ct_2\); the \(\alpha\) copy multiplies each output by \(\alpha\). These arguments prove the original honest-combination identity under the new forms. Correctness is derived in Targets, including the key-weight failure bound for the amended distribution.

### Counts and asymptotics

For `prop:efficiency`, the group-element counts and operations count the unchanged loop ranges, with extension degree \(n\). The published left factors and cancellation column give \((2K_1+1)(N+1)\) G1 elements. The quotient adds \(\Delta\) and the remainder adds \((\nu-1)n_{\mathsf c}/J'\), because the allowed exponents omit the multiples of \(\nu\) in a range of length \(\Delta'=\nu n_{\mathsf c}/J'\). In each G2 sector there are \(6K_2(N+1)\) right-factor elements, \(3K_2\) search bases, \((3n/2)(J+J')\) QAP bases, \(3(n-1)\) tie bases and \(5(2n-1)\) cross bases. This gives the unchanged \(n_1,n_2\) formulas. The scalar multiplications for publishing them are unchanged, as are the single \(G_T\) element, seed and masked message.

The witness sums use \(2K_1+1\) arrays in G1 and \(12K_2\) arrays across both G2 sectors. Their model counts use the same witness-weight convention. The weighted factor combination in G1 has \(6K_2 K_1=3n\) points. The full chunk lengths for the two quotient and the one remainder MSM groups give \(2J\Delta+(\nu-1)n_{\mathsf c}\); only \(D\) quotient powers per family can have nonzero coefficients, so the proposition's bound with \(2D\) also follows when zeros beyond the polynomial degrees are skipped. In each G2 sector the key and other quotient sums together use \(3n/2\) points per chunk, as does the remainder, and the cancellation MSMs use the counted tie and cross bases. Each Comb takes \(6K_2\) pairings for the factor coordinates, \(2J+J'+1\) for subtractions and \(3K_2\) for the search bases. Adding both sectors gives \(18K_2+4J+2J'+2\); the two kinds of scalar corrections after Comb operate on the same \(G_T\) elements as in the original algorithm. The unchanged centered sampler and search bound give the same `lem:range` and expected search. The argument for the search algorithm depends on \(\Psi,\Lambda'\) and its result only. One can search increasing intervals of radii \(4^j\) with steps of order \(2^j\); the geometric sum is \(O(\sqrt{|t|}+1)\) for the found result \(t\). With \(\mathbb E t^2\le(g\sqrt{(N+4)/48}+3/2)^2\), Jensen's inequality gives the stated expected work.

The number of ring products for computing all the forms at \(w\) and the number of coefficients of \(M\) are identical for fixed \(k,\ell,d_R\). A row coefficient formerly placed at \(i d_R+s\) is now placed at \(p+(i-1)d_R+s\), so the same transform-based evaluation over \(R_q\) can write its output to that slot. Evaluating \(z\) at a scalar requires the same linear weights on matrix coefficients, with the index shift common to all nonconstant contributions. Those weights can start at the precomputed powers for the new indices. The count of three per matrix coefficient for the three evaluations and one per coefficient for the witness-side forms still has the same shape in the model. Precomputing the powers uses \(O(n)\) operations. The weight and cancellation polynomial products use only degree below \(n\) factors and the same transform lengths, since \(n\le D\); the QAP transforms depend only on \(D,\nu,n_{\mathsf c}\). This gives the same asymptotic field work and the same oracle query counts as `prop:efficiency`. 

For the asymptotic corollary, use its original regime \(\log q=\Theta(\lambda)\), bounded extension degrees for the group representations, \(K_1,K_2=O(\lambda)\), \(n=O(\lambda^2)\), \(g=\Theta(2^{\lambda/2})\), \(d_R=\Theta(\lambda)\), \(J=J'=1\), \(\Delta=D\), and linear-size systems with \(N\ge\lambda^2\). In that regime \(F_{\max} n_{\mathsf c}=O(N)\) and the chosen \(\nu\le2 F_{\max}\) give \(D=O(N)\), and \(\log D\le\log q=O(\lambda)\). Then \(n=O(N)\), so the size formulas give \(O(\lambda N)\) field elements. Also \(kd_R<n\) gives \(k(\ell+1)d_R=O(\lambda N)\), because \(\ell=\lceil N/d_R\rceil\) and \(d_R=\Theta(\lambda)\). The field work in the efficiency proposition is \(O(\lambda N\log \lambda)\) since \(|S|=O(\lambda N)\) there.

There are \(O(\lambda N)\) published multiples of generators by \(O(\lambda)\)-bit scalars. For the fixed-base batching bound, take a window width \(w_*=\max\{1,\lfloor\log_2\lambda\rfloor\}\). For each of \(O(\lambda/w_*)\) windows, precompute the \(2^{w_*}\) successive multiples of its shifted fixed base by repeated addition, moving to the next shifted base by \(w_*\) doublings. This takes \(O(\lambda 2^{w_*}/w_*+\lambda)\) group operations. Each requested multiple uses at most one lookup and addition per window. Thus the modeled asymptotic encryption work is \(O(\lambda^2N/\log\lambda)\) field operations, with the same batching available for the repacked scalar lists. For decryption outside the search there are \(O(\lambda)\) pairings at \(O(\lambda)\) field operations each, \(O(\lambda N)\) additions, and at most \(O(N)\) MSM entries and scalar multiplications at \(O(\lambda)\) group operations per scalar. They fit the \(O(\lambda N\log\lambda)\) bound including the field work. The search remains \(O(\sqrt g\,N^{1/4})=O(2^{\lambda/4}N^{1/4})\) in expectation. The original asymptotic families still fit the modified packing by setting \(p=d_R\). This proof of the asymptotic bound does not change the numerical scoring costs.

### Algebraic and reduction interfaces

In `lem:moore`, the field has degree \(n\) and \(z\) plays no role in the statement. To check the property at the new degree, if a combination of \(d\) successive Frobenius columns is zero then a polynomial \(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_j X^{q^j}\) vanishes at the entries of \(\sigma^s(h)\). Its roots are closed under \(\mathbb{F}_q\)-linear combinations, and the independent entries give \(q^d\) distinct roots. If any coefficient is nonzero, its degree is at most \(q^{d-1}\), a contradiction. This proof uses only the field and its Frobenius.

Here is the classification argument with the amended \(z\). Let \(W_c\) be symmetric and pass the Booleanity tests, with row space \(\mathcal V\) of dimension \(d\le3\) containing no kernel vector. The zero-dimensional case has zero stack. Let the columns of \(U_*\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) be a basis of \(\mathcal V\), with rows \(u_i^\top\). These rows span \(\mathbb F_q^d\). For \(s\) in \(\mathcal I_*=\{n/2-1,n/2,n/2+1\}\), put \(h_s=U_*^\top\sigma^s(z)\), and put \(h_0=U_*^\top z\). Injectivity of \(z\) on \(\mathcal V\) means the entries of \(h_0\) are independent over \(\mathbb F_q\). In particular \(d\le n\), and the Moore property proved above gives that the three \(h_s\) span over \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\). Write \(W_s\) for \(W_U,W_V,\sigma^{n/2+1}(W_U)\) in the indicated order. Each \(W_s\) has its rows and columns in the extension of \(\mathcal V\), so \(W_s=U_* X_s U_*^\top\) for a unique symmetric \(X_s\). Indeed a left inverse \(L_*\) of \(U_*\) exists, and \(X_s=L_*W_sL_*^\top\); projections back to \(\mathcal V\) act identically on the columns and rows. The ties give \(X_s h_{s'}=X_{s'}h_s\). If \(\sum_s a_sh_s=0\), then \((\sum_s a_sX_s)h_{s'}=0\) for every \(s'\), and spanning gives \(\sum_s a_sX_s=0\). Thus there is a linear map \(X\) with \(X(h_s)=X_s\). The trilinear form \(C(h,u,v)=u^\top X(h)v\) is symmetric in the last two slots and in the first and last slots by the ties, initially on spanning \(h_s\) and then on all vectors by linearity. It is therefore fully symmetric. Each Booleanity test gives \(C(h,u_i,u_i-u_0)=0\) on the spanning \(h_s\), hence on all \(h\). The identities \(X_s=\sigma^s(X_{n-s})\) for \(s=n/2-1,n/2\) follow from the relation between the first and third matrices and from evenness of \(W_V\), as \(U_*\) and \(L_*\) have field entries fixed by \(\sigma\).

First suppose \(u_0\ne0\). Extend \(l_0=u_0\) to a basis \(l_0,\ldots,l_{d-1}\) chosen from the rows. For each \(\delta\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\), let \(p_\delta\) satisfy \(l_0^\top p_\delta=1\), \(l_j^\top p_\delta=\delta_j\) for \(j>0\), and set \(w_\delta=U_*p_\delta\). Symmetry lets any repeated positive index in \(C(l_i,l_j,l_k)\) be reduced via the test replacing one occurrence by \(0\). Hence those values depend only on the set of positive indices present. Conversely the cube of the linear functional \(p_\delta\) takes the value \(\prod_{i\in A}\delta_i\) for this set \(A\), and all sets occur since \(d-1\le2\). The matrix of these values indexed by \(A,\delta\) is invertible: indexing \(\delta\) by its support gives the subset-incidence matrix, triangular with diagonal 1 in any inclusion-compatible ordering. Therefore
\[
 X(h)=\sum_\delta c_\delta(h^\top p_\delta)p_\delta p_\delta^\top
\]
for coefficients in the extension field. The squares \(p_\delta p_\delta^\top\) are independent by the same evaluation matrix, now on pairs of basis vectors since \(d-1\le2\). Write \(z_\delta=z(w_\delta)=h_0^\top p_\delta\ne0\). Comparing coefficients of the squares in \(X_s=\sigma^s(X_{n-s})\) gives \(c_\delta\sigma^s(z_\delta)=\sigma^s(c_\delta)z_\delta\) for the two consecutive \(s\). Hence \(c_\delta/z_\delta\) is fixed by \(\sigma\) and belongs to \(\mathbb F_q\), as the fixed points of \(x\mapsto x^q\) are exactly this field. Write it as \(\chi_\delta\). Returning from the matrix restriction gives \(W_s=\sum_\delta\chi_\delta z_\delta\sigma^s(z_\delta)w_\delta w_\delta^\top\). In particular the first two polynomial representatives give the stack \(\sum_\delta\chi_\delta\mathbf H(w_\delta)\).

Let \(f\) be any field-linear test that vanishes on the cells. It vanishes on the \(W_s\). Put \(Q_*=\sum_\delta\chi_\delta f(w_\delta w_\delta^\top)p_\delta p_\delta^\top\), a matrix over \(\mathbb F_q\). The vanishing gives \(h_0^\top Q_* h_s=0\) for all three \(s\), hence \(h_0^\top Q_*=0\). Independence of the entries of \(h_0\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) implies \(Q_*=0\), so independence of the squares implies \(\chi_\delta f(w_\delta w_\delta^\top)=0\) for every \(\delta\). Apply this to all Booleanity tests to deduce that any nonzero coefficient is on a Boolean assignment. If every constraint test passes, apply the same argument to them to see that each supported assignment is a witness. In any representation by honest stacks of Boolean assignments in \(\mathcal V\), the assignments equal some \(w_\delta\) because their evaluations on the chosen basis fix their \(p_\delta\). Hence the same test argument applies to any such representation.

If \(u_0=0\), take any basis \(l_1,\ldots,l_d\) from the other rows. Booleanity implies \(C(h,l_i,l_i)=0\), so by symmetry every coordinate of the trilinear form with a repeated index vanishes. This gives zero if \(d<3\). For \(d=3\) its only possible value on distinct indices is a shared scalar \(c\). Evaluating \(X_s=\sigma^s(X_{n-s})\) on pairs of different basis vectors gives \(c\, h_s=\sigma^s(c)h_0\) in the basis \(l_1,l_2,l_3\), since \(h_s\) is obtained from \(h_0\) by Frobenius and the basis is over \(\mathbb F_q\). If \(c\ne0\), the first two consecutive vectors \(h_s\) would both be proportional to \(h_0\), impossible by the Moore independence of the three vectors in dimension three. So again \(X=0\), and the stack vanishes. This proves the classification clauses, including that a nonzero usable stack in the stated case has a witness in its row space.

For `lem:heavy`, its rank and inverse-theorem arguments use the sampler and \(v_0=0\), the latter from the kernel identity. For part (c), an integer short multiple \(x\) of \(v\) has \(x_0=0\) because \(v_0=0\) and \(|x_0|<q\). Coordinate packing commutes with reduction and with field scaling. The matrix \(A\) therefore annihilates \(\hat x\bmod q\) by the kernel identity. The representation of \(\hat x\) includes every coordinate \(1\) through \(N\) once and pads only with zeros, so \(0<\|\hat x\|_\infty=\|x\|_\infty\le\beta\). The MSIS challenge, the infinity norm and the bound being costed are those of the model.

### Degree, view, and the recovery gradients

Fix \(M\), hence the coefficients of the amended \(z\), and treat all scalar coins as indeterminates. Use the two sectors \(\mathsf s\in\{\mathrm{plain},\alpha\}\) with factors \(a_{\rm plain}=1,\ a_\alpha=\alpha\). Let \(\mathcal X_1\) and \(\mathcal X_2\) be the two lists of source-group inputs including their generators. Write \(a(\mathbf c,r)\) for the scalar exponent of a label from \(\mathcal X_1\), and likewise for \(b\) from \(\mathcal X_2\). A target expression has value \(\sum_{a,b}D_{ab}a(\mathbf c,r)b(\mathbf c,r)+D_K\alpha\kappa\), with field coefficients \(D_{ab}\) and \(D_K\). Coefficients of a fixed expression do not depend on any of these indeterminates, even when distinguishing two evaluations of the same expression.

Put \(\varpi=\Omega_{22}\zeta\zeta'\zeta''\varepsilon\). This clears denominators in each term of the value because G1 exponents have none and the published G2 exponents have only the inverses in \(\varpi\), each to the first power. We now check the bound on the cleared degree. A left-leg scalar \(\pi_{k_1}=\pi\upsilon^{2k_1}(1+\varepsilon\omega^{2k_1})\) has degree at most \(2K_1\). Multiplying it by \(y_i\) or by \(\Omega_{12}y_i+\Omega_{22}A_i(\tau)\) costs at most \(D\) further degrees because \(A_i\) has degree less than \(D\). The G1 quotient powers have degree at most \(\Delta\), the remainder powers degree at most \(\Delta'\le D\), and the cancellation column degree two. Hence every G1 exponent has degree at most \(d_1=\max\{D+2K_1,\Delta\}\).

A cell scalar \(\gamma_c\) has degree at most \(k_c+3\le n+2\), and at the key cell at most three. The cleared quotient bases, including an alpha copy, have degree at most \(D+n+6+(J-1)\Delta\): the polynomial \(Z_c\) has degree \(D-1\) except at the key cell where it has degree \(D\), and clearing the one inverse of \(\zeta\) adds four degrees, with one more for \(\alpha\). The cleared remainder bases have degree at most \(N+n+9+(J'-1)\Delta'\le D+n+7\), using \(\deg Z_0=N+2\) and \((J'-1)\Delta'\le D-N-2\). For a cleared coordinate of a right factor, the QAP part has degree at most \(D+n+6\): it uses a right-leg scalar of degree at most \(n\), a polynomial in \(\tau\) of degree below \(D\) or a degree-one variable, and at most five degrees from \(\varpi\) and one from the sector. The ratio \(\Omega_{12}/\Omega_{22}\) has net degree zero after cancellation. The mask part uses a power of \(\upsilon\) of degree at most \(n-1\), an entry of \(z[x]\) of degree at most \(n-1\) in the evaluation scalar, one \(\eta_i\), and at most six degrees for clearing and the sector, for a total at most \(2n+5\). A search base needs at most \(n+6\) degrees cleared. The tie and cross bases need at most \(2n+6\) degrees cleared: the powers have degree at most \(2n-2\), with at most one each for \(\pi,\eta_i,\varepsilon,\alpha\) and four for clearing the inverse of \(\zeta''\). Thus all cleared G2 exponents have degree at most \(d_2=D+n+7+(J-1)\Delta\). The cleared key scalars with and without \(\alpha\) have degrees at most \(D+8\) and \(D+7\). This proves the target-expression bound \(d_f=3D+2n+2K_1+6\), since \(d_1+d_2\le d_f\): if \(d_1=\Delta\), use \(J\Delta\le D+n\), and if \(d_1=D+2K_1\), use \((J-1)\Delta\le D+n-1\). The separate source-expression bounds \(d_1+5,d_2\) also fit \(d_f\). The argument uses no index in the \(z\) polynomial other than its degree bound.

For fixed \(r\), the independent valid scalar-coin ranges each have size at least \(q-D\); for \(\varepsilon\) this uses \(1+K_1\le D\), and for \(\tau\) the excluded \(D\) evaluation points. The clearing factor is nonzero there. By (P5), \(24\varepsilon_f\le E(2)\le 2^{-\lambda}\), so \(d_f<q-D\) because \(\varepsilon_f=(d_f+61)/(q-D)\). The independent-coordinate polynomial root bound, proved in Targets, now has two consequences. If \(D\) has value \(\chi\kappa\) for all valid coins at a fixed \(r\), the cleared polynomial is identically zero in the coin indeterminates. Otherwise the coincidence probability is at most \(d_f/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\). In the first case we can compare coefficients before clearing as identities in Laurent polynomials in the nonzero scalar indeterminates.

We next prove the coefficient-vanishing property needed for recovery. With \(\ell_{j,1}^{\rm leg}\) the first entry of the untransformed right factor, its two transformed exponents in sector \(\mathsf s\) are
\[
 a_{\mathsf s}\left(\ell_{j,1}^{\rm leg}
       -\frac{\Omega_{12}}{\Omega_{22}}s_{\rm leg}(B_j(\tau)-y_j)\right),\qquad
 a_{\mathsf s}\frac{s_{\rm leg}(B_j(\tau)-y_j)}{\Omega_{22}}.
\]
The exponent \(\ell_{j,1}^{\rm leg}=s_{\rm leg}(r_j+A_j(\tau))+\vartheta_{{\rm leg},j}\) is independent of the hidden basis. The two left exponents are \(F_1^{k_1,i}=\pi_{k_1}y_i\) and \(F_2^{k_1,i}=\pi_{k_1}(\Omega_{12}y_i+\Omega_{22}A_i(\tau))\). No other G1 exponent involves \(\Omega_{12},\Omega_{22}\), and the other G2 exponents also do not. Let \(\widetilde a\) be the part of the exponent of \(a\in\mathcal X_1\) at \(\Omega_{22}=0\). Its value for \(a=F_2^{k_1,i}\) is \(\Omega_{12}F_1^{k_1,i}\).

Assume the target identity holds at a fixed \(r\). Its right-hand side, including the separate \(D_K\alpha\kappa\) term, is independent of the hidden basis. Take the terms with \(\Omega_{22}^{-1}\) and positive degree in \(\Omega_{12}\). From the first right coordinate every left input contributes \(-\Omega_{12}\widetilde a\) times \(a_{\mathsf s}s_{\rm leg}(B_j-y_j)/\Omega_{22}\). From the second right coordinate only left inputs of type \(F_2\) contribute positive degree in \(\Omega_{12}\); there the contribution is \(+\Omega_{12}F_1^{k_1,i}\) times the same factor. Define \(\delta_{\mathsf s,a,{\rm leg},j}\) to equal \(D_{a,(\mathsf H_{{\rm leg},j,1})^{\mathsf s}}\), minus \(D_{F_2^{k_1,i},(\mathsf H_{{\rm leg},j,2})^{\mathsf s}}\) when \(a=F_1^{k_1,i}\). After cancelling the common nonzero formal factor \(-\Omega_{12}/\Omega_{22}\), the isolated identity reads
\[
 \sum_{\mathsf s,a,{\rm leg},j}
 \delta_{\mathsf s,a,{\rm leg},j}\,
 a_{\mathsf s}\,\widetilde a\, s_{\rm leg}(B_j(\tau)-y_j)=0.
\]
For any fixed \(j_0\), differentiate formally in \(y_{j_0}\) and substitute \(y=B(\tau)\). Terms from differentiating \(\widetilde a\) vanish, leaving
\[
 \sum_{\mathsf s,a,{\rm leg}}
 \delta_{\mathsf s,a,{\rm leg},j_0}\,
 a_{\mathsf s}\,s_{\rm leg}\,\widetilde a\big|_{y=B(\tau)}=0 .
\]

### Coupling and bad events

Fix the uniform matrix realization \(M\) for the expressions, and fix the adversary's call budget \(Q\). The run up to the first query at the target label has the same law as the run with a uniform string in place of the masked message up to that query without its answer, by the coupling of the protected symbolic-game block. In the latter run the oracle answers and the uniform string can be chosen independently of the valid encryption coins and the search vector. The symbolic simulation with formal equality, including its label images, can therefore be sampled before evaluating the expressions at those coins. An entry introduced on a guessed operand is a new formal variable of its group. The simulation uses at most three new entries per call, for two operands and one result. Received entries are present at initialization. If a result is already formally equal to an entry, the simulation returns that label rather than inserting a new entry.

Evaluate the symbolic simulation at the independent coins and uniformly independent guess-variable values. Within a group a collision means equality of evaluated values for two formally distinct entries. One can couple the evaluated symbolic and random-mask runs until the first such collision. In the real run conditional on the label images, each unused label in any of the three groups, when guessed, has a uniform exponent among the field values not already assigned in its group, and each evaluated result with a new value receives a uniform unused label. Use the same fresh labels in the symbolic and real simulations for new values. Use the uniform guess-variable value for the real guess if it is new in that group, and redraw from the unused field values if it is already assigned. This has the required uniform unused law and disagrees only at a collision. Inductively, before a collision all assigned labels and their expressions agree between the two simulations, and calls and oracle queries agree because the adversary sees the same transcript. The first call on which a collision occurs is common to both runs, even when the answers at that call differ.

The initial collisions are an \(\mathsf{init}\) mark. Suppose a non-initial collision occurs first, before a target-label query has set a mark. If the collision is good in the protected symbolic-game sense, its two entries lie in the target group and their difference has no guess variable. Its later entry cannot be a guessed operand, because that would introduce a new formal variable absent from the earlier entry. It is a result. The real result takes the earlier label, so the monitor sets a good difference mark. Thus a collision that gives a charged first mark must be a bad event. If a target-label query sets a charged first mark before a collision, the queried label has an entry, since otherwise the mark is \(\mathsf{guess}\). This entry has the target value and its expression is bad by the monitor definition. If the first evaluated-symbolic collision instead triggers a redraw of a guessed operand in the real simulation, that collision already contains the fresh guess variable and is bad. It covers any later charged mark even if the real monitor made no mark at the redraw. Hence a charged first mark is covered by the bad collisions after initialization and the bad key-value events in the symbolic simulation.

For a fixed simulation a collision uses the difference expression \(D\) and \(\chi=0\), and a key-value event uses the entry \(D\) and \(\chi=1\). We bound the probability of a bad event over its evaluation. In either source group, if the difference has no guess variable, the source-group coincidence estimate of the protected view lemma bounds its probability by \(\varepsilon_f\). If the expression contains a guess variable in any group, there is a nonzero formal coefficient of a guess monomial. Such a coefficient is either a nonzero field constant or a formally nonzero expression in one of the source groups, since products of guesses arise only through a pairing of source-group expressions. In the latter case for every fixed \(r\) it is nonzero on some valid encryption coins by the source-group coincidence estimate and \(\varepsilon_f<1\). Thus after clearing the factor \(\varpi\), the event equation is a nonzero polynomial in coins and guess values for each fixed \(r\). Its total degree is at most \(d_f+1\). The terms without guesses have degree at most \(d_f\) by the degree lemma. A term with one guess multiplying a source-group expression adds one degree to the cleared source expression, and terms with two guess variables and no other exponents have degree at most seven after clearing. All independent sampled field coordinates have support size at least \(q-D\). The polynomial root bound gives probability at most \((d_f+1)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\).

The remaining case is a guess-free target-group difference or key-value expression. For any \(r\) at which \(D\) is not identically \(\chi\kappa\), the probability of coincidence over coins is at most \(\varepsilon_f\) by the view lemma. For an \(r\) at which it is identical, the mark stack is nonzero. For a collision this follows since the two entries were formally distinct, and for a key-value expression it follows from the no-formal-key conclusion of the view lemma. The view lemma also places every such \(r\) in a single fiber of the fixed stack, specified by the stack's fixed vectors \(u_c\). Thus the event in this case is bad only for a light stack, and the probability over \(r\) is at most \(\theta\). The combined bound on the bad event is \(\theta+\varepsilon_f\).

### Counting the light-stack charges

Only the last case uses the \(\theta\) summand. At call \(j\) a collision in this case must have a result as its later entry, since an operand entering as a fresh guessed entry has its own new guess variable. Among earlier target-group entries, the received ones number at most two and results of earlier calls at most \(j-1\). All remaining earlier target-group entries are single guessed variables introduced as operands. For a fixed result expression the part involving guess variables can be exactly one of those variables at most once. Thus the result can have guess-free difference with at most one entry of this last type, even if one or both operands of call \(j\) have just been guessed. Indeed if it had guess-free difference with two distinct such entries, their subtraction would be guess-free by linearity, whereas it is the difference of two distinct formal variables. This gives at most \(j+2\) candidate target-group comparisons at call \(j\). The possible guess-free key-value entries in the target group number at most \(Q+2\) since they must be received elements or results; any fresh guessed entry contains its guessed variable. Therefore the total number of events that can incur a \(\theta\) charge is at most
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^Q(j+2)+Q+2
 = \frac{Q(Q+1)}2+3Q+2
 = \frac{u(u+10)}8-1 .
\]
The full count for all other bad events is bounded by the number of pairs plus the number of entries in a table of at most \(L+3Q\) entries, and hence by \(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\). The pair count may overcount comparisons between different groups, which only increases the bound. Combining the union bounds establishes the amended charged-mark estimate
\[
 \Pr[\text{charged first mark}]
 \le N_{\mathrm{cmp}}(u)(\theta+\varepsilon_f)
       +\left(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\right)\varepsilon_f .
\]
This argument holds for every realized \(M\), independent of the form packing.

### Correctness with error \(2/q\)

Fix a witness \(w\). Conditional on the seed and the entries \(M_{i,j}\) with \(j\ge1\), the \(M_{i,0}\) are independent uniform ring elements because they are answers of the random oracle at distinct inputs. Since \(w_0=1\), adding \(\sum_{j\ge1}M_{i,j}\hat w_j\) to each \(M_{i,0}\) preserves this joint uniform distribution. In the monomial representation of the ring, its \(d_R\) coordinates are therefore independent uniform field elements, across all rows. Hence \(z(w)=1+\sum_{e=p}^{n-1}a_e T^e\) with \(kd_R\) independent uniform \(a_e\). The key weight is the degree-zero coefficient in the representative of \(z(w)\sigma^{n/2-1}(z(w))\). It is a polynomial of total degree at most 2 in the \(a_e\), as Frobenius substitutes the scalar multiple \(-T/\omega\) for \(T\) and taking the coefficient after reduction modulo \(T^n-c_0\) is a linear map. Its value at every \(a_e=0\) is 1, so the polynomial is nonzero.

A nonzero polynomial of total degree \(d_*\) in independent uniform field elements vanishes with probability at most \(d_*/q\). For completeness, in the final variable write it as a polynomial of degree \(j_*\) with nonzero leading coefficient of degree at most \(d_*-j_*\) in the earlier variables. By induction the leading coefficient vanishes with probability at most \((d_*-j_*)/q\). Where it is nonzero, at most \(j_*\) values of the final variable are roots. The one-variable bound follows by dividing out a linear factor for each root, and the constant case has no roots. This gives \(\Pr[\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0]\le2/q\).

If \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0\), the decryption algorithm returns \(\bot\). Otherwise the honest combination proved in Amendment n yields \(\Lambda'=(r(w)-c_w)\Psi^\alpha\), and the unchanged range lemma gives \(|r(w)-c_w|\le B\). If \(\Psi^\alpha\ne0\), it has order \(q\), so \(2B+1<q\) gives a unique matching integer in \([-B,B]\), namely \(r(w)-c_w\). If \(\Psi^\alpha=0\), also \(\Psi=0\) because \(\alpha\ne0\), and the least absolute matching integer is zero. In both cases \(\Lambda-(c_w+t)\Psi=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa]_T\), hence after dividing by \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)\) the key is \([\kappa]_T\). The final XOR returns the original message. This proves precisely the failure event and the error bound of Theorem 5.6.

### Extraction and the reduction

Let \(\mathcal A\) have the budget \(Q<2^\lambda\) of generic group calls and at most \(2^\lambda\) queries to each oracle. Fix one bit \(b\) and messages \(m_0,m_1\), and run the extraction experiment with \(m_b\). The extractor uses the two-sector sketches in the protected extractor block. It samples three independent probe vectors for each cell and sector, uses the linear combinations of source-group records and pairings to form sketches, tests the sketches of the result and the difference when there is an old record, and directly checks any candidate assignment against \(S\). In rank at most three, there are at most four assignments with first coordinate 1 to test: row echelon pivot coordinates determine the vector, pivot 0 must be present, and at most two remaining pivot choices are bits. Output is therefore always \(\bot\) or a witness. A record creation, result, or difference touches \(O(K_1N+n)\) field elements, since a source-group record uses at most \(K_1\) length-\((N+1)\) vectors or \(9n\) values, and the pairings compute six such combined sketch vectors. For a pairing, forming the inner products in the G2 record or summing per left leg and combining the G1 vectors uses \(O(K_1N+n)\) operations. A test uses \(O(N)\) for elimination with at most three rows and \(O(N+|S|)\) to generate and check the constant number of candidates. Initialization samples \(9n(N+1)\) field values, enters the \(L\) labels as records whose vectors are materialized on first use, and uses \(O(nN+L)\) time. There are at most four tests per result. Thus the extractor uses \(O((Q+1)(nN+|S|)+L)\) time because \(K_1\le n\) and \(N\ge1\).

The reduction on input a uniform \(A\in R_q^{k\times\ell}\) samples the seed, programs the seed oracle at that seed on matrix columns 1 through \(\ell\) using \(A\), and samples the other answers lazily, including the independent column 0. It samples encryption coins as specified, produces the ciphertext for \(m_b\), and simulates all generic labels and names and random oracles by the protected lazy tables. It runs \(\mathcal A\) once and uses its oracle transcript without using its output. Because the programmed entries are jointly independent uniform ring elements for uniform \(A\), all oracle answers including entries at the selected seed have exactly the joint law of the experiment. Private evaluation points are sampled independently of the run.

The reduction keeps records evaluated at its private test point, using the protected rules for guessed operands and for existing records. It tries the first GT result whose label has an existing record of different private value, using the difference expression, and also the target label's record if present. For each tried expression it computes three independent gradient vectors in each sector at private recovery points, tests their span for a kernel vector, using the plain sector first, and applies the unchanged LIFT algorithm if there is such a vector. An output from LIFT is a short multiple of that kernel vector. The kernel criterion in Amendment n then packs the output into a nonzero norm-at-most-\(\beta\) solution to \(A\hat x=0\), exactly for the challenge whose rank, columns, bound, and norm are costed by M1. If no such output is found the reduction returns \(\bot\). Denote its success probability by \(s_b\). The cost is \(t+148Q+(d_R+5)Q_H+C_R+2T_{\rm LIFT}\) with \(Q_H\) the maximum per-oracle query count, \(C_R=O((N+n)D+\mu)\) for the fixed simulation setup and postprocessing, and \(T_{\rm LIFT}\) the time of the lifting step. The preservation of the per-call accounting, query sizes, and preprocessing and kernel-check bounds is given in Amendment n. LIFT's running-time result applies by \(2\beta<q\) and (P4). In particular the cost is \(t+O(Q+d_R Q_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\) using \(n,N\le D\).

Here is how the first-mark bounds apply to these algorithms. The protected degree/view and record arguments use the interfaces verified in Amendment n. A good mark's stack is usable and has rank at most three. Indeed guessing probability above \(\theta\) and the rank bound give rank below four since \(g^{-4}<\mu_{\rm tr}<\theta\). A kernel vector in its row space has coordinate 0 equal to zero, and its one-dimensional guessing probability is at least the stack's, by the protected guessing-probability lemma. The protected inverse theorem gives a short multiple, so LIFT succeeds on it.

The first good difference mark is recognized by the private record difference except when the nonzero cleared expression vanishes at the independent private point. The degree bound and the uniform valid coin ranges give probability at most \(\varepsilon_f\) for this failure. For a first good key mark the target record already has its expression by the record invariant. For a recognized good mark, the gradient formula in Amendment n places all vectors in its sector row space and puts the plain vectors at zero in the alpha-mark case. For any proper subspace of the mark row space choose a linear form zero on the subspace and nonzero on the row space. Its value on one gradient, divided by \(\alpha_{\mathsf s}\pi\), is a polynomial of degree at most \(n+2\) which is nonzero by the distinct monomials \(s_X\upsilon^{k_c}y_i\). The valid values for the independent coin coordinates have sizes at least \(q-D\), by the original sampler and \(K_1\le n\le D\). The polynomial root bound, with \(q-D\) in place of \(q\) in its proof, bounds the chance of failing to increase the span while it is proper. Three independent points and rank at most three give failure at most \(3(n+2)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\), by \(n\le D\) and the formula for \(\varepsilon_f\). Thus on a good first mark with a kernel vector, except for the two exceptional events just bounded, the reduction finds a kernel vector in the appropriate span, and produces the MSIS output. The unconditional probability of such a mark is at most \(s_b+2\varepsilon_f\).

For a good first mark with no kernel vector, the classification result, whose affected interfaces are proved in Amendment n, gives a witness in its row space. In that sector the linear map from a tuple of probes to \(\sum_c W_cv_c\) has image the row space, as each matrix is symmetric and the sum of its images is the row space of the stack. Uniform independent probe tuples give three independent uniform vectors in this space, even conditional on the run. In dimension \(a\le3\), the chance of failing to span is bounded by \(\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}q^{j-3}<2/q\): equivalently the \(a\) rows of the random \(a\)-by-3 coordinate matrix are independent uniform three-dimensional vectors and the next row falls in the span of \(j\) independent previous rows with probability \(q^{j-3}\). At a difference mark the extractor tests the record difference. At a key mark it tests the record of the result that obtained the target label. An initially received record has zero stack and thus is no good mark. These facts from the record invariant show that if the sampled vectors span, the witness is among the assignments tested. So the probability of a good first mark with no kernel vector and with extractor failure is at most \(2/q\).

Let \(u=2Q+2\). Every target query run has a first mark. The protected initial and guessed-mark estimates and the charged-mark estimate of Amendment Ncmp bound their three probabilities by
\[
\left(\binom{L_1}{2}+\binom{L_2}{2}+\binom{L_T}{2}\right)\varepsilon_f,\quad
N_{\rm cmp}(u)(\theta+\varepsilon_f)
+\left(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\right)\varepsilon_f,\quad
2^\lambda/N_{\rm free},
\]
respectively. These estimates concern the same sampler, oracle and record laws and use only the already checked view and degree properties, budget, and label counts. Together with the two good-mark cases they cover the event of a target key query and no witness. Their sum is at most \(s_b+E(u)\): the good-mark excess is \(2\varepsilon_f+2/q\le (8u+8)\varepsilon_f\), using \(\varepsilon_f\ge1/q\); the remaining terms of \(E\) are nonnegative and its guess term contains \(2^\lambda/N_{\rm free}\). By (P5) and the generic-ledger implication proved in Amendment Ncmp \(E(2Q+2)\le(Q+1)/2^\lambda\). Hence for each \(b\) the target-query and no-witness probability is at most \((Q+1)/2^\lambda+s_b\), with exactly the stated oracle budgets and one MSIS instance. This proves Theorem 5.9 with its extraction time and soundness as above.
