# Independent form padding and sharper inverse bounds

## Amendment n

Let \(p=\max\{1,\lceil\mathtt{forms\_pad}\rceil\}\). The amended number of forms and extension degree is
\[
n=k d_R+p.
\]
Construction conditions (C5)–(C7) and (P1) continue to require the ring congruence, positive grid legs, positive even \(n\) dividing \(q-1\), \(K_1K_2=n/2\), and \(n\le D\). Use the same matrix \(M\) with \(k\) rows and \(\ell+1\) columns over \(R_q\), sampled from the same random oracle, and the same packing \(w\mapsto(\hat w_1,\ldots,\hat w_\ell)\) of coordinates other than 0. Define
\[
\begin{split}
\varphi_0(w)&=w_0,\\
\varphi_e(w)&=0\quad(1\le e<p),\\
\varphi_{p+(i-1)d_R+s}(w)
 &= [Y^s]\left(M_{i,0}w_0+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell} M_{i,j}\hat w_j\right)
 \quad(1\le i\le k,\ 0\le s<d_R).
\end{split}
\]
The last \(kd_R\) indices are distinct and range from \(p\) to \(n-1\), as division with remainder by \(d_R\) recovers \(i-1\) and \(s\). This defines every form. It agrees with the baseline encoding at \(p=d_R\).

The field for the weights is \(\mathbb{F}_q[T]/(T^n-c_0)\) with \(c_0\) a generator of \(\mathbb{F}_q^*\). To see directly that it is a field, take a root \(t\) of \(T^n-c_0\) in the algebraic closure. Then \(t^q=\omega t\) for \(\omega=c_0^{(q-1)/n}\) of order \(n\), and \(t^{q^j}=\omega^j t\) by induction since \(\omega\in\mathbb{F}_q^*\). The orbit of \(t\) under Frobenius consists of \(n\) distinct roots of its minimal polynomial, so that polynomial has degree at least \(n\). It divides the monic polynomial \(T^n-c_0\) of degree \(n\), giving irreducibility. In this field \(\sigma f(T)=f(\omega T)\), \(z(w)=\sum_{e<n}\varphi_e(w)T^e\), and \(\omega^{n/2}=-1\). For \(h=\sigma^{n/2-1}\), \(j=\sigma^{n/2}\), \(l=\sigma^{n/2+1}\), the products \(z h(z),\,z j(z),\,z l(z)\) satisfy \(j(z j(z))=z j(z)\) and \(l(z h(z))=z l(z)\), by \(j^2=l h=1\) and commutativity. The fixed polynomial of \(j\) has only even powers since \(j(T)=-T\) and \(q\) is odd. These are exactly the field, Frobenius, degree and parity properties used in `blob:copies-ties`.

The monomial basis also gives an exact kernel identity. If \(A\) is the matrix of columns \(1\) through \(\ell\) of \(M\), then
\[
 z(v)=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v_0=0
             \ \text{and}\ A(\hat v_1,\ldots,\hat v_\ell)^\top=0 .
\]
The forward implication follows by setting to zero the constant coefficient and all \(kd_R\) row coefficients. The converse sets every coefficient to zero. This is the kernel criterion in `blob:kernel-short`; the definition of a short multiple uses only the infinity norm and reduction modulo \(q\).

### Ciphertext and cancellation

Encryption uses the amended forms when evaluating \(z[\cdot]\) in `blob:enc`. Decryption uses them when evaluating \(z(w)\) and the cancellation polynomials in `blob:dec`. Every other ciphertext scalar, sampling rule, coordinate or capacity in those algorithms is specified by those blocks with this \(n\) and the amended forms, including both sectors of \(ct_2\). The algorithms continue to call \(H_{\rm seed}\) for exactly the entries of the same matrix shape. In particular all exponents for the indexed factors and masks use \(k_c=2k_1+k_{\mathrm{leg}}\), which still enumerates \(0\) through \(n-1\) for the two U kinds and even exponents \(0\) through \(n-2\) for V by \(K_1K_2=n/2\). The identities and polynomial manipulations of `blob:copies-ties` and `blob:cancellation-mask` use only these exponents, the conjugation identities above, and the degree bound on \(z\).

For detail on the cancellation identity, write \(U,V\) for the polynomial representatives of \(z(w)h(z(w)),z(w)j(z(w))\), and \(U_+=U(-\omega T)\) for the representative of \(z(w)l(z(w))\). Let \(z_-,z_\circ,z_+\) in this paragraph mean the three polynomials \(\sum_i w_i z_i[-T/\omega],\sum_i w_i z_i[-T],\sum_i w_i z_i[-\omega T]\). The three differences \(U z_\circ-V z_-\), \(U z_+-U_+ z_-\), \(V z_+-U_+ z_\circ\) vanish in the field, each being a difference of two copies of the same triple product. Call the quotients by \(P(T)=T^n-c_0\) \(x_1^0,x_2^0,x_3^0\). For either U or V, let the prime-weight polynomial multiply each coefficient \(\xi_c(w)\) by \(\omega^{2k_1(c)}\); call them \(U',V'\). Let \(U''\) multiply the U coefficients by \((-\omega)^{k_{\mathrm{leg}}}\). Expand \(\pi_{k_1}\vartheta_{\mathrm{leg}}\) in the tie masks using \(\pi_{k_1}=\pi\upsilon^{2k_1}(1+\varepsilon\omega^{2k_1})\). The terms of degree zero in \(\varepsilon\) on an E or O leg are \(\pi\upsilon^{k_c}(\eta_1 z_\circ+\eta_2 z_+)-\pi(-\omega\upsilon)^{k_c}(\eta_2 z_-+\eta_3 z_\circ)\), where the three \(z\)'s are evaluated at \(\upsilon\). On V legs they are \(\pi\upsilon^{k_c}(\eta_3 z_+-\eta_1 z_-)\). Summing with the weights gives \(\pi\sum_a\eta_a P x_a^0\). Terms of degree \(+1\) have polynomials \(U' z_\circ - V' z_-, U' z_+, V' z_+\) in the coefficients of \(\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3\), respectively. Terms of degree \(-1\) only occur on E and O legs, with polynomials \(0,-U'' z_-,-U'' z_\circ\). These are \(x_a^+,x_a^-\) of `blob:cancellation-mask`. Thus the weighted mask at the witness is exactly \(\pi\sum_a\eta_a(Px_a^0+\varepsilon x_a^++\varepsilon^{-1}x_a^-)(\upsilon)\). The numerator degree is at most \(2n-2\), giving quotient degree at most \(n-2\). Each cross polynomial has degree at most \(2n-2\). The published tie and cross bases cover precisely these ranges. For an arbitrary stack the terms of degree zero in \(\varepsilon\) are the same expansion with \(W_c\) in place of \(\xi_c(w)w^\top\), giving its three tie polynomials. This derivation of the mask identities does not use the old form count.

To establish `lem:honest`, sum the pairings of the witness factors. The \(\Omega\) and \(\Omega^{-1}\) cancel in each pairing, and \(y(w)A_w(\tau)-A_w(\tau)y(w)=0\). The scalar left is the sum of \(r(w)y(w)\gamma_c\xi_c(w)\), the QAP term \(\gamma_c\xi_c(w) A_w(\tau)B_w(\tau)\), and the weighted tie mask times \(y(w)\). The pairs using \(\mathsf{P}^{\mathrm{quo}}\) and \(\mathsf{P}^{\mathrm{rem}}\) subtract the quotient and remainder terms by `lem:qap`, because \(J\Delta\ge D\), \(\nu\mid\Delta'\) and the remainder degrees fit in the \(J'\) chunks. They leave the single key-copy remainder \(\xi_{\mathrm{key}}(w)\kappa\). The pair of \(\mathsf{Y}_w\) with \(\mathsf{B}_w\) subtracts the tie mask from the expansion above, with \(\zeta''\) cancelling between the operands. The search-base pairing is \(y(w)\sum_c\gamma_c\xi_c(w)\) since the first \(\Omega\)-transformed left coordinate is \(\pi_{k_1}y(w)\). Thus with \(\psi=y(w)\sum_c\gamma_c\xi_c(w)\) we have \(\Lambda=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa+r(w)\psi]_T\) and \(\Psi=[\psi]_T\). Every pairing in the combination uses one entry of \(ct_2\); the \(\alpha\) copy multiplies each output by \(\alpha\). These arguments prove the original honest-combination identity under the new forms. Correctness is derived in Targets, including the key-weight failure bound for the amended distribution.

### Counts and asymptotics

For `prop:efficiency`, the group-element counts and operations count the unchanged loop ranges, with extension degree \(n\). The published left factors and cancellation column give \((2K_1+1)(N+1)\) G1 elements. The quotient adds \(\Delta\) and the remainder adds \((\nu-1)n_{\mathsf c}/J'\), because the allowed exponents omit the multiples of \(\nu\) in a range of length \(\Delta'=\nu n_{\mathsf c}/J'\). In each G2 sector there are \(6K_2(N+1)\) right-factor elements, \(3K_2\) search bases, \((3n/2)(J+J')\) QAP bases, \(3(n-1)\) tie bases and \(5(2n-1)\) cross bases. This gives the unchanged \(n_1,n_2\) formulas. The scalar multiplications for publishing them are unchanged, as are the single \(G_T\) element, seed and masked message.

The witness sums use \(2K_1+1\) arrays in G1 and \(12K_2\) arrays across both G2 sectors. Their model counts use the same witness-weight convention. The weighted factor combination in G1 has \(6K_2 K_1=3n\) points. The full chunk lengths for the two quotient and the one remainder MSM groups give \(2J\Delta+(\nu-1)n_{\mathsf c}\); only \(D\) quotient powers per family can have nonzero coefficients, so the proposition's bound with \(2D\) also follows when zeros beyond the polynomial degrees are skipped. In each G2 sector the key and other quotient sums together use \(3n/2\) points per chunk, as does the remainder, and the cancellation MSMs use the counted tie and cross bases. Each Comb takes \(6K_2\) pairings for the factor coordinates, \(2J+J'+1\) for subtractions and \(3K_2\) for the search bases. Adding both sectors gives \(18K_2+4J+2J'+2\); the two kinds of scalar corrections after Comb operate on the same \(G_T\) elements as in the original algorithm. The unchanged centered sampler and search bound give the same `lem:range` and expected search. The argument for the search algorithm depends on \(\Psi,\Lambda'\) and its result only. One can search increasing intervals of radii \(4^j\) with steps of order \(2^j\); the geometric sum is \(O(\sqrt{|t|}+1)\) for the found result \(t\). With \(\mathbb E t^2\le(g\sqrt{(N+4)/48}+3/2)^2\), Jensen's inequality gives the stated expected work.

The number of ring products for computing all the forms at \(w\) and the number of coefficients of \(M\) are identical for fixed \(k,\ell,d_R\). A row coefficient formerly placed at \(i d_R+s\) is now placed at \(p+(i-1)d_R+s\), so the same transform-based evaluation over \(R_q\) can write its output to that slot. Evaluating \(z\) at a scalar requires the same linear weights on matrix coefficients, with the index shift common to all nonconstant contributions. Those weights can start at the precomputed powers for the new indices. The count of three per matrix coefficient for the three evaluations and one per coefficient for the witness-side forms still has the same shape in the model. Precomputing the powers uses \(O(n)\) operations. The weight and cancellation polynomial products use only degree below \(n\) factors and the same transform lengths, since \(n\le D\); the QAP transforms depend only on \(D,\nu,n_{\mathsf c}\). This gives the same asymptotic field work and the same oracle query counts as `prop:efficiency`. 

For the asymptotic corollary, use its original regime \(\log q=\Theta(\lambda)\), bounded extension degrees for the group representations, \(K_1,K_2=O(\lambda)\), \(n=O(\lambda^2)\), \(g=\Theta(2^{\lambda/2})\), \(d_R=\Theta(\lambda)\), \(J=J'=1\), \(\Delta=D\), and linear-size systems with \(N\ge\lambda^2\). In that regime \(F_{\max} n_{\mathsf c}=O(N)\) and the chosen \(\nu\le2 F_{\max}\) give \(D=O(N)\), and \(\log D\le\log q=O(\lambda)\). Then \(n=O(N)\), so the size formulas give \(O(\lambda N)\) field elements. Also \(kd_R<n\) gives \(k(\ell+1)d_R=O(\lambda N)\), because \(\ell=\lceil N/d_R\rceil\) and \(d_R=\Theta(\lambda)\). The field work in the efficiency proposition is \(O(\lambda N\log \lambda)\) since \(|S|=O(\lambda N)\) there.

There are \(O(\lambda N)\) published multiples of generators by \(O(\lambda)\)-bit scalars. For the fixed-base batching bound, take a window width \(w_*=\max\{1,\lfloor\log_2\lambda\rfloor\}\). For each of \(O(\lambda/w_*)\) windows, precompute the \(2^{w_*}\) successive multiples of its shifted fixed base by repeated addition, moving to the next shifted base by \(w_*\) doublings. This takes \(O(\lambda 2^{w_*}/w_*+\lambda)\) group operations. Each requested multiple uses at most one lookup and addition per window. Thus the modeled asymptotic encryption work is \(O(\lambda^2N/\log\lambda)\) field operations, with the same batching available for the repacked scalar lists. For decryption outside the search there are \(O(\lambda)\) pairings at \(O(\lambda)\) field operations each, \(O(\lambda N)\) additions, and at most \(O(N)\) MSM entries and scalar multiplications at \(O(\lambda)\) group operations per scalar. They fit the \(O(\lambda N\log\lambda)\) bound including the field work. The search remains \(O(\sqrt g\,N^{1/4})=O(2^{\lambda/4}N^{1/4})\) in expectation. The original asymptotic families still fit the modified packing by setting \(p=d_R\). This proof of the asymptotic bound does not change the numerical scoring costs.

### Algebraic and reduction interfaces

In `lem:moore`, the field has degree \(n\) and \(z\) plays no role in the statement. To check the property at the new degree, if a combination of \(d\) successive Frobenius columns is zero then a polynomial \(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}a_j X^{q^j}\) vanishes at the entries of \(\sigma^s(h)\). Its roots are closed under \(\mathbb{F}_q\)-linear combinations, and the independent entries give \(q^d\) distinct roots. If any coefficient is nonzero, its degree is at most \(q^{d-1}\), a contradiction. This proof uses only the field and its Frobenius.

Here is the classification argument with the amended \(z\). Let \(W_c\) be symmetric and pass the Booleanity tests, with row space \(\mathcal V\) of dimension \(d\le3\) containing no kernel vector. The zero-dimensional case has zero stack. Let the columns of \(U_*\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) be a basis of \(\mathcal V\), with rows \(u_i^\top\). These rows span \(\mathbb F_q^d\). For \(s\) in \(\mathcal I_*=\{n/2-1,n/2,n/2+1\}\), put \(h_s=U_*^\top\sigma^s(z)\), and put \(h_0=U_*^\top z\). Injectivity of \(z\) on \(\mathcal V\) means the entries of \(h_0\) are independent over \(\mathbb F_q\). In particular \(d\le n\), and the Moore property proved above gives that the three \(h_s\) span over \(\mathbb F_{q^n}\). Write \(W_s\) for \(W_U,W_V,\sigma^{n/2+1}(W_U)\) in the indicated order. Each \(W_s\) has its rows and columns in the extension of \(\mathcal V\), so \(W_s=U_* X_s U_*^\top\) for a unique symmetric \(X_s\). Indeed a left inverse \(L_*\) of \(U_*\) exists, and \(X_s=L_*W_sL_*^\top\); projections back to \(\mathcal V\) act identically on the columns and rows. The ties give \(X_s h_{s'}=X_{s'}h_s\). If \(\sum_s a_sh_s=0\), then \((\sum_s a_sX_s)h_{s'}=0\) for every \(s'\), and spanning gives \(\sum_s a_sX_s=0\). Thus there is a linear map \(X\) with \(X(h_s)=X_s\). The trilinear form \(C(h,u,v)=u^\top X(h)v\) is symmetric in the last two slots and in the first and last slots by the ties, initially on spanning \(h_s\) and then on all vectors by linearity. It is therefore fully symmetric. Each Booleanity test gives \(C(h,u_i,u_i-u_0)=0\) on the spanning \(h_s\), hence on all \(h\). The identities \(X_s=\sigma^s(X_{n-s})\) for \(s=n/2-1,n/2\) follow from the relation between the first and third matrices and from evenness of \(W_V\), as \(U_*\) and \(L_*\) have field entries fixed by \(\sigma\).

First suppose \(u_0\ne0\). Extend \(l_0=u_0\) to a basis \(l_0,\ldots,l_{d-1}\) chosen from the rows. For each \(\delta\in\{0,1\}^{d-1}\), let \(p_\delta\) satisfy \(l_0^\top p_\delta=1\), \(l_j^\top p_\delta=\delta_j\) for \(j>0\), and set \(w_\delta=U_*p_\delta\). Symmetry lets any repeated positive index in \(C(l_i,l_j,l_k)\) be reduced via the test replacing one occurrence by \(0\). Hence those values depend only on the set of positive indices present. Conversely the cube of the linear functional \(p_\delta\) takes the value \(\prod_{i\in A}\delta_i\) for this set \(A\), and all sets occur since \(d-1\le2\). The matrix of these values indexed by \(A,\delta\) is invertible: indexing \(\delta\) by its support gives the subset-incidence matrix, triangular with diagonal 1 in any inclusion-compatible ordering. Therefore
\[
 X(h)=\sum_\delta c_\delta(h^\top p_\delta)p_\delta p_\delta^\top
\]
for coefficients in the extension field. The squares \(p_\delta p_\delta^\top\) are independent by the same evaluation matrix, now on pairs of basis vectors since \(d-1\le2\). Write \(z_\delta=z(w_\delta)=h_0^\top p_\delta\ne0\). Comparing coefficients of the squares in \(X_s=\sigma^s(X_{n-s})\) gives \(c_\delta\sigma^s(z_\delta)=\sigma^s(c_\delta)z_\delta\) for the two consecutive \(s\). Hence \(c_\delta/z_\delta\) is fixed by \(\sigma\) and belongs to \(\mathbb F_q\), as the fixed points of \(x\mapsto x^q\) are exactly this field. Write it as \(\chi_\delta\). Returning from the matrix restriction gives \(W_s=\sum_\delta\chi_\delta z_\delta\sigma^s(z_\delta)w_\delta w_\delta^\top\). In particular the first two polynomial representatives give the stack \(\sum_\delta\chi_\delta\mathbf H(w_\delta)\).

Let \(f\) be any field-linear test that vanishes on the cells. It vanishes on the \(W_s\). Put \(Q_*=\sum_\delta\chi_\delta f(w_\delta w_\delta^\top)p_\delta p_\delta^\top\), a matrix over \(\mathbb F_q\). The vanishing gives \(h_0^\top Q_* h_s=0\) for all three \(s\), hence \(h_0^\top Q_*=0\). Independence of the entries of \(h_0\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) implies \(Q_*=0\), so independence of the squares implies \(\chi_\delta f(w_\delta w_\delta^\top)=0\) for every \(\delta\). Apply this to all Booleanity tests to deduce that any nonzero coefficient is on a Boolean assignment. If every constraint test passes, apply the same argument to them to see that each supported assignment is a witness. In any representation by honest stacks of Boolean assignments in \(\mathcal V\), the assignments equal some \(w_\delta\) because their evaluations on the chosen basis fix their \(p_\delta\). Hence the same test argument applies to any such representation.

If \(u_0=0\), take any basis \(l_1,\ldots,l_d\) from the other rows. Booleanity implies \(C(h,l_i,l_i)=0\), so by symmetry every coordinate of the trilinear form with a repeated index vanishes. This gives zero if \(d<3\). For \(d=3\) its only possible value on distinct indices is a shared scalar \(c\). Evaluating \(X_s=\sigma^s(X_{n-s})\) on pairs of different basis vectors gives \(c\, h_s=\sigma^s(c)h_0\) in the basis \(l_1,l_2,l_3\), since \(h_s\) is obtained from \(h_0\) by Frobenius and the basis is over \(\mathbb F_q\). If \(c\ne0\), the first two consecutive vectors \(h_s\) would both be proportional to \(h_0\), impossible by the Moore independence of the three vectors in dimension three. So again \(X=0\), and the stack vanishes. This proves the classification clauses, including that a nonzero usable stack in the stated case has a witness in its row space.

For `lem:heavy`, its rank and inverse-theorem arguments use the sampler and \(v_0=0\), the latter from the kernel identity. For part (c), an integer short multiple \(x\) of \(v\) has \(x_0=0\) because \(v_0=0\) and \(|x_0|<q\). Coordinate packing commutes with reduction and with field scaling. The matrix \(A\) therefore annihilates \(\hat x\bmod q\) by the kernel identity. The representation of \(\hat x\) includes every coordinate \(1\) through \(N\) once and pads only with zeros, so \(0<\|\hat x\|_\infty=\|x\|_\infty\le\beta\). The MSIS challenge, the infinity norm and the bound being costed are those of the model.

The proof interfaces in `blob:reductions` are the same. Programming the oracle for the first \(\ell\) ring columns leaves the distribution uniform for a uniform challenge, the zero column is independently uniform, and every query keeps the original index and original ring output. The coefficient map is deterministic. The private records and gradients use the fact that the only \(r\)-dependent source coordinates are the first coordinates of the right factors, with derivative \(s_{\mathrm{leg}}\) in its matching \(r_j\) coordinate. This fact is unchanged, since the new \(z\) affects only the masks independent of \(r\). The gradients of a good first mark consequently have the expression
\[
 \alpha_{\mathsf s}\sum_{c,i}\gamma_c y_i(W^{\mathsf s}_c)_{i,\cdot}
\]
in the corresponding sector. Their spanning argument uses the unique monomial \(s_X\upsilon^{k_c}y_i\) after dividing by \(\alpha_{\mathsf s}\pi\), its total degree bound \(n+2\) including the higher term, and the range of valid coins. These are unchanged. Formal detection at the private point depends on cleared-degree bounds, which use \(z_-,z_\circ,z_+\) of degree below \(n\), and all other factors already have their original bounds. Recovery then intersects a span of at most three vectors with \(\ker z\), calls LIFT on a kernel vector and packs it using the established kernel identity. Thus the module rows are used only by sampling, by computing the values of the public forms and by this last check.

The running-time statement under the amended packing has the bound
\[
 t+148Q+(d_R+5)Q_H+C_R+2T_{\rm LIFT},\qquad
 C_R=8I+3L+2C_{\rm post}+C_{\rm set}+C_{\rm desc},
\]
with \(I\) for computing the initial exponents at one point, \(C_{\rm post}\) for processing one tried expression, \(C_{\rm set}\) for fixed transcript initialization, and \(C_{\rm desc}\) for the description. Here \(I,C_{\rm post},C_{\rm set},C_{\rm desc}\) are the costs for the amended instance, and \(C_R=O((N+n)D+\mu)\). We give the dependence on the form indices explicitly.

During the adversary run the per-call operations use exponent records which have already been computed. The linear and pairing operations, node/arithmetic bookkeeping, tests of the private value, handling up to two unrecognized operand strings, and the six gradient passes are identical loops on those records, because exponents and their derivatives at an input to each loop are stored field values. In particular the same allotments are 14 for the result's value, check and record actions, at most \(6\cdot19\) for the gradient passes, up to \(2\cdot8\) for guessed operand handling, two for zero records and two for a name. Their sum is 148. The query lengths and lookup/insertion counts are also identical. A fresh seed-oracle answer needs \(d_R\) coefficient samples plus lookup and insertion, and a key-oracle answer uses lookup, sample and insertion. This gives \(d_R+5\) per pair of oracle calls. The change to the form indices occurs outside those loops.

In \(I\), the published-element and mask loops have exactly the same lengths at given \(n,K_1,K_2,J,J'\), the QAP evaluation costs \(O(ND)\), and the three evaluations of the \(kd_R\) row forms take \(O(kd_R N+n)\) using the precomputed powers at the new slots. Here a term of \(z[x]\) coming from a row uses the same coefficients with multiplier \(x^{p+(i-1)d_R+s}\). The table of all powers from 0 through \(n-1\) already contains this multiplier, and one can address the entries in that table starting at \(p\). The coordinate-0 coefficient is 1 and the leading zero coefficients are implicit. If an implementation stores the zero slots explicitly, at most \(n\) writes to initialize them are included in \(I\). In \(C_{\rm post}\), compute the \(kd_R\) row forms and the coordinate-0 constraint on at most three gradients per sector, giving \(O(kd_R N+N)\) field operations. Because the form slots are disjoint, the kernel is the nullspace of those equations on the gradient span, found by eliminating with at most three unknowns in \(O(kd_R+N)\) operations including a row span computation and packing. No arithmetic is required for zero equations. In \(C_{\rm set}\), the ring oracle entries, message masking, and initial labels take the same number of operations, with \(O(n)\) more than sufficient if the index offset and a table of new slots are explicitly recorded. In \(C_{\rm desc}\) one can encode \(p\) as an extra integer at most \(n\le D\), with the remaining instance data including the same system polynomials. Thus \(8I+3L+2C_{\rm post}+C_{\rm set}+C_{\rm desc}=O((N+n)D+\mu)\) by \(kd_R<n\le D\), \(J+J'\le3D\), and \(N<D\). The lifting step is called at most twice, once per tried expression. This establishes the bound above and the stated \(t+O(Q+d_R Q_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\) specialization.

This accounts for each entry in the background list for `blob:forms-weights`. For the remaining proof interfaces used in Targets, the symbolic expressions only use the scalar formulas, mask identity, degree of the three public polynomials, and tests of an actual stack. None uses any particular zero slots. Specifically, the degree lemma bounds each term by its power of \(\upsilon\), \(\tau\) and the remaining scalar variables after clearing denominators. The view lemma uses separation by the hidden basis and monomials, the zero-degree mask identity just expanded, and the QAP tests. The record and mark bounds then depend on that view, the sampler and the label counts. The extractor's sketches use only the coefficient arrays of the left and right factors, not the internal representation of the form list.

### Which inverse-theorem steps use the change

In `blob:admissibility`, (P3) refers to these inverse conditions, and the bounds in (P1), (P2), (P4) and (P5) have no occurrence of a geometric factor \(\Lambda_d\). In `blob:trimming` the ellipsoid depends on \(R_{\rm tr}\), \(g\), and the basis through \(\Sigma_\Theta\), with no \(\Lambda_d\). Thus the prefix and trimming lemmas have the same hypotheses and arguments. We prove `lem:sparse`, `lem:regularize\), and `lem:inverse` under the amended condition below.

### Projected fiber counts

The claim of `lem:sparse` is as follows. Let \(B\) be a basis of an integral lattice \(\Theta\) on a coordinate set \(I\) with \(|I|\le N\), of rank \(d\in[3]\). Let \(F\ne\varnothing\) be a set of points of the integer box on \(I\cup\{i'\}\), \(i'\notin I\), trimmed by \(\Theta\), and \(\Phi(r)=(B^\top r_I,r_{i'})\). If (a) \(\Phi(F)\) has affine dimension \(d+1\) and lies in a coset of an index-\(q\) subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\), then
\(\Pr[F]\le\Lambda_d g^d h(\Theta)m(\Theta)/q\).
If (b) \(F^\perp\) has rank \(d'\) with \(1\le d'<d\), a largest minor on \(J\subseteq I\), and every vector of \(F^\perp\) restricts to a vector in \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Theta\) on \(I\), then
\(\Pr[F]h(F^\perp)\le\Lambda_{d-d'}\,g^{d-d'}h(\Theta)m(\Theta)\).
Here \(F^\perp\) is the lattice of integer vectors normal to the differences in \(F\).

For each possible image \((y,x)\), the probability of \(B^\top r_I=y,r_{i'}=x\) is at most \(m(\Theta)/g\) by independence, so \(\Pr[F]\le|\Phi(F)|m(\Theta)/g\). We count images in \(C=\mathcal{T}_\Theta\times[2,g+1]\).

For (a), put \(s=d+1\), \(\delta=q\), \(J=\varnothing\), and let \(p\) be the identity. Write \(B=(B_1,B_2)\) with \(B_1\) empty, and use \(|\det(B_1)_J|=1\).
For (b), put \(s=d+1-d'\) and let \(Y\) be an integral basis matrix of \(F^\perp\); \(Y_J\) is invertible. The submatrix \(B_J\) has row rank \(d'\) since the columns \(Y_J\) lie in its real column span. The integral kernel of \(B_J\) is saturated in \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), of rank \(d-d'\). It has a basis that extends to an integral basis of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\): the quotient by a saturated subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\) is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence free, and lifts of a quotient basis give the extension. Using this basis change, write \(B=(B_1,B_2)\) where \((B_2)_J=0\) and \((B_1)_J\) is invertible square. The columns of \(B_2\) number \(s-1\). Trimming is basis independent since the mean and covariance transform by the same invertible change on the image coordinates.

In case (b), the injective map \(a=(a_{[d]},a_{d+1})\mapsto(Ba_{[d]},a_{d+1})\) maps the real annihilator of the differences of \(\Phi(F)\) isomorphically onto the real annihilator of the differences of \(F\), by the restriction assumption. The first annihilator has dimension \(d'\) and its integer points form a saturated lattice with basis matrix \(V\). Split its rows into \(V_1,V_2,V_3\) of lengths \(d',s-1,1\). The image of its basis is \(Y A\) for an integral nonsingular \(d'\)-square matrix \(A\), as its columns are integral independent normals. At rows \(J\), this gives
\[
(B_1)_J V_1=Y_J A .
\]
Consequently \(V_1\) is invertible and \(|\det(B_1)_J|\,\delta\ge h(F^\perp)\), where \(\delta=|\det V_1|\). Write \(y=(y_1,y_2)\) according to the basis split. Projection \(p:(y,x)\mapsto(y_2,x)\) is injective on any affine translate of the kernel of \(V^\top\), because there the first block of a difference is determined by \(V_1^\top\). It therefore preserves the affine dimension of \(\Phi(F)\), which is \(s\). The projections of differences of image points lie in the kernel of
\[
 (y_2,x)\longmapsto V_2^\top y_2 + x V_3^\top
          \pmod{V_1^\top\mathbb{Z}^{d'}} .
\]
The map is onto because \(V^\top \mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\mathbb{Z}^{d'}\) by saturation and `lem:lattice-facts` (b), while the \(y_1\) contribution vanishes in the quotient. The kernel has index \(\delta\) in \(\mathbb{Z}^s\). Thus in both cases \(p(\Phi(F))\) has full affine dimension \(s\), cardinality \(|\Phi(F)|\), and lies in \(p(C)\) and in a coset of an index-\(\delta\) lattice.

A full-dimensional finite set \(X\) of \(m\) points in such a coset has \(\operatorname{vol}_s\operatorname{conv}(X)\ge(m-s)\delta/s!\). To see this, start from any \(s+1\) affinely independent points forming a simplex. One can add the points successively while maintaining a triangulation of their hull using all inserted points as vertices. For a point outside the hull, each boundary facet simplex strictly visible to the point gives a new simplex by coning it to the point. Those simplices have disjoint interiors and fill the new region, as rays from the point first hit the old hull in its visible boundary. At least one facet is strictly visible, so at least one full simplex is added. If a point is in the hull, take the unique smallest face of the triangulation containing it. This face has positive dimension since the point is distinct from all earlier vertices. Subdivide that face by coning its boundary faces to the point and make the corresponding subdivision of every full simplex incident to it, by adjoining the complementary simplex vertices. This covers exactly those full simplices and replaces each by at least two, since the point lies in the relative interior of the face. Hence there are at least \(m-s\) full simplices. Each has volume at least \(\delta/s!\), since the determinant of \(s\) independent differences of coset points is a nonzero integer multiple of the determinant \(\delta\) of its lattice. Since \(m\ge s+1\), the region containing \(X\), if convex, has volume at least \(\delta/s!\). In particular, applying both consequences to \(p(C)\) gives
\[
 |\Phi(F)|\le s!\operatorname{vol}_s p(C)/\delta+s
             \le (s+1)!\operatorname{vol}_s p(C)/\delta .
\]

The projection of the ellipsoid \(\mathcal{T}_\Theta\) to the last \(s-1\) coordinates has volume \(v_{s-1}R_{\rm tr}^{s-1}(g/\sqrt{12})^{s-1}\sqrt{\det(B_2^\top B_2)}\), where \(v_j\) is the volume of the Euclidean unit \(j\)-ball. Indeed, write the ellipsoid as the image of a unit ball by \(R_{\rm tr}\Sigma_\Theta^{1/2}\) plus the center. The projected map has a Gram matrix on its range equal to the bottom-right block \(R_{\rm tr}^2(g^2/12)B_2^\top B_2\), which gives the product of its nonzero singular values. The interval in \(p(C)\) has length \(g-1\). In either case \((B_2)_J=0\). By expansion of \(\det(B_2^\top B_2)\) into squared row minors, and because \(|\det(B_1)_J\det(B_2)_{J'}|=|\det B_{J\cup J'}|\le h(\Theta)\) for each \(J'\subseteq I\setminus J\) of size \(s-1\),
\[
 |\det(B_1)_J| \sqrt{\det(B_2^\top B_2)}
 \le h(\Theta)\sqrt{\binom{N}{s-1}} .
\]
Combining with the counting bound and \(\Pr[F]\le |\Phi(F)|m(\Theta)/g\) gives
\[
 \Pr[F]|\det(B_1)_J|\delta
 \le g^{j}h(\Theta)m(\Theta)
      (j+2)!v_j R_{\rm tr}^{j}\sqrt{\binom{N}{j}/12^j},
 \qquad j=s-1 .
\]
Use \(\binom{N}{j}\le N^j/j!\) and \(v_1=2,v_2=\pi,v_3=4\pi/3\). The three upper bounds on the geometric factor are \(2\sqrt3 R_{\rm tr}\sqrt N\), \(\sqrt2\pi R_{\rm tr}^2N\), and \(20\pi R_{\rm tr}^3N^{3/2}/(9\sqrt2)\). They are at most the three allowed \(\Lambda_j\). In case (a), divide by \(q\) to obtain the claim. In (b), replace \(|\det(B_1)_J|\delta\) by \(h(F^\perp)\) on the left to obtain the claim.

### Regularization with the new count

The claim of `lem:regularize` is that a saturated lattice \(\Theta\) of rank \(d\in[3]\) with \(m(\Theta)\ge\mu_{\rm tr}+(3-d)\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\) is contained in a regular saturated lattice of rank in \([d,3]\) and maximum fiber density at least \(m(\Theta)-(3-d)\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\).

Write \(\mu=\mu_{\rm tr}\) and \(e=\varepsilon_{\rm tr}\) in this paragraph and the next two. Induct downwards on the initial rank. For \(d=3\), `lem:prefix` (c) gives regularity directly. For \(d\le2\), use `lem:prefix` with a largest minor at \(J\) and \(J\) first in the coordinate order. Denote the prefixes by \(\Theta_k\), their heights and max densities by \(h_k,m_k\), and the step order by \(s_k\). The densities are nonincreasing, so each is at least \(\mu+(3-d)e\). Take a densest fiber of \(\Theta_k\) to form \(F_k\). If the preceding \(F_{k-1}^\perp\) strictly contains \(\Theta_{k-1}\), trim this fiber by the regular containing lattice \(\widetilde\Theta_{k-1}\) specified below. Otherwise leave the fiber whole. The density of each \(F_k\) is at least \(m_k-e\) by `lem:trim`. Its normal lattice contains \(\Theta_k\). If this inclusion is strict, saturation gives a rank \(d_k>d\). Its rank is at most three since a fiber of a rank-\(a\) lattice has density at most \(g^{-a}\) by fixing coordinates outside an invertible \(a\)-row minor, and \(g^{-4}<\mu\le m_k-(3-d)e\) by (I5). Its max fiber density is at least \(\Pr[F_k]\ge\mu+(2-d)e\ge\mu+(3-d_k)e\). By induction let \(\widetilde\Theta_k\) be a regular saturated containing lattice of rank at least \(d_k\), with \(m(\widetilde\Theta_k)\ge m(F_k^\perp)-(3-d_k)e\ge m_k-(3-d)e\). This constructs the trimming lattice for the next step, and if \(F_{|I|}^\perp\ne\Theta\) it gives the desired final lattice.

If \(F_{|I|}^\perp=\Theta\), let \(j<|I|\) be the last strict index, or \(j=d-1\) when there is none. For \(j<k<|I|\) the step order \(s_k\) is below \(g\). Otherwise `lem:prefix` (b) would make the new coordinate constant in the fiber of \(\Theta_{k+1}\) containing \(F_{k+1}\); its unit vector would then be in \(F_{k+1}^\perp=\Theta_{k+1}\) and vanish at \(J\), forbidden by `lem:lattice-facts` (a). Iterating the power inequality in `lem:prefix` (b) gives
\[
 \mu(m(\Theta)h(\Theta))^{b-1}
 \le m(\Theta)^b h(\Theta)^{b-1}
 \le m_{j+1}^{b}h_{j+1}^{b-1}.
\]
When \(j=d-1\), \(m_d=g^{-d}, h_d=1\). The bound on \(\Gamma_d^{b-1}\) in (I4) gives \(m_d^{b}h_d^{b-1}\le\mu(\Gamma_d/g^d)^{b-1}\).

When \(j\ge d\), let \(\tilde d\) be the rank of \(\widetilde\Theta_j\). The set \(F_{j+1}\) was trimmed by this lattice. Its normal lattice is exactly \(\Theta_{j+1}\), of rank \(d<\tilde d\), and with largest minor on \(J\) by `lem:prefix` (a). Restrictions to the first \(j\) coordinates lie in the span of \(\Theta_j\) by the definition of the prefix lattice, hence in the span of \(\widetilde\Theta_j\). Apply the proved projected count (b). Since \(\Pr[F_{j+1}]\ge m_{j+1}-e\ge m_{j+1}/A_{\rm inv}\) by (I4) and \(m_{j+1}\ge\mu\), regularity gives
\[
 m_{j+1}h_{j+1}\le A_{\rm inv}\Lambda_{\tilde d-d}\Gamma_{\tilde d}/g^d .
\]
Also \(m_{j+1}\le m_j\le\Pr[F_j]+e\le g^{-d-1}+e\le\mu P_d^{b-1}\) by the rank of \(F_j^\perp\) and (I4). For \(d=1\), (I4) allows \(2e\) in the numerator and therefore implies the same inequality. In all three rank pairs \((d,\tilde d)\in\{(1,2),(1,3),(2,3)\}\), the last growth inequalities of (I4) imply \(A_{\rm inv}\Lambda_{\tilde d-d}\Gamma_{\tilde d}P_d\le\Gamma_d\). Multiplying yields \(m_{j+1}^b h_{j+1}^{b-1}\le\mu(\Gamma_d/g^d)^{b-1}\). Now \(b>1\) and \(\mu>0\) give \(m(\Theta)h(\Theta)\le\Gamma_d/g^d\). This proves regularity and finishes the induction.

### An ellipsoid in the coordinate constraints

Let \(a_i\) be finitely many vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^3\) including the three standard unit vectors, with \(|\det(a_i,a_j,a_l)|\le 1\) for every triple. Define
\[
K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^3:\ |a_i^\top x|\le 1\text{ for each }i\}.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{vol}_3 K\ge 4\pi\sqrt2/9\).

To prove this, maximize \(\det A\) over real symmetric positive semidefinite matrices \(A\) with \(a_i^\top A a_i\le1\) for all \(i\). This set is closed and bounded, since the unit vectors bound each diagonal entry by 1 and positive semidefiniteness bounds each off-diagonal entry in absolute value by the geometric mean of its two diagonal entries. A small positive scalar multiple of the identity is feasible, so a maximizer is positive definite. Set \(u_i=A^{1/2}a_i\), and let \(I_*\) be the set of indices with \(\|u_i\|_2=1\). The identity matrix lies in the cone generated by \(u_i u_i^\top\) for \(i\in I_*\). Indeed, the cone is closed: the trace of a nonnegative combination is the sum of its coefficients, so a convergent sequence in the cone has bounded coefficients and a convergent subsequence of coefficients. If the identity were outside that cone, its nearest point \(C\) in the cone would exist by closedness. Put \(S=I-C\). The minimizing property gives \(\langle S,X-C\rangle\le0\) for every \(X\) in the cone under the trace inner product. Taking \(X=0\) and \(X=2C\) gives \(\langle S,C\rangle=0\). Thus \(u_i^\top S u_i\le0\) for all active \(i\), but \(\operatorname{tr}S=\langle S,I\rangle=\|S\|_{\mathrm F}^2>0\). For small \(t>0\),
\[
A_t=A^{1/2}(I+tS)A^{1/2}
\]
would be positive definite and feasible, by the active inequalities and the strict slack for finitely many inactive inequalities. The determinant of \(I+tS\) is \(1+t\operatorname{tr}S+O(t^2)>1\). This contradicts the maximizing choice of \(A\).

Write \(I=\sum_i w_i u_i u_i^\top\) with \(w_i\ge0\), zero outside the active set. Taking the trace gives \(\sum_i w_i=3\). The determinant expansion for the product of the matrix with columns \(\sqrt{w_i}\,u_i\) and its transpose gives
\[
1=\sum_{i<j<l}w_iw_jw_l\det(u_i,u_j,u_l)^2
  \le \det A\sum_{i<j<l}w_iw_jw_l
  \le \det A\cdot 27/6 .
\]
Here the first inequality uses \(u_i=A^{1/2}a_i\) and the determinant bound on the \(a_i\), and the last uses the cubic expansion of the sum of nonnegative weights. Thus \(\det A\ge 2/9\). The ellipsoid \(A^{1/2}\{x:\|x\|_2\le1\}\) lies in \(K\) by Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the \(u_i\). Its volume is \((4\pi/3)\sqrt{\det A}\), giving the asserted bound.

We also use the following elementary lattice-volume implication. For a full lattice \(L\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) and a centrally symmetric compact convex body \(K'\) with \(\operatorname{vol}K'\ge 2^d\det L\), there is a nonzero lattice point in \(K'\). For strict inequality, translate the pieces of \(K'/2\) lying in distinct translates of a half-open fundamental parallelepiped for \(L\) back into that parallelepiped. The sum of their volumes exceeds \(\det L\), so two pieces overlap. The two corresponding distinct points of \(K'/2\) differ by a nonzero lattice vector, lying in \(K'\) by convexity and symmetry. For equality apply the strict argument to \((1+1/j)K'\) for each positive integer \(j\); these sets lie in \(2K'\), which contains only finitely many lattice points. A nonzero point occurring infinitely often lies in the closed body \(K'\).

### Amended inverse theorem

For prime \(q\), with the remaining hypotheses in `lem:inverse` and the (I6) above, every nonzero \(v\in\mathbb{F}_q^{N+1}\) with \(v_0=0\) and \(\operatorname{gp}(v^\top)>\theta\) has an integer short multiple \(x\) with \(x_0=0\), \(0<\|x\|_\infty\le\beta\), and \(x\bmod q=a v\) for some \(a\in\mathbb{F}_q^*\).

Remove zero coordinates and order the nonzero coordinates as \(v_1,\dots,v_{N_0}\). For a prefix of length \(i\), let \(p_i\) be its maximum modular fiber density for independent box coordinates uniform on \([2,g+1]\cap\mathbb{Z}\). Each fiber of the longer prefix, conditional on the last coordinate, is a fiber of the shorter prefix. Hence \(p_i\ge p_{i+1}\). The single-coordinate map is injective on the box since \(v_1\neq0\) and \(g+1<q\), so
\[
\theta<p_{N_0}\le p_i\le p_1=1/g.
\]
Inductively we construct a saturated regular lattice \(\Theta\) on the first \(i\) coordinates with rank \(d\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(v_{[i]}\in\bar\Theta\), and \(m(\Theta)\ge p_i-3\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}\). This gives \(m(\Theta)>\mu_{\mathrm{tr}}\) and \(h(\Theta)\le H_d\), using regularity when \(d\le2\) and `lem:prefix` (c) when \(d=3\). For \(i=1\) use \(\mathbb{Z}\). Its height is 1, density is \(1/g\) and \(\Gamma_1\ge1\) follows from (I4) and \(\mu_{\mathrm{tr}}<1/g\).

For the extension step we first choose a basis \(B\) with product of the column infinity norms at most \(h(\Theta),2h(\Theta),9h(\Theta)\) in ranks 1, 2, 3, respectively. Choose a row set \(J\) of a largest minor. Restriction identifies \(\Theta\) with the full lattice \(L_J=B_J\mathbb{Z}^d\) of determinant \(h(\Theta)\). Every lifted basis vector has infinity norm bounded by the \(\ell_1\) norm of its \(J\) coordinates by `lem:lattice-facts` (a).

For successive minima we use the upper bound in Minkowski's second theorem, J. W. S. Cassels, *An Introduction to the Geometry of Numbers*, Chapter VIII, Theorem V, p. 218, [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-62035-5). For a convex symmetric body of volume \(V_F\) and a full lattice \(\Lambda\) of determinant \(d(\Lambda)\), the theorem states for its successive minima \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d\) that
\[
  (2^d/d!)\,d(\Lambda)\le \lambda_1\cdots\lambda_d V_F\le 2^d d(\Lambda).
\]
Applying it to the \(\ell_1\) unit ball, a union of \(2^d\) orthant simplices of volume \(1/d!\), gives \(\prod_j\lambda_j\le d!\,h(\Theta)\). Choose independent vectors \(u_1,\ldots,u_d\) with \(\|u_j\|_1\le\lambda_j\). These exist by choosing each \(u_j\) outside the span of the earlier ones from the rank-\(\ge j\) span of points of norm at most \(\lambda_j\). Intersect their first \(j\) real spans with \(L_J\) to get a primitive flag of lattices, of ranks \(j\). The successive quotient is free of rank one since the intersections are saturated. In each quotient choose a generator with sign such that \(u_j\) projects to \(m_j\) times that generator with integer \(m_j\ge1\). If \(m_j=1\), use \(u_j\) as a lift of the generator. Otherwise, any lift is \(u_j/m_j+\sum_{l<j}a_l u_l\) with real \(a_l\). Subtract integer multiples of the \(u_l\) to obtain a lift with \(|a_l|\le1/2\). Its norm is at most \(\lambda_j/2+\sum_{l<j}\lambda_l/2\le j\lambda_j/2\). The rank-one lift has norm at most \(\lambda_1\) in either case. These quotient generators form a basis by induction. Thus the product of the \(\ell_1\) norms is at most \(\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_d\) in ranks one and two and \(3\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3/2\) in rank three. Lifting back via \(J\) proves the stated product bounds for \(B\).

Let \(v_{[i]}=Bc'\bmod q\), and \(c=(c',v_{i+1})\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\). Take a densest modular fiber of \(v_{[i+1]}\) and trim it by \(\Theta\), obtaining \(F\) of density at least \(p_{i+1}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}\), by `lem:trim`. Write \(\Phi(r)=(B^\top r_{[i]},r_{i+1})\). The points \(\Phi(F)\) lie in one coset of the subgroup \(c^\top y=0\bmod q\) of index \(q\) in \(\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\), since the last entry of \(c\) is nonzero. If their affine dimension were \(d+1\), `lem:sparse` (a) and regularity would give
\[
\Pr[F]\le \Lambda_d\, g^d h(\Theta)m(\Theta)/q \le \Lambda_d\Gamma_d/q<\mu_{\mathrm{tr}},
\]
using (I5). This contradicts \(\Pr[F]>\theta-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}\). Let \(k'\le d\) be the actual dimension and \(\Delta_*\) the integer matrix whose columns are differences of points of \(\Phi(F)\) from any fixed point. Its rank is \(k'\).

For a point in the trimming ellipsoid, the absolute deviation in coordinate \(j\) from the center is at most \(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\sqrt{(\Sigma_\Theta)_{jj}}\), by Cauchy–Schwarz after applying \(\Sigma_\Theta^{-1/2}\). Thus the corresponding row of \(\Delta_*\) has entries bounded by \(2R_{\mathrm{tr}}(g/\sqrt{12})\sqrt{N}\|B e_j\|_\infty=T_{\mathrm{tr}}\|Be_j\|_\infty\). The last row is bounded by \(g-1<T_{\mathrm{tr}}\). In a minor of order \(k'\), products of row norm factors are bounded by \(\prod_{j=1}^d\|Be_j\|_\infty\) since each basis vector is nonzero and integral. Also \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\ge1\). Expansion by permutations gives the bound
\[
d! T_{\mathrm{tr}}^d \prod_{j=1}^d\|Be_j\|_\infty
\]
on each such minor. This is at most \(H_1T_{\mathrm{tr}}\), \(4H_2T_{\mathrm{tr}}^2\), or \(54H_3T_{\mathrm{tr}}^3\) according to rank, each below \(q\) by (I5). If \(k'=0\), the matrix is zero and the following lift is immediate. Otherwise `lem:lattice-facts` (c) applies to \(c^\top\Delta_*=0\bmod q\) and gives an integral \(u\equiv c\bmod q\) with \(u^\top\Delta_*=0\). Consequently \(u^\top\Phi(r)\) is constant on \(F\), and \((Bu_{[d]},u_{d+1})\) lies in the saturated normal lattice \(F^\perp\), reducing to \(v_{[i+1]}\). In particular \(F^\perp\) has positive rank. A rank-\(s\) lattice fiber has density at most \(g^{-s}\), since fixing the coordinates outside an invertible row minor leaves at most one assignment to the remaining \(s\) coordinates. Since \(\Pr[F]>\theta-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}>\mu_{\mathrm{tr}}\) and \(g^{-4}<\mu_{\mathrm{tr}}\), its normal lattice has rank at most three. Its maximum fiber density is at least \(\Pr[F]>\mu_{\mathrm{tr}}+2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}\). Apply `lem:regularize` to \(F^\perp\) to get a regular saturated containing lattice with density at least \(m(F^\perp)-2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}\ge p_{i+1}-3\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tr}}\). It contains the lift of \(v_{[i+1]}\). This proves the induction.

For the terminal lattice choose \(J\) as above. Let \(z_0\in\mathbb{Z}^d\) with \(Bz_0\equiv v_{[N_0]}\bmod q\). It is nonzero mod \(q\) because \(v_{[N_0]}\ne0\). The preimage \(Z\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^d\) of the line \(\mathbb{F}_q z_0\) has index \(q^{d-1}\), since reduction of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\) is onto and the line has \(q\) elements out of \(q^d\). Hence \(L=B_J Z\) has determinant \(h(\Theta)q^{d-1}\). Consider the body of feasible first coordinates
\[
K_B=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^d:\|B B_J^{-1}u\|_\infty\le1\}.
\]
The rows indexed by \(J\) are the unit rows. Each \(d\)-row determinant has absolute value at most 1 because it is a minor of \(B\) divided by its largest minor, up to sign. The body is symmetric, closed, convex and bounded. In ranks 1 and 2 the unit \(\ell_1\) ball lies in it by `lem:lattice-facts` (a) and has volume \(2^d/d!\). The first two height inequalities in the amended condition therefore imply \(\operatorname{vol}(\beta K_B)\ge 2^d\det L\) in those ranks. In rank 3 apply the ellipsoid argument just proved to get
\[
\operatorname{vol}(\beta K_B)\ \ge\
 (4\pi\sqrt2/9)\beta^3
 \ \ge\ (4\pi\sqrt2/9)(41/10)\lfloor H_3\rfloor q^2
 \ \ge\ 8h(\Theta)q^2.
\]
Here \(h(\Theta)\le\lfloor H_3\rfloor\) because it is integral, and \( (4\pi\sqrt{2}/9)(41/10)>8\) follows already from \(\pi>3.14\) and \(\sqrt2>1.41\). The volume implication gives a nonzero \(u=B_J z\) in \(L\cap\beta K_B\) in each rank. Put \(x'=Bz\). It is nonzero because its coordinates in \(J\) are \(u\), and its norm is at most \(\beta\) by the definition of \(K_B\). Modulo \(q\), \(x'\) is a multiple of \(v_{[N_0]}\) since \(z\in Z\). If that multiple were zero, each integer entry of \(x'\) would be divisible by \(q\). As \(\|x'\|_\infty\le\beta<q/2\) this would force \(x'=0\). Extend \(x'\) by zeros in the deleted positions, including coordinate 0, to obtain the required short multiple.

## Targets

Fix any parameter set satisfying the amended model, the protected system shape, any admissible evaluation points, and the stated group and oracle models. Use the forms of Amendment n in the encryption and decryption algorithms. The amended inverse theorem applies by (P3) with Amendments I3 and I6. The meanings of correctness, extraction, oracle access, adversary budgets and failure events are those of the original two theorems.

### Correctness with error \(2/q\)

Fix a witness \(w\). Conditional on the seed and the entries \(M_{i,j}\) with \(j\ge1\), the \(M_{i,0}\) are independent uniform ring elements because they are answers of the random oracle at distinct inputs. Since \(w_0=1\), adding \(\sum_{j\ge1}M_{i,j}\hat w_j\) to each \(M_{i,0}\) preserves this joint uniform distribution. In the monomial representation of the ring, its \(d_R\) coordinates are therefore independent uniform field elements, across all rows. Hence \(z(w)=1+\sum_{e=p}^{n-1}a_e T^e\) with \(kd_R\) independent uniform \(a_e\). The key weight is the degree-zero coefficient in the representative of \(z(w)\sigma^{n/2-1}(z(w))\). It is a polynomial of total degree at most 2 in the \(a_e\), as Frobenius substitutes the scalar multiple \(-T/\omega\) for \(T\) and taking the coefficient after reduction modulo \(T^n-c_0\) is a linear map. Its value at every \(a_e=0\) is 1, so the polynomial is nonzero.

A nonzero polynomial of total degree \(d_*\) in independent uniform field elements vanishes with probability at most \(d_*/q\). For completeness, in the final variable write it as a polynomial of degree \(j_*\) with nonzero leading coefficient of degree at most \(d_*-j_*\) in the earlier variables. By induction the leading coefficient vanishes with probability at most \((d_*-j_*)/q\). Where it is nonzero, at most \(j_*\) values of the final variable are roots. The one-variable bound follows by dividing out a linear factor for each root, and the constant case has no roots. This gives \(\Pr[\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0]\le2/q\).

If \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)=0\), the decryption algorithm returns \(\bot\). Otherwise the honest combination proved in Amendment n yields \(\Lambda'=(r(w)-c_w)\Psi^\alpha\), and the unchanged range lemma gives \(|r(w)-c_w|\le B\). If \(\Psi^\alpha\ne0\), it has order \(q\), so \(2B+1<q\) gives a unique matching integer in \([-B,B]\), namely \(r(w)-c_w\). If \(\Psi^\alpha=0\), also \(\Psi=0\) because \(\alpha\ne0\), and the least absolute matching integer is zero. In both cases \(\Lambda-(c_w+t)\Psi=[\xi_{\rm key}(w)\kappa]_T\), hence after dividing by \(\xi_{\rm key}(w)\) the key is \([\kappa]_T\). The final XOR returns the original message. This proves precisely the failure event and the error bound of Theorem 5.6.

### Extraction and the reduction

Let \(\mathcal A\) have the budget \(Q<2^\lambda\) of generic group calls and at most \(2^\lambda\) queries to each oracle. Fix one bit \(b\) and messages \(m_0,m_1\), and run the extraction experiment with \(m_b\). The extractor uses the two-sector sketches in the protected extractor block. It samples three independent probe vectors for each cell and sector, uses the linear combinations of source-group records and pairings to form sketches, tests the sketches of the result and the difference when there is an old record, and directly checks any candidate assignment against \(S\). In rank at most three, there are at most four assignments with first coordinate 1 to test: row echelon pivot coordinates determine the vector, pivot 0 must be present, and at most two remaining pivot choices are bits. Output is therefore always \(\bot\) or a witness. A record creation, result, or difference touches \(O(K_1N+n)\) field elements, since a source-group record uses at most \(K_1\) length-\((N+1)\) vectors or \(9n\) values, and the pairings compute six such combined sketch vectors. For a pairing, forming the inner products in the G2 record or summing per left leg and combining the G1 vectors uses \(O(K_1N+n)\) operations. A test uses \(O(N)\) for elimination with at most three rows and \(O(N+|S|)\) to generate and check the constant number of candidates. Initialization samples \(9n(N+1)\) field values, enters the \(L\) labels as records whose vectors are materialized on first use, and uses \(O(nN+L)\) time. There are at most four tests per result. Thus the extractor uses \(O((Q+1)(nN+|S|)+L)\) time because \(K_1\le n\) and \(N\ge1\).

The reduction on input a uniform \(A\in R_q^{k\times\ell}\) samples the seed, programs the seed oracle at that seed on matrix columns 1 through \(\ell\) using \(A\), and samples the other answers lazily, including the independent column 0. It samples encryption coins as specified, produces the ciphertext for \(m_b\), and simulates all generic labels and names and random oracles by the protected lazy tables. It runs \(\mathcal A\) once and uses its oracle transcript without using its output. Because the programmed entries are jointly independent uniform ring elements for uniform \(A\), all oracle answers including entries at the selected seed have exactly the joint law of the experiment. Private evaluation points are sampled independently of the run.

The reduction keeps records evaluated at its private test point, using the protected rules for guessed operands and for existing records. It tries the first GT result whose label has an existing record of different private value, using the difference expression, and also the target label's record if present. For each tried expression it computes three independent gradient vectors in each sector at private recovery points, tests their span for a kernel vector, using the plain sector first, and applies the unchanged LIFT algorithm if there is such a vector. An output from LIFT is a short multiple of that kernel vector. The kernel criterion in Amendment n then packs the output into a nonzero norm-at-most-\(\beta\) solution to \(A\hat x=0\), exactly for the challenge whose rank, columns, bound, and norm are costed by M1. If no such output is found the reduction returns \(\bot\). Denote its success probability by \(s_b\). The cost is \(t+148Q+(d_R+5)Q_H+C_R+2T_{\rm LIFT}\) with \(Q_H\) the maximum per-oracle query count, \(C_R=O((N+n)D+\mu)\) for the fixed simulation setup and postprocessing, and \(T_{\rm LIFT}\) the time of the lifting step. The preservation of the per-call accounting, query sizes, and preprocessing and kernel-check bounds is given in Amendment n. LIFT's running-time result applies by \(2\beta<q\) and (P4). In particular the cost is \(t+O(Q+d_R Q_H+\mu)+\operatorname{poly}(D,\log q)\) using \(n,N\le D\).

Here is how the first-mark bounds apply to these algorithms. The protected degree/view and record arguments use the interfaces verified in Amendment n. A good mark's stack is usable and has rank at most three. Indeed guessing probability above \(\theta\) and the rank bound give rank below four since \(g^{-4}<\mu_{\rm tr}<\theta\). A kernel vector in its row space has coordinate 0 equal to zero, and its one-dimensional guessing probability is at least the stack's, by the protected guessing-probability lemma. The amended inverse theorem gives a short multiple, so LIFT succeeds on it.

The first good difference mark is recognized by the private record difference except when the nonzero cleared expression vanishes at the independent private point. The degree bound and the uniform valid coin ranges give probability at most \(\varepsilon_f\) for this failure. For a first good key mark the target record already has its expression by the record invariant. For a recognized good mark, the gradient formula in Amendment n places all vectors in its sector row space and puts the plain vectors at zero in the alpha-mark case. For any proper subspace of the mark row space choose a linear form zero on the subspace and nonzero on the row space. Its value on one gradient, divided by \(\alpha_{\mathsf s}\pi\), is a polynomial of degree at most \(n+2\) which is nonzero by the distinct monomials \(s_X\upsilon^{k_c}y_i\). The valid values for the independent coin coordinates have sizes at least \(q-D\), by the original sampler and \(K_1\le n\le D\). The polynomial root bound, with \(q-D\) in place of \(q\) in its proof, bounds the chance of failing to increase the span while it is proper. Three independent points and rank at most three give failure at most \(3(n+2)/(q-D)\le\varepsilon_f\), by \(n\le D\) and the formula for \(\varepsilon_f\). Thus on a good first mark with a kernel vector, except for the two exceptional events just bounded, the reduction finds a kernel vector in the appropriate span, and produces the MSIS output. The unconditional probability of such a mark is at most \(s_b+2\varepsilon_f\).

For a good first mark with no kernel vector, the classification result, whose affected interfaces are proved in Amendment n, gives a witness in its row space. In that sector the linear map from a tuple of probes to \(\sum_c W_cv_c\) has image the row space, as each matrix is symmetric and the sum of its images is the row space of the stack. Uniform independent probe tuples give three independent uniform vectors in this space, even conditional on the run. In dimension \(a\le3\), the chance of failing to span is bounded by \(\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}q^{j-3}<2/q\): equivalently the \(a\) rows of the random \(a\)-by-3 coordinate matrix are independent uniform three-dimensional vectors and the next row falls in the span of \(j\) independent previous rows with probability \(q^{j-3}\). At a difference mark the extractor tests the record difference. At a key mark it tests the record of the result that obtained the target label. An initially received record has zero stack and thus is no good mark. These facts from the record invariant show that if the sampled vectors span, the witness is among the assignments tested. So the probability of a good first mark with no kernel vector and with extractor failure is at most \(2/q\).

Let \(u=2Q+2\). Every target query run has a first mark. The protected initial, charged and guessed-mark estimates bound their three probabilities by
\[
\left(\binom{L_1}{2}+\binom{L_2}{2}+\binom{L_T}{2}\right)\varepsilon_f,\quad
N_{\rm cmp}(u)(\theta+\varepsilon_f)
+\left(\binom{L+3u}{2}+L+3u\right)\varepsilon_f,\quad
2^\lambda/N_{\rm free},
\]
respectively. These estimates concern the same sampler, oracle and record laws and use only the already checked view and degree properties, budget, and label counts. Together with the two good-mark cases they cover the event of a target key query and no witness. Their sum is at most \(s_b+E(u)\): the good-mark excess is \(2\varepsilon_f+2/q\le (8u+8)\varepsilon_f\), using \(\varepsilon_f\ge1/q\); the remaining terms of \(E\) are nonnegative and its guess term contains \(2^\lambda/N_{\rm free}\). By (P5) and the protected generic-bound lemma \(E(2Q+2)\le(Q+1)/2^\lambda\). Hence for each \(b\) the target-query and no-witness probability is at most \((Q+1)/2^\lambda+s_b\), with exactly the stated oracle budgets and one MSIS instance. This proves Theorem 5.9 with its extraction time and soundness as above.
